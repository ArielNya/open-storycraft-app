# Pass: kill the explain-it simile

The model reaches for similes and "the way / the kind of" constructions to explain a
feeling the concrete detail already carries. The comparison tells the reader how to feel
instead of letting the image work.

**FIX (reword or cut):**
- Explain-it similes that gloss an emotion the scene already shows: "her face fell, like a child who'd been promised something" → trust the falling face, or use a concrete physical beat.
- Meta-comparison scaffolding: "the way that…", "in the kind of way…", "a kind of…", "as if to say…", "like someone who…". Cut the scaffold and state the thing.
- Stacked comparisons: two or more "like / as" figures in one beat. Keep at most the strongest.
- Abstract vehicles: "like a promise," "like something ending" add no image. Cut or ground in a concrete vehicle.

**KEEP:**
- A fresh, concrete simile that adds an image the prose didn't already have (sparingly, a couple per chapter at most).
- In-voice idiomatic comparisons a character would actually speak.
- Literal "like" (preference) and "as" (temporal/causal: "as he turned").

Prefer cutting the comparison to rewriting the whole sentence. Restraint is the goal: end
with a few strong earned comparisons, not zero. Don't mechanically strip every "like."

Triage grep: `grep -rniE "\b(like|as if|the way|a way|the kind|a kind)\b" <files>`
