# Anti-Flat Outline Rules

Dynamic outlines create dynamic stories. Enforce these rules to prevent flat, predictable plots.

---

## RULE 1: Varied Scene Purpose

No two consecutive beats serve the same narrative purpose.

**Alternate between:**
- Plot advancement
- Character revelation
- Relationship shift
- World-building

If two beats in a row are both "action sequence" or both "emotional conversation," restructure.

---

## RULE 2: Power Must Oscillate

Power cannot stay with the protagonist throughout.

**Requirements:**
- Power shifts to antagonist at least ONCE after Act 1
- At least one protagonist victory becomes a setback
- Protagonist loses something unrecoverable (not just a death)
- Antagonist WINS at least one meaningful exchange

**Bad pattern**: Protagonist wins → wins → wins → final victory
**Good pattern**: Protagonist wins → loses badly → partial win → devastating loss → hard-won victory

---

## RULE 3: Goals Must Evolve

The protagonist cannot want the same thing from beginning to end.

**Requirements:**
- Act 1 goal is WRONG or incomplete
- Midpoint redefines what they're actually fighting for
- Final goal differs from opening goal

**Example progression:**
- Act 1: "I want to save my sister"
- Midpoint: "I realize my sister doesn't want to be saved—she wants justice"
- Act 3: "I want to expose the system that hurt us both"

---

## RULE 4: Moral Complexity

No one is purely evil or purely good.

**Requirements:**
- At least one ally has a conflicting agenda
- Antagonist has a sympathetic element
- At least one character switches alignment
- Protagonist makes at least one morally questionable choice

**Test**: Can you argue the antagonist's perspective? If not, they're too flat.

---

## RULE 5: Meaningful Costs

Victory cannot be free.

**Requirements:**
- Victory costs something established in Act 1
- NOT just "supporting character dies" (cheap)
- Something the protagonist CHOSE or VALUED is sacrificed

**Good costs:**
- The relationship they built must be severed
- The power they gained must be given up
- The identity they clung to must be abandoned
- The person they became must revert (or the person they were must die)

**Test**: Would the Act 1 protagonist consider this victory worth it? If yes, the cost isn't high enough.

---

## RULE 6: Information Asymmetry

Secrets and reveals drive tension.

**Requirements:**
- At least one reveal recontextualizes earlier events
- Reader knows something characters don't, OR vice versa
- At least one Act 1 "truth" is revealed as false

**Types:**
- **Dramatic irony**: Reader knows the mentor is the villain; protagonist doesn't
- **Mystery**: Reader and protagonist both searching for truth
- **Revelation**: Something the protagonist believed is shattered

---

## Complexity Checklist

Before finalizing any outline, verify:

- [ ] Goal changes at least twice
- [ ] Power shifts to antagonist after Act 1
- [ ] At least one ally has conflicting agenda
- [ ] Antagonist has sympathetic element
- [ ] Victory costs something from Act 1 (not just death)
- [ ] At least one truth revealed as false
- [ ] No two consecutive beats serve same purpose
- [ ] At least one twist recontextualizes earlier events
- [ ] Major twist tests protagonist's identity/beliefs
- [ ] Twists vary in type (not all betrayals)
- [ ] Quiet shifts balance dramatic reversals
