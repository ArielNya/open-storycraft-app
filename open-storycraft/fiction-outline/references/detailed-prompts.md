# Detailed Beat Prompts (3-Act Structure)

Use these prompts when developing each beat. Focus on internal conflict: desire vs fear.

---

## ACT 1

### Hook: Introduction to Protagonist's Inner Conflict

Internal conflict is simple: **desire vs fear**. This conflict has been boiling below the surface long before the inciting incident. External conflict (plot) is about what happens; internal conflict (story) is about why it matters.

**Ask yourself:**
- What does my protagonist think will bring true happiness or contentment? How is fear stopping them from going after it?
- What is my character's misbelief and what happened in their past to create it?
- How can I show in the FIRST FIVE MINUTES why "what's happening" MATTERS, given their desire, fear, and misbelief?

---

### Inciting Incident: Protagonist Pushed Outside Comfort Zone

This event sets your story in motion. It doesn't have to be epic or crazy. It just has to push your protagonist outside their comfort zone. A conflict surfaces that they must face head-on.

**Ask yourself:**
- Why does this inciting incident matter to my protagonist? How does it push them outside their comfort zone?
- What internal conflict arises in wake of the inciting incident?

---

### Build-Up: The Impossible Question

Protagonist must face this thing head-on. The reader can now see what the protagonist's internal struggle really is.

**Ask yourself:**
- What is the "impossible choice"? Protagonist must choose: stay inside comfort zone and risk NEVER getting what they desire, OR venture into the unknown and accomplish their goal while STILL avoiding their fear.

---

### First Plot Point: Fear-Based Decision

Your protagonist is torn between desire and fear. When shoved outside their comfort zone, fear takes over and they respond based on their misbelief, setting up obstacles for the rest of the book. The brain avoids the most pain. What is the LEAST painful option? Venturing into the unknown and risking danger... but ultimately getting what they desire.

**Ask yourself:**
- How will my protagonist react to the inciting incident, given their fear and misbelief?
- What decision will they make to avoid the most pain? (Get what they want while steering clear of what they fear)

---

### First Pinch Point: Opposition Looms

Your "opposition" doesn't have to be a villain or even a person. It can be the protagonist's misbelief looming in the background, ready to attack later.

**Ask yourself:**
- What opposition/antagonistic force will my protagonist face head-on later?
- How can I show the reader it's already looming in the distance?

---

## ACT 2

### Pre-Midpoint: Reactionary Hero Pursues Fear-Based Goal

Thanks to the Act 1 decision, the protagonist is now a person with a plan. They're going after what they think will make them happy while steering clear of what they fear. Whether running away or running to something, motivation is the same: avoid pain, get to the finish line where happiness awaits.

**Ask yourself:**
- How will my protagonist execute their plan (achieve goal, avoid fear)?
- What is their step-by-step plan? (Even if they don't know every step)
- How can I show the REASONING behind my protagonist's actions (how every decision is based on their misbelief)?

---

### Game-Changing Midpoint: Plot Twist

A game-changing midpoint doesn't have to be epic. It just has to surprise the protagonist and shift their goal. It can be as dramatic as "everything protagonist believes is actually a lie" OR as simple as "protagonist realizes they are falling in love with their nemesis."

**Ask yourself:**
- What unexpected thing will upend my protagonist's plan (and entire life)?
- Why does it matter, given their desire and fear?
- How will it change the game?

---

### Post-Midpoint: Action Hero with New Angle

Figure out how your protagonist handles the plot twist. It's like another inciting incident, shoving them further outside their comfort zone. Give your protagonist a plan going forward and a sense of where this is going. Readers want to foresee what MIGHT happen.

**Ask yourself:**
- What is my protagonist's NEW plan, based on the game-changing midpoint?
- How do they think this new plan will work and bring them to their goal while STILL avoiding their fear?

---

### Second Pinch Point: Opposition Gets Closer

Whatever major "opposition" your protagonist will face at the climax, give the reader a taste of it here.

**Ask yourself:**
- How can I show the opposition/antagonistic force now getting closer to disrupting the protagonist's life?

---

## ACT 3

### Supposed Victory (Optional)

Making progress with their new plan, the protagonist feels confident they will be victorious. Little do they know, disaster is on the way.

**Ask yourself:**
- Why does my protagonist think they will be victorious?
- What's happening to make them feel SO CLOSE to achieving their goal?

---

### Disaster: Everything Goes Wrong

Bring your protagonist to their knees by rooting the disaster in their greatest fear and misbelief. Go beyond "something terrible" and make it something especially bad for THIS protagonist because of their fear and misbelief. Every decision they've made has led them here... they are to blame for their own ruin.

**Ask yourself:**
- What does this disaster specifically mean to my protagonist?
- How does it force them to realize they're to blame for this crisis?
- How does it completely disarm them and make them face their fear and misbelief?

---

### Dark Moment: Protagonist Is Hopeless

Always darkest before the dawn. Your protagonist needs a rock-bottom moment to have an "aha" moment. Right now, in wake of the disaster, they feel utterly broken, confused, lost, and disappointed.

**Ask yourself:**
- How is my protagonist forced into the ring with their kryptonite?

---

### Aha Moment: Renewed Hope

The biggest pivotal moment in your whole story. After being brought to their knees, they have a revelation. They can suddenly see how their fear and misbelief led them to make wrong decisions about everything. They realize they'll have to overcome their fear/misbelief and make the RIGHT decision to achieve their goal: true happiness.

**Ask yourself:**
- How will my protagonist overcome their fear and continue to the climax, therefore developing as a character?
- What lesson will they learn (and simultaneously teach the audience)?

---

### Alternative: Temptation (Instead of Disaster + Dark Moment)

Instead of everything going wrong... everything goes right. They're offered the chance to finally have what they've ALWAYS wanted (the misbelief-based goal). But now, they turn it down. Because they've found something better. They've found true happiness.

**Ask yourself:**
- What is the specific misbelief-based goal my protagonist has been chasing?
- How is my protagonist offered the chance to finally have this?
- How will they prove their transformation by turning down this offer?

---

### Climactic Confrontation: Biggest Challenge Yet

The moment everyone has been waiting for. A true test of their character. How they respond proves they've TRANSFORMED. They've already won the internal battle (aha moment). Now they must win the external battle (which forces them to face their greatest fears).

**Ask yourself:**
- How does my protagonist prove their transformation by crushing their misbelief and facing their greatest fear with courage?

---

### Victory: Protagonist Overcomes

This doesn't mean they win the external battle or things turn out exactly as hoped. But they experience a personal victory because they overcame their fear and destroyed their misbelief.

**Rule of thumb**: If your reader doesn't know how the character transformed as a result of their journey, you need to rewrite until they do.

**Ask yourself:**
- How has my protagonist changed as a result of their journey?

---

### Resolution: Wrapping Up Loose Ends

Tie up all loose ends. The reader should have no missing information or silent questions. For a series, you may want a cliffhanger instead of a victory beat. For a standalone, make sure all questions are answered by the final page.

**Ask yourself:**
- Where will my protagonist go from here?
- What does their life look like, now that they have overcome their misbelief?
