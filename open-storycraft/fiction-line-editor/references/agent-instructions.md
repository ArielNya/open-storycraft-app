## Line Edit Criteria

Check every line in your assigned chunk against ALL of the following categories. This is comprehensive sentence-level editing.

### 1. Narrative Fragments
- Sentences in narrative prose that lack a subject and/or verb
- Staccato lists used as atmosphere ("Dust. Silence. Nothing.")
- Exception: Dialogue can have fragments. Battle scenes get limited fragment allowance.

### 2. Cliches and Tired Phrasing
- Stock phrases: "crystal clear," "razor focus," "dropped into darkness," "ice-cold [noun]," "closing the distance," "let out a breath he didn't know he was holding"
- The "exhaled for the first time since [place]" construction
- "Heart raced," "breath caught," "knuckles white," "jaw clenched," "eyes narrowed"
- Any phrase you've read in a hundred thrillers or fantasy novels
- The "beat of silence" / "loaded silence" / "heavy silence" cluster

### 3. AIisms
- "all [adjective noun] and [adjective noun]" patterns
- Over-qualified adverbs: "entirely involuntary," "completely unnecessary"
- Vague abstract descriptors: "that brightness," "a sense of," "something akin to"
- "A mix/mixture/combination of X and Y"
- "Couldn't help but [verb]" / "found himself [verbing]"
- Paradiastole: "not [X] but [Y]" / "it wasn't [X], it was [Y]"
- Forbidden personification: inanimate things with human agency
- "The [noun] of someone who [clause]"
- Any forbidden words/phrases from the style/review guide

### 4. Repetition
- Same word used within 3-4 paragraphs (proximity repetition)
- Same detail described twice (same fact, descriptor, or measurement)
- Same sentence structure repeated 3+ times in a row (S-V-O chains)
- Same emotional beat restated (fear described, then described again differently)

### 5. Telling Over Showing
- Labeling emotions directly: "her voice warm and curious," "his expression grim"
- Vague emotional descriptors: "that brightness in her eyes"
- Stating quality instead of showing: "she said convincingly"
- Emotion-body formulas: "fear gripped his chest," "dread coiled in his stomach"
- **Show-then-tell:** action/dialogue already conveys the emotion, then narration explains it. Cut the explanation.

### 6. Awkward Constructions
- Comma splices (two independent clauses joined by only a comma)
- Dangling or misplaced modifiers
- Temporal confusion (describing something as happening when it already did)
- Contradictory adjective pairs ("blank and relaxed")
- Misattributed actions (questions don't yell, doors don't feel)
- Gerund chains (comma-linked -ing actions creating simultaneous confusion)

### 7. POV Violations
- Camera POV: describing actions without filtering through the POV character's perception
- Other characters' internal states presented as fact rather than observed
- Unfiltered actions: "Hands went to radios" instead of the POV character perceiving it
- Info dumps that read like a textbook rather than the character's processing

### 8. Voice Violations
- Constructions that don't match the POV character's age, background, or personality
- Overly literary narration when the character is casual/pragmatic
- Jargon or vocabulary the character wouldn't know
- Observations too abstract or atmospheric for the character's personality
- Narration that sounds like a neutral author rather than the character's mind
- Analytical, robotic, clinical, engineer-like, therapist-like, or outline-like narration when the protagonist would experience the moment bodily or emotionally
- Repeated processor verbs such as registered, filed, clocked, tracked, calculated, processed, generated, analyzed, or logged unless the project guide explicitly permits them
- Motif overreach: a motif that works in one domain gets applied to every emotion until the prose sounds mechanical

### 9. Paired Adjectives for Mood
- "dark and foreboding," "cold and unforgiving," "warm and inviting"
- Pick the stronger adjective or find a more specific descriptor

### 10. Style Guide Violations
- Any project-specific rules from the review/style guide
- Punctuation bans (e.g., EM dashes in narration)
- Escalation patterns ("Not just tired, but exhausted")
- Any other flagged constructions from the guide

## Important Notes
- Be specific: every issue needs a line number, quoted text, and a concrete fix.
- Don't over-correct: if a construction works for character voice, skip it.
- Prioritize impact: lead with the worst offenders.
- No false positives: a short list of real problems beats a long list of nitpicks.
- Trust the voice: if the style guide defines a character voice, respect it.
