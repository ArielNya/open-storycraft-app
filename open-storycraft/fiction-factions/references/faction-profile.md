# Faction profile and scale guide

Use this as a field checklist, not a demand for equal-length sections. A short story may need only a paragraph per section; a series-level power may need more detail.

## Identity

Before filling identity or leadership, use the sibling `name-generator` skill to produce the culture-appropriate pool required by the main workflow. Record selected names in the finished profile, not the discarded pool, unless the user asks to see candidates.

- `name`: public or most useful display name
- `also_known_as`: official name, street name, enemy label, acronym, or translation when relevant
- `tag`: one writer-facing line stating what the faction is and why it matters to the story
- `subtype`: government office, guild, household, faith, union, crew, company, army, school, movement, criminal network, insurgency, research body, species polity, artificial collective, or setting-specific form
- `scale`: the setting-appropriate reach tier
- `public_face`: what ordinary people think the faction does
- `symbol_or_motto`: optional; include only if it can appear on the page

## Scale and footprint

Scale is relational to the setting. Prefer a plain label from the setting, with these tiers as reasoning aids:

| Tier | Typical reach | Leadership depth | Footprint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local | One neighborhood, ship, school, clan, habitat, or town | One leader and one key deputy | One base; few specialized assets |
| Regional | One city, province, planet, trade corridor, or social sector | Leader, deputies, several cells or offices | Multiple holdings and a reliable force or network |
| Major | Realm, nation, system, continent, or civilization-wide institution | Leadership body, competing senior figures, subordinate branches | Strategic assets, durable legitimacy, broad logistics |
| Vast | Multi-realm, planetary, interstellar, interdimensional, or epoch-spanning | Layered command with semi-autonomous arms | Infrastructure and influence beyond any one ruler or locale |

Define:

- `reach`: where its decisions have practical effect
- `membership`: approximate size and the types of people or entities who belong
- `headquarters`: a real place suited to the setting
- `holdings`: controlled sites, jurisdictions, routes, businesses, ships, archives, shrines, systems, or other durable footholds
- `subsidiaries_or_cells`: only when the scale warrants them; state each one's distinct function

Use rounded estimates when exact counts would imply false precision.

## Purpose and conduct

- `purpose`: its legitimate function or grievance
- `current_objective`: the concrete result it is pursuing now
- `success_looks_like`: an observable new state, not “more power”
- `methods`: recurring tactics that reflect its resources and culture
- `lines_it_will_not_cross`: a real constraint, taboo, principle, dependency, or reputational limit
- `crossing_point`: the pressure that could make it violate that limit
- `recruitment_and_exit`: who joins, why they stay, and the cost or ritual of leaving
- `disposition`: how it treats members, outsiders, dependents, defectors, and rivals; distinguish these groups when useful

## Power

- `assets_and_resources`: wealth, territory, law, legitimacy, labor, information, magic, technology, supply, expertise, or public trust
- `forces_and_enforcement`: how it protects interests and compels compliance; not always soldiers
- `leadership`: structure plus only the named people needed for the story
- `internal_divisions`: at least two sides, each with a coherent argument and something to lose

Do not list an asset that the faction cannot maintain at its scale. Separate nominal authority from power it can actually exercise.

## Instability

- `secret`: a concealed truth that changes current choices, methods, or vulnerability
- `current_crisis`: the active problem that makes the faction move now
- `failure_consequence`: what happens to members, dependents, and the setting if it fails
- `succession_or_continuity`: optional; include when leadership loss, reproduction, maintenance, financing, or institutional memory matters

## Relationships

Under `## relationships`, use one subsection per named faction, institution, or character:

```markdown
### The Other Faction
- **public relationship:** Formal allies with a mutual-defense charter.
- **actual relationship:** Each side is trying to control the same transit gate.
- **they want:** Access to the Other Faction's navigators.
- **the other side wants:** Recognition of its disputed charter.
- **contested resource:** The only safe winter pass.
- **breaking point:** Proof that either side negotiated separately with the crown.
```

For the protagonist, also capture:

- what the faction wants from them;
- what they want from the faction;
- the price of help or neutrality;
- the likely trajectory if the protagonist complies, refuses, or exposes the secret.

## Scene pressure

Each field is one present-tense sentence tied to action:

- `prevents`: closes an option the protagonist would otherwise have
- `forces`: imposes a choice, duty, deadline, performance, compromise, or danger
- `enables`: offers access, safety, legitimacy, knowledge, transport, force, or another costly route

Weak: “The order creates political tension.”

Strong: “The order prevents Mira from entering the archive unless she publicly swears the oath that would expose her false identity.”

## Optional story use

- `first_reveal`: the action, consequence, object, or member through which readers first encounter the faction
- `escalation`: how its pressure changes after the protagonist acts
- `payoff`: the decision, reversal, sacrifice, fracture, or alliance through which its role in this story resolves

These are story-specific, not permanent faction canon. Keep spoilers in internal project files, never in a public-facing summary unless the user asks.
