# Genre-Age Compatibility Matrix

This reference helps the audience skill cross-reference a selected genre/subgenre with an age bracket to identify compatibility issues.

## How to Use This File

1. Find the genre row
2. Check the age columns
3. Note compatibility level and any required adjustments

## Compatibility Levels
- **Natural Fit**: The genre and age bracket are commonly paired. No adjustments needed.
- **Compatible**: Works with minor adjustments (usually content heat reduction).
- **Stretch**: Possible but requires significant genre adaptation. Flag to author.
- **Conflict**: The genre's core elements violate the age bracket's constraints. Recommend changing one or the other.

### Adjustment Notes — Fantasy
- **Grimdark for YA**: Possible if you soften graphic violence, remove nihilism, and ensure the protagonist's arc offers growth. See: *Nevernight* by Jay Kristoff as the outer edge.
- **LitRPG for MG**: Simplify the stat interface, reduce information density, focus on the adventure rather than optimization. Games are natural to kids — lean into that.
- **Romantic Fantasy for YA**: Romance stays below explicit. The emotional arc can be intense, but physical content stays at fade-to-black.

### Adjustment Notes — Sci-Fi
- **Hard Sci-Fi for YA**: The science can be real, but explain it through character experience, not exposition. The protagonist learns alongside the reader.
- **Military Sci-Fi for YA**: Boot camp and squad dynamics work for YA. Full-scale war with graphic casualties does not.
- **Cyberpunk for MG**: The anti-corporate themes work. The drugs, explicit content, and nihilism do not. Focus on hacking-as-adventure.

### Adjustment Notes — Romance
- **All romance for Children/MG**: Romance as A-plot does not work for these age brackets. Crushes as subplot only.
- **Dark Romance for NA**: The power dynamics, dubcon, and taboo elements that define dark romance require mature emotional processing. NA is the youngest appropriate bracket, and even then, content warnings are expected.
- **YA Romance**: Always sweet-to-warm heat level. Emotional intensity replaces physical explicitness.

### Adjustment Notes — Horror
- **Horror for Children**: Only works as "spooky," not genuinely terrifying. Goosebumps, not Stephen King. The threat is real but conquerable, and the resolution restores safety.
- **Horror for MG**: Can be genuinely scary, but the protagonist must have agency and the darkness must be conquerable. No nihilistic endings.
- **Body Horror for YA**: The outer edge. Transformation horror (werewolves, mutations) works better than graphic body mutilation.

## Literary Fiction

| Subgenre | Children | Middle Grade | YA | New Adult | Adult |
|----------|----------|-------------|-----|-----------|-------|

| Historical Literary | Conflict | Stretch | Compatible | Natural Fit | Natural Fit |

### Adjustment Notes — Literary
- **Literary for YA**: Works well when the literary voice serves a strong story. Pure literary fiction with minimal plot struggles in YA. See: *The Book Thief* as a successful example.
- **Magical Realism for MG**: A natural fit. The matter-of-fact acceptance of the magical mirrors how children already see the world.
