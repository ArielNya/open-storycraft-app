# Audience: Adult (Ages 25+)

## Reader Profile
- **Age range**: 25+ (no upper limit)
- **Protagonist age**: Any age. Adult fiction is defined by complexity, not protagonist age.
- **Reading level**: Unrestricted
- **Attention span**: Highest tolerance for complexity, delayed gratification, and slow build. But patience is not infinite — every page must earn its place.

## Content Boundaries

All content is unrestricted. What matters is execution, tone, and genre norms.

### Violence
- **Range**: From cozy (zero violence) to grimdark (graphic, visceral, consequence-laden)
- **Rule**: Genre sets the expectation. A cozy mystery reader and a grimdark fantasy reader have completely different tolerance levels. Match the audience, not a universal standard.

### Romance
- **Range**: From fade-to-black to explicit. Genre determines expectations.
- **Rule**: Romance readers expect heat. Thriller readers expect minimal. Literary fiction can go either way. Know your genre's norms.

### Language
- **Range**: From clean to unrestricted.
- **Rule**: Language should match character and setting. A period drama and a modern crime novel have different norms.

### Darkness
- **Range**: From warm/cozy to nihilistic.
- **Rule**: Adult fiction can explore anything. The question is whether the exploration serves the story.

## Tone Variants

Adult fiction spans a wide tonal range. The audience profile should specify which zone:

### Cozy / Warm
- The world is fundamentally safe. Bad things may happen but justice prevails.
- Violence: 1, Romance: 1-3, Language: 1-2, Darkness: 1
- Readers want comfort, charm, satisfying resolution, and a pleasant reading experience.
- Low stakes, high charm. Think: small-town mystery, feel-good fantasy, gentle romance.
- Word count: 65,000-85,000

### General / Mainstream
- The world has real problems but the story believes in solutions.
- Violence: 2-3, Romance: 2-4, Language: 2-3, Darkness: 2-3
- Readers want engagement, emotional resonance, satisfying (not necessarily happy) endings.
- Moderate stakes. Characters face genuine challenges with realistic consequences.
- Word count: 80,000-110,000

### Dark / Gritty
- The world is harsh. Consequences are real and sometimes permanent.
- Violence: 3-5, Romance: 1-5, Language: 3-5, Darkness: 4-5
- Readers want intensity, moral complexity, and unflinching honesty about human nature.
- High stakes. Characters may fail. Victories come at significant cost. Happy endings are earned, not guaranteed.
- Word count: 90,000-150,000+

### Literary
- Prose quality and thematic depth are the product. Plot may be secondary.
- Violence: varies, Romance: varies, Language: varies, Darkness: varies
- Readers want beautiful sentences, deep character interiority, and thematic resonance.
- Stakes may be entirely internal. The "plot" might be a character coming to a realization.
- Word count: 60,000-100,000

## Thematic Expectations

Adult fiction has no mandatory themes. Instead, themes are determined by genre, tone, and story. However, certain themes resonate particularly with adult readers:

- **Consequence and Accountability**: Unlike YA where the system is the enemy, adult fiction often explores what happens when YOU are the system. The compromises of power, parenthood, leadership, marriage.
- **Legacy and Meaning**: What will you leave behind? Does your life matter? What are you building?
- **The Cost of Choices**: Every door you walk through closes others. Career vs. family, safety vs. ambition, loyalty vs. truth.
- **Relationships Under Pressure**: Not first love — love tested by time, failure, betrayal, boredom, children, illness.
- **Moral Complexity**: No clear right answer. The villain has a point. The hero is complicit. The system works for some and destroys others.
- **Mastery and Competence**: Adult readers love watching skilled people do skilled things. The surgeon, the detective, the general, the hacker. Competence porn is a legitimate draw.
- **Mortality**: Time is finite. Bodies fail. People you love die. What do you do with the time you have?

## Vocabulary & Complexity Guidance

- **Sentence complexity**: Fully unrestricted. Can range from punchy commercial prose to dense literary construction.
- **Vocabulary**: No limits. Domain-specific jargon is expected in genre fiction (legal thrillers, hard sci-fi, military fiction).
- **Reference pool**: Unrestricted. Can reference history, politics, philosophy, literature, pop culture, professional domains.
- **Internal monologue**: Ranges from minimal (action/thriller) to central (literary/character-driven). Match the genre and POV style.
- **Abstraction tolerance**: Highest. Can engage with complex philosophical, political, moral, and existential questions at length.

## Structural Norms

- **Chapter length**: Genre-dependent. Thrillers: 2,000-3,000. Fantasy: 3,000-6,000. Literary: 2,000-5,000.
- **Total word count**: 80,000-150,000+ (genre-dependent)
- **Chapter count**: 20-50 (genre-dependent)
- **Pacing**: Genre-dependent. Thrillers are relentless. Literary fiction breathes. Epic fantasy builds slowly. Romance follows a specific emotional arc.
- **Series potential**: All formats work. Standalones, duologies, trilogies, long series (10+), interconnected standalones. Genre determines what's expected.
- **Multiple POV**: Common in fantasy, thriller, and literary fiction. Less common in romance (dual POV max). Single POV dominates in mystery and first-person literary.

## What Makes Adult Different from NA

- **Distance from emotions**: Adult characters (and narrators) have context. They've felt this before. The prose reflects experience, not raw first-contact intensity.
- **Established identity**: The protagonist knows who they are (or thought they did). The crisis is disruption of an established life, not formation of a new one.
- **Wider lens**: Adult fiction can zoom out to societal, political, or historical scope. NA stays close to the individual.
- **Higher tolerance for ambiguity**: Adult readers accept unresolved questions, morally gray endings, and complexity without clean resolution.

## Successful Examples by Tone

### Cozy
- The House in the Cerulean Sea (TJ Klune): Fantasy, found family, gentle stakes, warm resolution
- Thursday Murder Club (Richard Osman): Cozy mystery, elderly protagonists, humor and heart

### General
- Project Hail Mary (Andy Weir): Sci-fi, competence-driven, hopeful, puzzle-solving
- The Martian (Andy Weir): Survival, humor under pressure, engineering as character

### Dark
- The First Law trilogy (Joe Abercrombie): Grimdark fantasy, moral complexity, subverted expectations
- The Road (Cormac McCarthy): Post-apocalyptic, survival, father-son, darkness with thread of love

### Literary
- All the Light We Cannot See (Anthony Doerr): WWII, dual POV, prose-forward, thematic depth
- Lincoln in the Bardo (George Saunders): Experimental, grief, American history, genre-defying
