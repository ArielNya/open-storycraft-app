# Audience: Children (Ages 8-12)

## Reader Profile
- **Age range**: 8-12 years old
- **Protagonist age norm**: 10-12 (1-2 years older than the target reader's upper range)
- **Reading level**: Middle elementary to early middle school
- **Attention span**: Shorter chapters, frequent scene breaks, action-driven pacing

## Content Boundaries

### Violence
- **Allowed**: Peril, danger, conflict, battles with clear stakes
- **Boundaries**: No graphic injury detail, no torture, no realistic war violence
- **Rule**: Consequences exist but are not dwelled upon. A character can be hurt, but you don't describe the wound in visceral detail.
- **Death**: Off-page or implied for significant characters. Monsters/villains can be defeated on-page. No child death.

### Romance
- **Allowed**: Crushes, awkward first feelings, blushing
- **Boundaries**: No kissing beyond a peck. No romantic relationships as plot drivers. No sexual content of any kind.
- **Rule**: Romance is background texture, never the engine.

### Language
- **Allowed**: Mild exclamations ("darn," "crud," "oh no")
- **Boundaries**: No profanity, no slurs, no crude humor beyond what would appear in a Pixar movie
- **Rule**: Characters can be frustrated and express it, but through action and G-rated language.

### Darkness
- **Allowed**: Scary moments, villains with menace, loss of a pet or mentor figure
- **Boundaries**: No nihilism, no hopelessness sustained beyond a chapter, no existential dread
- **Rule**: The world may be dangerous, but it is fundamentally fair. Good effort leads to good outcomes.

## Thematic Expectations

These are the themes children's fiction MUST engage with. Readers (and their parents/teachers/librarians) expect these:

- **Friendship**: The power of loyalty, making and keeping friends, navigating social dynamics
- **Bravery**: Courage is not the absence of fear but acting despite it
- **Fairness**: Right and wrong exist. Injustice should be confronted, not accepted.
- **Discovering Your Place**: Finding where you belong, what makes you special, your role in a group
- **Kindness Over Cruelty**: Empathy wins. Bullies are defeated not by becoming bullies but by refusing to be.
- **Responsibility**: Actions have consequences. Taking ownership matters.
- **Curiosity**: Asking questions is good. The world rewards those who explore it.

## Vocabulary & Complexity Guidance

- **Sentence complexity**: Simple to moderate. Short sentences for action, slightly longer for description. Avoid subordinate clause chains.
- **Vocabulary**: Concrete, active verbs. New/unusual words are fine when context makes meaning clear (this is how kids learn vocabulary). Avoid jargon, abstractions, and philosophical language.
- **Reference pool**: School, family, pets, sports, games, nature, pop culture appropriate for the age (video games, cartoons, comics).
- **Internal monologue**: Light. Children's fiction is externally driven. The protagonist thinks in simple, direct reactions: "That was weird." "She didn't trust him." Not extended introspection.
- **Abstraction tolerance**: Very low. Everything should be concrete and demonstrable through action.

## Structural Norms

- **Chapter length**: 1,500-2,500 words
- **Total word count**: 25,000-45,000 words
- **Chapter count**: 15-25 chapters
- **Pacing**: Fast. Something must happen in every chapter. Long stretches of dialogue or description will lose the reader.
- **Series potential**: Series are king in children's fiction. Plan for 3-7 books. Each book should stand alone while advancing a larger arc.
- **Cliffhangers**: End every chapter with a hook, but resolve the book's main conflict within the book itself.

## Structural Rules

- **No POV switching** within a chapter (kids get confused)
- **Clear cause-and-effect** chains (readers need to follow the logic)
- **Villains must be comprehensible** (scary is fine, but their motivation should make sense to a child)
- **Adults are present but not the solution** (the child protagonist must solve the core problem through their own agency)
- **The ending must deliver justice** (not necessarily "everyone is happy," but fairness must be restored)

## Successful Examples (Pattern Reference)

Children's fiction that nails these constraints:
- Percy Jackson series: danger + humor + mythology, protagonist solves problems, adults help but don't save
- Harry Potter (books 1-3): school setting, friendship core, escalating threat, clear moral framework
- Wings of Fire: animal POV, complex politics simplified for kids, violence present but not graphic
- The Wild Robot: nature setting, found family, emotional depth without darkness
