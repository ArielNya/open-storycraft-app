# Sentence Structure & Variation

Avoid repetitive "She did X. She did Y." patterns by rotating structures.

---

## Structure Types

### 1. Action-First
Lead with motion before subject.

> Scanner raised, she moved through the corridor.
> Fist clenched, he turned away.
> Breath held, they waited.

### 2. Inverted Clauses
Rearrange for emphasis.

> From the shadows came the sound.
> Into the silence fell his words.
> Through the door burst the guards.

### 3. Prepositional Leads
Start with time/place.

> In the dim light, his expression was unreadable.
> After three days, the silence broke.
> Beneath the surface, something moved.

### 4. Compressed Compounds
Stack actions for urgency. Use two or four+ items to avoid the rule of threes.

> She ducked and rolled.
> He grabbed the keys and hit the switch.
> She stopped and breathed.

### 5. Absolute Constructions
Background detail while action continues.

> Heart pounding, she reached for the door.
> Eyes fixed on the horizon, he waited.
> Gun drawn, she entered.

## Pacing by Tension Level

### Low Tension
- Longer sentences
- More observation and description
- Internal thought allowed to wander
- Slower, wider lens

**Example**: "The afternoon stretched out before them, lazy and warm, the kind of day where nothing urgent could possibly exist. She watched the clouds drift, cotton-white against blue, and let her mind empty."

### Rising Tension
- Sentences shorten
- More action verbs
- Internal thought becomes focused
- Pacing accelerates

**Example**: "Something was wrong. The street was too quiet. She checked her watch. Five minutes late. Her hand found her phone."

### High Tension
- Short, punchy complete sentences
- Rapid cuts between actions
- Minimal internal thought
- Reader swept along
- NO fragments - short complete sentences create urgency

**Example**: "The door was locked. The window was too high. Footsteps echoed on the stairs. They were getting closer. She grabbed the chair."

### Aftermath
- Return to longer sentences
- Processing and reflection
- Physical sensation of come-down
- Echoes opening style

**Example**: "It was over. She sat against the wall, chest heaving, hands still shaking. The room was silent now except for her ragged breathing. She'd made it. Somehow."

## Pattern Breaking

### The Three-Beat Problem
If you write three sentences in a row with the same structure, readers notice. Mix it up:

**Repetitive**:
> She opened the door. She stepped inside. She looked around.

**Varied**:
> The door swung open at her touch. Inside was only darkness. She waited for her eyes to adjust.

### Paragraph Rhythm
Each paragraph should have internal variation. Start long, go short. Or start short, expand. Don't stay flat.
