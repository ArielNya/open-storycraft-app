# Age-Appropriate Voice

What a character notices - and how they describe it - changes with age. A 12-year-old and a 45-year-old in the same room will register completely different details.

---

## Age Cohorts

### Child (8-12)

**What They Notice**
- Immediate physical sensations (hungry, bored, tired)
- Whether adults seem angry or pleased
- Fairness and unfairness (keen sense of justice)
- Sensory extremes (loud, smelly, bright)
- Animals and unusual things
- Whether other kids are present

**What They Wouldn't Notice**
- Political subtext
- Romantic tension between adults
- Economic indicators (expensive furniture)
- Professional hierarchy
- Subtle emotional states

**Voice Markers**
- Simple vocabulary, concrete comparisons
- Present-focused (what's happening NOW)
- Emotional reactions unfiltered
- Adults as slightly alien beings
- Rules as arbitrary but important

---

### Teen (13-17)

**What They Notice**
- Social hierarchy and status signals
- Whether they're being watched/judged
- Attractiveness of peers
- Hypocrisy in adults
- Cool vs. uncool markers
- Whether they're being treated like a child

**What They Wouldn't Notice**
- Professional nuance
- Long-term consequences
- Subtle manipulation
- Financial concerns
- Their own contradictions

**Voice Markers**
- Slang and current references
- Self-conscious awareness
- Dramatic internal reactions
- Adults as obstacles or allies
- Strong opinions, flexible principles

---

### Young Adult (18-25)

**What They Notice**
- Physical discomfort first (hungry, cold, need to piss)
- Random specific details (someone's weird haircut)
- Whether that person was checking them out
- Pop culture comparisons
- Status relative to peers
- Wandering attention even in serious moments

**What They Wouldn't Notice**
- Long-term political implications
- Institutional history
- Subtle power dynamics
- Health concerns
- Their own mortality

**Voice Markers**
- Casual profanity
- Self-deprecating humor
- Confidence performed more than felt
- References to recent media
- Quick judgments, willing to revise

**Example (Young Adult)**
> Someone had left a half-eaten sandwich on the console. Rowan wondered who abandons a sandwich in the middle of a crisis. The station smelled like old socks and burnt coffee. Someone's mug was still sitting on a workstation, growing something green.

Note: Random details, physical discomfort, humor amid tension.

---

### Adult (26-45)

**What They Notice**
- Professional dynamics and hierarchy
- What things cost
- Competence and incompetence
- Long-term implications
- Who's actually in charge
- Warning signs from experience

**What They Wouldn't Notice**
- Youth culture details
- Physical discomfort (more stoic)
- Pop culture references (dated ones instead)
- Idealistic possibilities
- Their own assumptions

**Voice Markers**
- Measured observations
- Pattern recognition
- Awareness of consequences
- Professional vocabulary
- Cynicism tempered by pragmatism

---

### Elder (46+)

**What They Notice**
- Historical parallels
- What hasn't changed
- Physical limitations (stairs, chairs, temperature)
- Youth's naivety and energy
- Institutional memory
- What actually matters vs. noise

**What They Wouldn't Notice**
- Current technology details
- Youth slang
- Contemporary references
- Urgency for its own sake
- New social dynamics

**Voice Markers**
- Longer view perspective
- Comparisons to past events
- Physical awareness (body complaints)
- Patience or impatience with youth
- Wisdom or bitterness (depends on character)

---

## Mental Comparisons By Age

The metaphors and comparisons a character uses reveal their age.

| Age | Comparison Style |
|-----|------------------|
| Child | "Like in that cartoon" / "Like when Mom..." |
| Teen | Current memes, TikTok, whatever's trending |
| Young Adult | Recent movies, games, internet culture |
| Adult | Classic references, professional analogies |
| Elder | Historical events, "back when..." |

---

## Physical Discomfort Priority

Younger characters prioritize physical sensation. This decreases with age (or increases again in elderly).

**Young adult internal monologue:**
> His back ached from sleeping on the shuttle floor. When did he last eat? The briefing room was freezing. He needed coffee.

**Middle-aged adult internal monologue:**
> The briefing started late. Typical. He reviewed the agenda while waiting.

---

## Wandering Attention

Young characters' attention wanders, even in serious moments.

**During a combat briefing:**
> Theo was explaining the entry points. Rowan nodded along, but part of his brain was wondering what was for dinner. Also, who cut Theo's hair? It looked like someone had used a bowl.

This isn't character flaw - it's realistic young-person cognition.

---

## What They'd NEVER Notice

| Age | Blind Spots |
|-----|-------------|
| Child | Adult motivations, money, romance |
| Teen | Their own hypocrisy, long-term consequences |
| Young Adult | Their mortality, institutional history, subtle manipulation |
| Adult | Youth culture, physical invincibility, idealism |
| Elder | Current trends, quick adaptation, new social norms |

---

## Implementation

When writing, ask:
1. How old is my POV character?
2. What would someone this age actually notice first?
3. What comparisons would they naturally make?
4. What are they blind to?

Then filter ALL descriptions through these answers.
