# Intimate Scene Writing

Detailed guidance for romance and erotica. Adapt based on heat level and story tone.

---

## Heat Level Calibration

Define the heat level in the style guide. This shapes everything else.

| Level | What's Shown | Internal Monologue | Language |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|----------|
| **Fade to Black** | Lead-up, then skip | Anticipation, aftermath | Suggestive |
| **Closed Door** | Some touching, implied rest | Desire, then cut | Sensual |
| **Steamy** | Most action, tasteful language | Present but controlled | Evocative |
| **Explicit** | Full detail, direct language | Raw and present | Frank |
| **Gratuitous** | Extended, graphic detail | Chaotic, overwhelming | Crude/clinical mix |

Choose ONE level for the story. Consistency matters.

## Age-Appropriate Intimacy

How experience level affects the scene.

### Inexperienced (First times, young adults)

**What it IS:**
- Urgent, almost desperate
- Sometimes clumsy
- Overwhelming because everything is new
- Can't believe this is actually happening
- Focused on raw sensation, not technique

**What it ISN'T:**
- Smooth porn-star choreography
- Confident dirty talk
- Knowing exactly what to do
- Lasting forever

**Internal monologue:**
> His mouth was actually on her. Right there. She had imagined this but the reality was— Oh god. She needed more.

Thoughts interrupted by sensation. Disbelieving. Desperate. Use em dashes for interrupted thoughts.

### Experienced (Adults, established relationships)

**What it IS:**
- Confident but not mechanical
- Communication (verbal or nonverbal)
- Knowing what works
- Savoring rather than rushing
- Emotional connection layered with physical

**What it ISN'T:**
- Frantically urgent (unless circumstances demand it)
- Constantly surprised by sensation
- Fumbling with basics

**Internal monologue:**
> She knew exactly what that look meant. And she knew exactly how to answer it.

Assured. Anticipatory. Present.

## Internal Monologue During Intimacy

The POV character's thoughts during sex should match their experience and emotional state.

### Inexperienced/Overwhelmed
- Short, urgent sentences
- Thoughts interrupted by sensation (use em dash for interruption)
- Disbelief that this is happening
- Sensation overload
- "More" and "please" and "oh god"
- NOT poetic or articulate

**Example:**
> His hands were actually on her. She could not think. Could not form a complete— Oh. She needed more of that.

### Confident/In Control
- More coherent
- Anticipation and intention
- Awareness of partner's reactions
- Savoring rather than drowning

**Example:**
> She watched his face as she moved. There. That expression. She filed it away for later.

### Emotional/Meaningful
- Slower internal rhythm
- Connection over sensation
- Awareness of the moment's significance
- Still physical, but weighted

**Example:**
> This was different. Everything before had been fun, but this. This meant something. She let herself feel it.

## The ASMR Trap

The biggest mistake in intimate scenes: over-describing sensation until it becomes numbing.

### Signs You're In The Trap
- Paragraphs of texture descriptions
- Every micro-movement catalogued
- Scene feels like a massage parlor recording
- Reader is relaxed instead of engaged
- You've described the same sensation three different ways

### How To Avoid It
- Focus on core action, not peripheral detail
- Character reaction > physical description
- Keep descriptions punchy, not lingering
- Cut 30% of your sensory detail on revision
- If it sounds like it belongs in a spa menu, delete it

**Too Much:**
> His fingers traced along her spine, each vertebra a separate point of contact, the warmth of his touch spreading through her skin like honey, slow and golden and impossibly sweet, the texture of his calluses catching slightly on her smooth skin as he explored every inch, every curve, every—

**Just Right:**
> His hand slid down her spine. She arched into it.

The second one moves. The first one lingers.

## No Poetry In Bed

Kill your darlings. If it sounds like a poem, cut it.

### Forbidden
- Extended metaphors for body parts
- Flowery descriptions of sensation
- Literary comparisons during climax
- Anything that sounds like a romance novel parody

### Instead
- Direct language
- Specific physical details
- Raw reaction
- Character-authentic observations

**Poetic (cut this):**
> He was the ocean and she was drowning, willingly, eagerly, letting the waves of sensation crash over her as she surrendered to the tide of—

**Direct (keep this):**
> She stopped thinking. She just felt his hands, his mouth, the weight of him. She needed more.

## Climax Descriptions

Vary the vocabulary. Don't repeat the same words.

### Female POV Options
- Shatter, break apart, come undone
- Waves, pulses, ripples
- Vision whites out, goes bright
- Muscles clench, body arches
- Cry out, moan, scream
- Collapse, melt, go boneless

### Male POV Options
- Surge, release, let go
- Shudder, pulse, empty
- Grunt, groan, his voice breaks
- Tension snaps, coils release
- Grip tightens, rhythm falters

### Avoid
- Same word twice in one scene
- Clinical terms (unless character's voice demands it)
- Euphemisms that pull reader out ("his member," "her flower")
- Purple prose ("exploded into a thousand stars of ecstasy")

## Heat Escalation Across Story

How explicit content should build.

### Early Story (Teasing)
- Suggestion only
- Almost-moments interrupted
- Internal monologue notices, wants, doesn't act
- Physical awareness without action
- Building tension

### Mid Story (First Contact)
- Over clothes, hands exploring
- First kisses, first touches
- Still nervous, can't believe it's happening
- Interruptions still likely
- Heat rising but not boiling

### Later Story (Full Heat)
- Explicit scenes with detail
- Multiple encounters possible
- Characters more confident
- Emotional stakes integrated with physical
- Less fumbling, more intention

### Late Story (Variation/Deepening)
- New dynamics (threesome, kink, power exchange)
- Emotional weight increases
- Scenes mean something beyond physical
- Complexity in what characters want
- Setup for resolution

## Comedy in Intimate Scenes

Erotica can be funny. Timing matters.

### The Pattern
Build tension → Almost there → INTERRUPTION → Scramble → Aftermath humor

### Comedy Sources
- Walked-in-on situations
- Wardrobe malfunctions
- Inconvenient timing (phone rings, door knocks)
- Body making unexpected sounds
- Saying the wrong name
- Awkward positions not working
- "Did you hear something?"

### Aftermath Humor
The scramble to look innocent. The terrible excuse. The knowing look from whoever interrupted.

**Example:**
> By the time the door opened, they stood five feet apart, breathing hard, faces flushed with absolutely nothing suspicious happening.
>
> "You two okay?" Mom asked. "You look warm."
>
> "Fine," they said together. Too fast. Too high-pitched.

### What NOT To Do
- Laugh-track obvious jokes
- Slapstick that breaks immersion
- Making fun of characters during vulnerable moments
- Comedy that undermines emotional stakes

## Environmental Aphrodisiacs

The setting can do some of the work.

### Heat/Humidity
- Clothes stick, come off easier
- Inhibitions melt with composure
- Sweat makes everything slick
- Hard to think straight

### Isolation
- No interruptions possible
- Different rules apply
- Permission to let go
- What happens here stays here

### Confined Spaces
- Forced proximity
- Can't escape awareness of each other
- Accidental contact
- Tension with nowhere to go

### After Danger/Adrenaline
- "We survived" urgency
- Need to feel alive
- Grabbed each other without thinking
- Adrenaline redirected

## Scene Choreography

Keep track of bodies in space.

### The Basics
- Where is everyone?
- What's already been removed?
- What position are they in?
- Where are hands/mouths right now?

### Common Errors
- Three hands when character only has two
- Clothes removed twice
- Position changes without transition
- Physically impossible configurations
- Forgetting secondary characters in group scenes

### Fix
- Quick mental diagram
- Note position changes explicitly
- Track clothing status
- In group scenes, check in with each participant
