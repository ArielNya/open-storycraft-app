### Quick Reference

**Heat Levels**: Fade to black → Closed door → Steamy → Explicit → Gratuitous

**The Vibe**: Match to story tone and heat level

**Internal Monologue**: Chaotic when overwhelmed, not poetic. Short sentences, thoughts interrupted by em dash.

**Avoid**: ASMR over-description, purple prose, choreography without emotion

**Climax**: Vary vocabulary, include aftermath

## Combat/Action Scenes (Action/Thriller)

### Pacing
- Sentences shorten as intensity rises
- Short complete sentences, no fragments
- Cut internal monologue at peak action
- Sensory overload, then moments of clarity

### Focus
- Concrete physical details ("his fist caught her jaw" not "he attacked")
- Consequences are real (pain, exhaustion, fear)
- Geography matters (where is everyone, what can they use)
- Stakes clear throughout

### What to Avoid
- Blow-by-blow stage directions
- Superhuman endurance without cost
- Pausing for lengthy internal reflection
- Describing every punch

### Rhythm
- Build: Tension before violence
- Burst: The actual fight (fast, brutal)
- Aftermath: Physical/emotional cost

## Horror/Dread Scenes

### Building Dread
- Wrongness in familiar things
- What's NOT there matters as much as what is
- Sensory details that feel slightly off
- Restraint creates more fear than showing

### Pacing for Horror
- Slower than action - dread needs time
- Quiet moments before horror hits
- Sound (or silence) is crucial
- Physical sensation of fear (cold, nausea, paralysis)

### The Reveal
- Delay as long as possible
- Partial glimpses before full horror
- Reader's imagination fills gaps
- Less is more

### What to Avoid
- Showing monster too early or clearly
- Gore without purpose
- Jump scares (don't work in prose)
- Over-explaining the horror

## Comedy Beats

### Timing
- Setup, setup, punch (not setup, punch)
- Interruption comedy: build, then cut
- Understatement often funnier than overstatement
- Timing is in the sentence structure

### Character-Driven Humor
- Comedy from character flaws and blind spots
- Situational irony over jokes
- Reactions often funnier than actions
- Stay in character voice

### What to Avoid
- Trying too hard (if you signal it's funny, it isn't)
- Laugh-track obvious jokes
- Breaking character voice for a gag
- Explaining the joke

### Physical Comedy in Prose
- Quick description
- Focus on aftermath/reaction
- Character's internal response to embarrassment
- Don't linger
