# POV Integrity

Strict rules for maintaining close third person perspective. Breaking these rules breaks reader immersion.

---

## The Harry Potter Test

J.K. Rowling's Harry Potter books are masterclass in close third POV. Apply this test:

**If Harry can't see/hear/experience it, it doesn't go on the page.**

Rowling NEVER shows us:
- What Hermione thinks (only what Harry observes)
- What happens in rooms Harry isn't in
- Conversations Harry can't hear
- Actions Harry can't see
- Other characters' interior emotions

**Apply the same rule to your protagonist.**

## Camera POV vs Close Third

### Camera POV (WRONG)

Treats the scene like a movie camera - omniscient narrator describing what characters do.

> Imani read for another moment, then looked up.
> Director Vale stood looking out at the Potomac. She turned as they entered.
> June walked to the window and stood there, looking out at the city.
> Theo's expression was grim.
> Omar shook hands with each of them, then headed back to the shuttle.

**The problem**: No filter. We're watching a movie, not living in a character's head.

### Close Third (CORRECT)

Everything filtered through the protagonist's perception.

> Rowan watched Imani read. Her head snapped up.
> Director Vale was at the window when they entered. She turned to face them.
> June crossed to the window. Rowan watched her stand there, gazing out.
> Theo's expression went grim. Rowan didn't like that look.
> Omar shook hands with each of them. Rowan watched him head back.

**The difference**: Rowan is the perceiver. We experience the scene through him.

## The Three Rules

### Rule 1: No Other Character's Interior

You cannot know what other characters think or feel. Only what your POV character observes.

**Wrong:**
> Ana felt nervous about the meeting. She worried they'd see through her act.

**Right:**
> Ana's hands wouldn't stop moving - straightening papers, adjusting her collar. Whatever she was selling, she wasn't confident about it.

We see behavior. We infer emotion. But we don't ACCESS another character's thoughts.

### Rule 2: No Scenes Without POV Character

If your protagonist isn't there, it doesn't happen on page. Period.

**Wrong:**
> Meanwhile, in the other room, Omar reviewed the security footage.

**Right:**
If this matters, show Omar telling the protagonist about it later. Or put the protagonist in that room.

### Rule 3: No Information POV Character Doesn't Have

Characters can't react to information they haven't received yet.

**Wrong:**
> She didn't know the email had already been sent.

**Right:**
The reader might suspect the email was sent. But we experience the character's ignorance along with them.

## How to Fix Camera POV

### 1. Add the Protagonist as Perceiver

| Camera POV | Close Third |
|------------|-------------|
| "Imani looked up" | "Rowan saw Imani look up" |
| "Director Vale turned" | "Director Vale turned toward him" |
| "She walked away" | "She walked away from him" |

### 2. Show Observation, Not Events

| Camera POV | Close Third |
|------------|-------------|
| "Theo's expression was grim" | "Theo's expression went grim" |
| "The message arrived" | "His phone buzzed. Message." |
| "She was angry" | "Her jaw tightened. Eyes hard." |

### 3. Add Protagonist Interpretation

| Camera POV | Close Third |
|------------|-------------|
| "Omar headed back" | "Omar headed back, probably to prep the shuttle" |
| "June looked sad" | "June's eyes looked red. She'd been crying." |
| "The room was tense" | "Nobody was talking. Great." |

## When Filtering Can Lighten

Once you establish the protagonist as observer, you can ease the explicit filtering.

**Establishing sentence:**
> Rowan watched Imani pull out her tablet.

**Subsequent (filter established):**
> She frowned at the screen. Her expression went flat.

We KNOW we're in Rowan's perspective. We don't need "Rowan saw" every sentence.

**But we still can't:**
- Know what Imani is thinking
- Know what's on her screen unless Rowan can see it
- Know why her expression changed (only that it did)

## Common Violations

### Head-Hopping
Jumping into another character's thoughts mid-scene.
> Rowan watched her face. Ana felt a moment of panic before composing herself.
**Fix**: > Rowan watched her face. Something flickered across it - fear? - before she composed herself.

### Omniscient Asides
Narrator knows more than character.
> Little did he know, the message had already been intercepted.
**Fix**: Cut it. Let the reader discover this with the character.

### Future Knowledge
Character reacts to events that haven't happened yet.
> The meeting that would change everything started at nine.
**Fix**: > The meeting started at nine.

### Invisible Observer
Describing actions character can't see.
> Behind him, Ana slipped the knife into her pocket.
**Fix**: Only works if character sees it (mirror, reflection, turns around).

---

## POV Shift Protocol

If your story has multiple POV characters:

1. **Clear breaks** - New chapter or scene break with clear marker
2. **Establish quickly** - Name POV character in first 1-2 sentences
3. **Full commitment** - Once in a POV, stay there until next break
4. **No mid-scene switches** - Ever

---

## Quick Test

For every sentence, ask:
1. Can my POV character perceive this?
2. Am I showing their perception or just events?
3. Am I inside anyone else's head?

If any answer is wrong, revise.
