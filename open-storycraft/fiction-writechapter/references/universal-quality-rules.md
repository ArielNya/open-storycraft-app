# Universal Quality Rules

Apply these unless style.md explicitly contradicts.

---

## Narrative Voice

### Limited POV
When using limited POV (first person or third limited):
- Narrative prose IS the character's internal experience
- Descriptions reflect what POV character notices and feels
- Opinions, reactions, judgments woven INTO description
- NOT italicized asides or separate internal monologue

### What They Notice
POV character's attention reveals character:
- A suspicious person notices exits and hands
- A romantic person notices gestures and tension
- A practical person notices tools and efficiency

### Emotional Coloring
Don't describe objectively. Describe through the character's emotional state:
- **Neutral**: "The room was small."
- **Anxious**: "The room pressed in. Too small. Walls too close."
- **Comfortable**: "The room felt intimate. Contained."

---

## Show, Don't Tell

### Emotions Through Action
Never state emotions directly. Show through:
- Physical reactions (heart rate, breathing, muscle tension)
- Micro-actions (what hands do, where eyes go)
- Internal sensation (stomach drops, chest tightens)
- Behavioral changes (speech patterns shift)

**Don't**: She felt afraid.
**Do**: Her hand found the wall. Steadied herself. Breathed.

### Character Through Behavior
What they do reveals who they are:
- Choices in pressure moments
- How they treat people who can't help them
- What they notice and ignore

### Relationships Through Interaction
Don't tell us they're friends. Show:
- Shorthand communication
- Comfortable silences
- Knowing references
- How they disagree

## Dialogue

### Tags
- Prefer "said" and "asked" - they're invisible
- Action beats often better than tags
- Occasionally: "muttered," "shouted," "whispered" (volume only)
- Never: "breathed," "mused," "quipped," "retorted"

### Distinct Voices
Each character speaks differently (check characters file):
- Vocabulary level
- Sentence length
- Topics they gravitate toward
- Verbal tics

### Subtext
Best dialogue has gap between what's said and what's meant:
- Characters avoid certain topics
- They deflect with humor
- They answer different questions than asked

---

## Structure

### Sentence Variety
Vary openings - not every sentence starts with "She":
- Action-first: "Scanner raised, she moved forward."
- Prepositional: "In the dim light, his face was unreadable."
- Inverted: "From the shadows came the sound."

### Paragraph Rhythm
Each paragraph should have internal variation:
- Don't cluster similar sentence lengths
- Mix long and short
- End paragraphs with punch or transition

### Chapter Endings
End with forward momentum:
- Hook (question raised)
- Shift (new information)
- Decision (character commits)
- NOT cliffhanger every time (varies tension)

---

## Pacing

### Match Tension to Prose
- Low tension: longer sentences, more observation
- Rising tension: sentences shorten, more action verbs
- High tension: fragments allowed, rapid cuts
- Aftermath: return to longer sentences, processing

### Scene Transitions
- White space or symbol break
- Don't over-explain time gaps
- First line of new scene orients reader quickly
