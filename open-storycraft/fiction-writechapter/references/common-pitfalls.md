# Common Pitfalls to Avoid

Watch for these while writing.

---

## AI-ism Phrases

These mark amateur or AI-generated prose. Never use:

- "Couldn't help but [verb]"
- "Found herself [verbing]" / "Found himself [verbing]"
- "A mix of X and Y" / "A mixture of" / "A combination of"
- "Something akin to"
- "A sense of"
- "Not X but Y" / "It wasn't X, it was Y"
- "The kind of [X] that..."
- "Practiced precision" / "Practiced ease"
- "With a knowing [noun]"
- "Quiet efficiency"
- "Seemed to [verb]" (when you mean they did)

---

## Purple Prose Words

Avoid these overused literary words:

- Cacophony
- Symphony (unless literal music)
- Tapestry / tapestries (metaphorical)
- Testament
- Sentinel
- Verdant
- Cerulean
- Palpable
- Tangible (for emotions)
- Mellifluous
- "Sun kissed the horizon"

---

## Punctuation Problems

### Em Dashes
- ONLY for dialogue interruptions: "I was just—" "No."
- NEVER in narrative prose
- Replace with periods, commas, or colons

### Overuse
- Semicolons: use sparingly
- Ellipses: trailing off in dialogue only, not dramatic pauses
- Exclamation points: almost never in narrative

---

## Structural Tells

### Rule of Threes
Triadic lists are an AI writing tell:
- "Cold, dark, and empty" - avoid
- Three-beat descriptions - avoid

Use two items, or four+, or rephrase.

### Repetitive Sentence Starts
- "She did X. She did Y. She did Z."
- Vary openings with different structures

### Monotonous Rhythm
- All sentences similar length
- All paragraphs similar length
- Mix it up

---

## Crutch Words

Search and destroy:

- "Just" (unless essential)
- "Really" / "Very"
- "Suddenly"
- "And then" (starting sentences)
- "Began to" / "Started to" (just do the action)
- "That" (often unnecessary)
- "Seemed to" (either they did or didn't)

---

## Dialogue Tag Errors

### Never Use
- "Breathed"
- "Murmured" (unless actual murmur)
- "Intoned"
- "Mused"
- "Quipped"
- "Retorted"
- "Exclaimed"

### Never Personify Voices
- NO: "His voice carried weight"
- NO: "Her voice was velvet"
- YES: "He spoke quietly"
- YES: "She dropped her voice"

---

## ASMR Over-Description

Avoid excessive sensory detail without purpose:

- Lingering on irrelevant textures
- Redundant descriptions of same state
- Decorative language that doesn't serve story

Every sensory detail should reveal character or advance tension.

---

## Continuity Traps

- Character names inconsistent
- Location details contradict world.md
- Timeline doesn't track
- Injuries/conditions forgotten
- Information characters shouldn't have

Check characters file and previous chapters.

---

## Robotic Voice Drift

A protagonist can be observant, tactical, intellectual, or practical without sounding like a processor.

Watch for narration that turns lived experience into:
- calculations, systems, variables, data, architecture, geometry, or logic
- therapy-style emotional diagnosis
- craft-outline labels such as beat, arc, midpoint, reversal, dark night, or theme
- logging verbs such as registered, filed, clocked, tracked, processed, or generated

If a motif appears repeatedly, keep it local and embodied. Do not let one motif become the syntax for every scene.

Fix by translating abstraction into:
- body: jaw, hands, throat, breath, stomach
- object: bill, door, phone, knife, cup, steering wheel
- action: stepping back, swallowing, looking away, putting something down
- silence: what the character refuses to say
- direct thought: short, biased, character-native
