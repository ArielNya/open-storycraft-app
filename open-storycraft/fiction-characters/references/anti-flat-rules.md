# Anti-Flat Character Rules

Dynamic characters drive dynamic stories. These rules prevent flat, predictable casts.

---

## Every Character Must Have

### 1. WANT (External Goal)
What they're actively pursuing. This drives their behavior and creates conflict when wants clash.

### 2. NEED (Internal Requirement)
What they actually require for fulfillment - often different from (or opposed to) their want. The gap between want and need creates internal tension.

### 3. FLAW (Real Problem)
A character trait that causes actual problems in the story. Not a "cute flaw" - something that hurts them or others.

**Weak flaws** (avoid): "Too loyal", "Works too hard", "Cares too much"
**Strong flaws**: Refuses to trust, prioritizes pride over people, cannot admit mistakes

### 4. SECRET
Something they're hiding OR something they know that others don't. Secrets create tension, reveal opportunities, and drive betrayals.

---

## Antagonist Requirements

The antagonist is NOT pure evil. They MUST have:

### Legitimate Grievance or Motivation
They want something reasonable, or have been genuinely wronged. The reader should be able to see their point.

**Weak**: "Wants power because evil"
**Strong**: "Wants power because their family was destroyed by the current system"

### At Least One Sympathetic Quality
Something that makes the reader understand them:
- Love for family
- Loyalty to a cause
- Genuine belief they're helping
- Past trauma that explains (not excuses) behavior

### Reason They Believe They're Right
From their perspective, their actions make sense. They have justified their choices internally.

---

## Ally Requirements

At least ONE ally must have:

### Conflicting Agenda
Their goals partially conflict with the protagonist's. This creates natural tension within the team.

**Example**: Ally wants to rescue the kingdom, but also wants revenge on a specific person the protagonist needs alive.

### Potential to Complicate
They can make situations harder, not just easier. They have their own values that might clash with "the mission."

---

## Voice Differentiation

### No Two Characters Sound the Same

Each character needs distinct:
- **Vocabulary level** (educated vs. street vs. formal)
- **Sentence rhythm** (short and clipped vs. rambling vs. measured)
- **Topics they gravitate toward** (what they talk about when nervous)
- **Topics they avoid** (what they never discuss)
- **Verbal tics** (repeated phrases, filler words, speech patterns)

### Test: The Untagged Dialogue Test

If you remove dialogue tags, can you still tell who's speaking? If not, the voices aren't distinct enough.

---

## Red Flags to Avoid

- **The Perfect Friend**: Ally with no agenda, no flaws, exists only to support protagonist
- **The Pure Evil Villain**: Antagonist with no motivation beyond "being bad"
- **The Identical Squad**: Multiple allies who could be interchanged with no story impact
- **The Static Character**: Someone who never changes, learns, or grows
- **The Exposition Dispenser**: Character who exists only to explain things
