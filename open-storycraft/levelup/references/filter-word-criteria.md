# Filter Word Criteria — LevelUp

## What Is a Filter Word?

A filter word places the POV character between the reader and the scene. Instead of letting the reader experience the moment directly, it tells the reader that the character is experiencing it. This creates an extra layer of distance and weakens immersion.

**Core principle:** Every filter word is a missed opportunity to be inside the moment. Cut the filter and put the reader there instead.

---

## Master Filter Word List

### Perception (Sight)
appear, appear like, become aware of, catch sight of, detect, discern, distinguish, give the impression of, identify, look, look like, note, notice, observe, perceive, realize, recognize, reveal, seem, sense, sight, spot, watch, see, gaze, glance, stare, survey

### Perception (Sound)
catch, eavesdrop, overhear, listen to, sound, sound like, hear

### Perception (Touch)
feel, feel like

### Perception (Smell)
detect the smell of, get a whiff of, scent, smell like, whiff, smell

### Perception (Taste)
appreciate, delight in, enjoy, relish, savor, taste, take pleasure in

### Cognition (Knowing / Thinking)
ascertain, assume, believe, bring to mind, decide, deem, discover, gather, get, glean, guess, infer, intuit, learn, posit, regard, remember, suspect, think, understand, wonder, realize, know

### Experience (Passive Suffering)
be subjected to, face, go through, live through, suffer, take in, undergo, experience

### Ability (Hedging)
be capable of, be equal to, can, could, have the ability to, have what it takes

## How to Fix Them

### Rule 1: Cut the filter, promote the sensation
The detail itself implies perception. Let the reader experience it directly.

- Weak: `Rowan felt the heat from the blast wash over him.`
- Strong: `Heat from the blast rolled over him in a wall.`

- Weak: `He saw a grenade land in the foxhole.`
- Strong: `A grenade landed in the foxhole.`

- Weak: `She heard horns blaring in the street below.`
- Strong: `Horns blared in the street below.`

### Rule 2: Use active verbs to replace perception verbs
Let the world act on the character instead of the character sensing the world.

- Weak: `Rowan noticed the ship was listing to starboard.`
- Strong: `The ship listed to starboard.`

- Weak: `She realized she'd made a mistake.`
- Strong: `She'd made a mistake.` (or: `The error landed on her like a weight.`)

### Rule 3: Replace cognition filters with direct interiority
When a character thinks or decides something, just say the thing.

- Weak: `He thought that the situation was getting out of hand.`
- Strong: `The situation was getting out of hand.`

- Weak: `She wondered whether she could trust him.`
- Strong: `Could she trust him?`

### Rule 4: Let dialogue and body language carry the perception
Reaction shows awareness without a filter word.

- Weak: `Arno heard the ringing and winced.`
- Strong: `"Will this infernal ringing never stop?" Arno pressed his palms to his ears.`

### Rule 5: Watch for double filters
Using two filter verbs in sequence is doubly weak.

- Weak: `She looked and saw the door was open.`
- Weak: `He listened and heard footsteps.`
- Fix: Cut both and lead with the detail.

---

## When to Keep a Filter Word

Not every instance must be cut. Keep a filter when:

1. **The act of perception IS the point.** If the story is about whether the character can see or hear something (stealth, blindness, confusion), the filter carries meaning.
   - `He strained to hear her voice through the static.` — The straining is the content.

2. **Distinguishing whose perception it is matters.** In multi-POV or crowd scenes where attribution is necessary.

3. **The filter is doing emotional work.** `She realized she still loved him.` — The moment of realization is the beat, not just the fact.

When in doubt: cut it and read the sentence aloud. If it sounds stronger, cut stays.

---

## Close-third POV Notes

This is a close-third POV novel. The POV narration should feel like his mind processing the world, not a camera reporting what he perceives. Every filter word is a step away from his voice.

The POV voice is wry and direct. His processing is fast and opinionated. Filter words make the prose slow and neutral — both are wrong for his character.

- Wrong: `Rowan felt unease settle in his chest.`
- Right: `Something was very wrong.` (or a physical reaction that implies it)

- Wrong: `He noticed June wasn't looking at him.`
- Right: `June wasn't looking at him.`
