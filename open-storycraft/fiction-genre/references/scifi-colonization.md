# Colonization

**Definition**: Settling new worlds — terraforming, cultural adaptation, conflict with indigenous life or other factions, and the challenge of building civilization from scratch.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **New World**: A planet, moon, or habitat being settled for the first time.
- **Adaptation**: Humans adjusting to an alien environment.
- **Community Building**: Establishing governance, culture, and infrastructure.
- **Environmental Challenges**: The new world fights back — weather, biology, geology.

## Optional Tropes
- **Terraforming**: Transforming an alien world to support human life.
- **Indigenous Life**: Pre-existing organisms or intelligence on the target world.
- **Corporate Colony**: Colonization driven by profit rather than exploration.
- **Generation Gap**: Children born on the new world who identify differently than Earth-born parents.
- **Supply Line Problems**: Isolation from Earth, delayed communication, self-sufficiency pressure.
- **Colonial Politics**: Who leads, who decides, who owns the land.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: The pioneer fantasy — building something new on the frontier.
- **Resolution**: The colony survives its crisis. The settlers find a way to belong.
- **Pacing**: Moderate. World-building and community dynamics need room.
- **Tone**: Ranges from hopeful pioneering to gritty survival.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The satisfaction of building — watching civilization take root.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Arrival or early settlement. Establish what they're working with.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Moderate.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act. Crisis threatens the colony; adaptation saves it.

## Comp Titles
1. **Red Mars** by Kim Stanley Robinson — Hard sci-fi Mars colonization, political factions.
2. **The Martian** by Andy Weir — Solo survival on Mars (adjacent to colonization).
3. **Planetfall** by Emma Newman — Mystery + colonization + mental health.
4. **Children of Time** by Adrian Tchaikovsky — Colonization from multiple species' perspectives.
5. **Semiosis** by Sue Burke — Multi-generational colonization, alien plant intelligence.

## Themes That Work
- **What we bring with us**: Colonists carry their cultures, biases, and conflicts.
- **Belonging to a new world**: When does a colony become a home?
- **Ethical colonization**: Can you settle a world without exploitation?
- **Adaptation**: Changing yourself to fit a world rather than changing the world to fit you.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Ignoring colonial history**: Real colonization involved exploitation and genocide. The genre must engage with this, not replicate it uncritically.
- **Earth nostalgia only**: If characters just want to recreate Earth, the new world becomes wallpaper.
- **Easy terraforming**: Making a planet habitable should take centuries and be fraught with failure.
