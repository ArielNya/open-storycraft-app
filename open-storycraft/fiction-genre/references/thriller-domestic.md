# Domestic Thriller

**Definition**: A thriller set within the intimate sphere of home, family, and relationships where the people closest to the protagonist become the source of danger.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **The Trusted Threat**: The antagonist is someone the protagonist loves, lives with, or depends on (spouse, neighbor, friend, family member).
- **Suburban or Domestic Facade**: An outwardly normal life concealing rot underneath.
- **Gaslit Reality**: The protagonist's perception of their own life is systematically distorted by someone they trust.
- **Escalating Entrapment**: As the protagonist discovers the truth, their options for escape narrow.

## Optional Tropes
- **The Perfect Couple**: A marriage or partnership that looks enviable from the outside but is toxic within.
- **The New Arrival**: A new neighbor, nanny, friend, or partner who disrupts the domestic equilibrium.
- **Missing Person**: A disappearance (child, spouse, neighbor) that triggers the unraveling of secrets.
- **The Secret Room / Hidden Life**: A physical or digital space that reveals a double life.
- **Unreliable Memory**: Alcohol, medication, or trauma creates gaps the protagonist cannot account for.
- **The Mommy Group / Social Circle**: A community that enforces conformity and punishes deviation.
- **Financial Control**: Money used as a tool of domination and entrapment.
- **The Child in Danger**: Stakes elevated by a vulnerable child caught between warring adults.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: The most dangerous place in the world is your own home, and the person you trust most is lying.
- **Resolution**: The protagonist escapes or overcomes the domestic threat, often through their own resourcefulness.
- **Pacing**: Slow-burn dread that accelerates into a sprint in the final act.
- **Tone**: Intimate, suffocating, punctuated by moments of normalcy that make the danger feel more real.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The moment the protagonist stops doubting themselves and fights back.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Something feels wrong but cannot be named. A detail out of place, a lie detected, a feeling of unease.
- **Chapter Length**: 1,500-3,000 words, short chapters to create compulsive page-turning.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Every chapter or nearly so.
- **Act Structure**: Three-act: suspicion, investigation and gaslighting, confrontation and escape.

## Comp Titles
1. **Gone Girl** by Gillian Flynn — The marriage thriller that launched the domestic thriller boom, with dueling perspectives and weaponized intimacy.
2. **The Woman in the Window** by A.J. Finn — An agoraphobic woman witnesses something across the street that no one believes she saw.
3. **Big Little Lies** by Liane Moriarty — Three mothers, a murder, and the secrets that perfect suburban lives conceal.
4. **The Couple Next Door** by Shari Lapena — A baby disappears while the parents are at a dinner party next door, and everyone is lying.
5. **Behind Closed Doors** by B.A. Paris — A seemingly perfect husband who is a meticulous, calculating captor.

## Themes That Work
- **The prison of normalcy**: Social expectations about marriage, motherhood, and respectability become the bars of a cage.
- **Who you marry, who you become**: Intimate relationships shape identity, and the wrong partner can erase you.
- **The invisibility of domestic abuse**: Coercive control happens in plain sight because no one wants to look closely at a happy family.
- **Self-trust as survival**: The protagonist must learn to believe their own perceptions against sustained denial from everyone around them.
- **Complicity of community**: Neighbors, friends, and family who look away because the truth is inconvenient.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Protagonist too passive for too long**: If the main character simply endures without taking action or investigating for 250 pages, readers lose patience. Give them agency, even small acts of resistance.
- **Villain too cartoonish**: The domestic antagonist must be charming and plausible enough that the reader understands why the protagonist stayed. Pure evil is less frightening than evil that wears a loving face.
- **Twist that undermines sympathy**: If the final twist reveals the protagonist was the real villain all along with no prior clues, readers feel manipulated rather than surprised.
