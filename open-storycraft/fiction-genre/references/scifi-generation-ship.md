# Generation Ship

**Definition**: An enclosed society traveling to a distant star over generations, where the ship IS the world and social/political/ecological systems must sustain themselves for centuries.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Enclosed World**: The ship is the entire universe for its inhabitants.
- **Generational Passage**: The journey takes longer than a human lifetime.
- **Social System**: A governance/cultural structure that must endure for centuries.
- **Resource Constraints**: Limited space, energy, food, air — scarcity drives conflict.

## Optional Tropes
- **Forgotten Origins**: Later generations don't know they're on a ship.
- **Class Stratification**: Different sections of the ship with different privileges.
- **Mutiny/Revolution**: The social order breaks down mid-journey.
- **Ecological Collapse**: The ship's life support systems failing.
- **Arrival Crisis**: What happens when they finally reach the destination.
- **Generational Drift**: Culture, language, or biology changing over centuries.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: A society in a bottle — all of humanity's problems compressed into a finite space.
- **Resolution**: A crisis is resolved, or the ship reaches its destination, or a fundamental truth is revealed.
- **Pacing**: Moderate. Character and social dynamics drive the story.
- **Tone**: Often claustrophobic, political, philosophical.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The revelation — what the ship really is, where they're really going, or what they've become.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Establish the ship as a world. Make the reader feel the walls.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Moderate.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act, often with a major reveal at the midpoint.

## Comp Titles
1. **Record of a Spaceborn Few** by Becky Chambers — Cozy generation ship, community-focused.
2. **Non-Stop** by Brian Aldiss — Classic: inhabitants don't know they're on a ship.
3. **Aurora** by Kim Stanley Robinson — Hard sci-fi generation ship, unflinching about the challenges.
4. **The Long Way to a Small, Angry Planet** by Becky Chambers — Ship-as-community (not strictly generation ship but adjacent).
5. **Ship of Fools** by Richard Paul Russo — Mystery aboard a generation ship.

## Themes That Work
- **Society as experiment**: What happens when you can't leave?
- **Legacy and purpose**: Why continue a journey you'll never complete?
- **Ecological limits**: The ship as metaphor for Earth's finite resources.
- **Identity and origins**: Who are you when your "world" was built by ancestors?

## Common Pitfalls
- **Ignoring the logistics**: Readers expect thought-out systems for food, air, governance, population control.
- **No sense of enclosure**: If the ship feels like a planet, you've lost the genre's core tension.
- **Static society**: Cultures change over centuries. A 10th-generation ship society should be unrecognizable to the founders.
