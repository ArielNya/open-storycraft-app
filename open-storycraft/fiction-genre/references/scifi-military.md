# Military Sci-Fi

**Definition**: Future warfare — soldiers, tactics, chain of command, alien enemies, and advanced weaponry in a science fiction setting.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Military Structure**: Ranks, units, chain of command, military culture.
- **Combat**: Battles are central, depicted with tactical detail.
- **Camaraderie**: Bonds between soldiers under fire.
- **Advanced Weaponry**: Sci-fi weapons, armor, ships, or tactics that go beyond today's military.

## Optional Tropes
- **Boot Camp/Training**: Transformation from civilian to soldier.
- **Alien Enemy**: An extraterrestrial threat requiring military response.
- **Power Armor/Mechs**: Technological force multipliers worn by soldiers.
- **Ship-to-Ship Combat**: Naval-style fleet engagements in space.
- **Veteran's Perspective**: War through the eyes of experienced soldiers, not officers.
- **Moral Ambiguity**: Questioning whether the war is justified.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: The experience of future combat — what it feels like to fight with tomorrow's weapons.
- **Resolution**: The battle or campaign concludes. Characters survive or sacrifice meaningfully.
- **Pacing**: Fast during combat, moderate during planning/downtime.
- **Tone**: Ranges from gung-ho adventure to somber realism.
- **Satisfaction Source**: Tactical cleverness, brotherhood under fire, and the visceral thrill of combat.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Start with action or imminent threat. No slow world-building introductions.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,500-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: High during operations.
- **Act Structure**: Mission-based arcs within larger campaign structure.

## Content Heat Map
| Dimension | Level (1-5) | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Violence  | 4-5 | Combat is the genre. Graphic detail expected. |
| Romance   | 1-2 | Usually minimal; comrades, not lovers |
| Language   | 3-4 | Soldiers talk like soldiers |
| Darkness  | 3-4 | War is hell; the genre acknowledges it |

## Comp Titles
1. **Starship Troopers** by Robert A. Heinlein — The template. Power armor, boot camp, alien war.
2. **Old Man's War** by John Scalzi — Accessible mil-sci-fi, elderly soldiers in young bodies.
3. **The Forever War** by Joe Haldeman — Anti-war mil-sci-fi, time dilation as alienation.
4. **Terms of Enlistment** by Marko Kloos — Modern mil-sci-fi, ground-level perspective.
5. **On Basilisk Station** by David Weber — Naval mil-sci-fi, ship combat, political intrigue.

## Themes That Work
- **Duty vs. morality**: Following orders that may be wrong.
- **Brotherhood**: The bonds forged in combat that civilians can't understand.
- **The cost of war**: What soldiers lose — physically, mentally, spiritually.
- **Technology vs. humanity**: Weapons evolve, but the human cost remains.

## Common Pitfalls
- **War without cost**: Glorifying combat without showing consequences.
- **Cardboard enemies**: Aliens that exist only to be shot. Give the enemy logic and motivation.
- **All tactics, no characters**: Detailed battle plans are meaningless if the reader doesn't care who lives or dies.
