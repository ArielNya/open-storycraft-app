# Dark Fantasy / Grimdark

**Definition**: Fantasy that subverts heroic tropes with morally gray characters, brutal consequences, and a world where virtue is not rewarded and power is earned through compromise.

## Mandatory Tropes

- **Morally Gray Protagonist**: No clear heroes. The protagonist has done terrible things, or will.
- **Brutal Consequences**: Actions have real, permanent costs. Characters die, lose limbs, betray each other.
- **Subverted Expectations**: Classic fantasy tropes (chosen one, noble quest, righteous victory) are undermined or inverted.
- **Harsh World**: The setting itself is unforgiving. Institutions are corrupt, nature is hostile, kindness is exploited.

## Optional Tropes

- **Unreliable Narrator**: The protagonist lies to the reader or themselves about their nature.
- **Political Machination**: Power games where everyone has an agenda and alliances shift.
- **War as Horror**: Combat depicted as chaotic, traumatic, and unglamorous.
- **Anti-Hero Redemption Arc**: A terrible person slowly finding a line they won't cross.
- **Magic with Terrible Cost**: Power that extracts suffering, sanity, or humanity.
- **Cycle of Violence**: Characters trapped in patterns they can see but can't escape.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: An unflinching look at what fantasy would actually feel like — no sanitized adventure.
- **Resolution**: Bittersweet at best. Victory costs more than defeat would have. The world is marginally better, or just differently broken.
- **Pacing**: Varies. Can be slow-burn character study or relentless action.
- **Tone**: Dark, cynical, occasionally bleakly funny.
- **Satisfaction Source**: Characters who feel real because they're flawed, and a world that respects the reader's intelligence enough not to simplify morality.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Often starts in media res — violence, consequence, or moral compromise on page one.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-5,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Moderate to high. Shocking twists are genre currency.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act, but Act 3 may not deliver traditional resolution.

## Comp Titles

1. **The First Law** trilogy by Joe Abercrombie — The modern grimdark benchmark. Every character is compromised.
2. **The Prince of Nothing** by R. Scott Bakker — Philosophical grimdark with unreliable protagonist.
3. **The Black Company** by Glen Cook — Military grimdark from the soldiers' perspective.
4. **Best Served Cold** by Joe Abercrombie — Revenge plot where revenge solves nothing.
5. **The Poppy War** by R.F. Kuang — Grimdark inspired by Chinese history; power as corruption.

## Themes That Work
- **Power corrupts absolutely**: No one wielding power stays clean.
- **The cost of survival**: What you become to stay alive.
- **Moral relativism**: There are no good sides, only sides.
- **Cycles of violence**: Revenge begets revenge. War creates the next war.
- **The banality of evil**: Cruelty committed by ordinary people for ordinary reasons.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Darkness without purpose**: Grimdark that's just edgy — piling on suffering without exploring what it means. Readers need a reason to endure the darkness.
- **No one to root for**: Even in grimdark, the reader needs someone they want to survive. Morally gray doesn't mean morally empty.
- **Trauma as aesthetic**: Using sexual violence, torture, or abuse as world-building decoration rather than meaningful narrative element. Readers notice and reject this.
