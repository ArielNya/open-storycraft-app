# Comedy — Genre Index

## Overview
Comedy fiction exists to make the reader laugh, smile, or feel delight. The core appeal is pleasure — the release of tension through humor, the joy of absurdity, the satisfaction of wit.

## Humor Mechanics in Prose
- **Timing through sentence structure**: Short sentences land punchlines. Long setups make the payoff sharper. The rhythm of prose IS comedic timing.
- **Comedic escalation**: Each beat must be funnier than the last within a sequence. Stop before the peak, never after.
- **The Rule of Threes**: Two items establish a pattern, the third breaks it. "He brought a sword, a shield, and a firm opinion about cheese."
- **Character-driven vs Situational**: Character humor comes from who the person IS. Situational humor comes from what happens TO them. Character humor ages better.
- **Deadpan**: The narrator or character doesn't acknowledge the humor. The contrast between tone and content creates the laugh.

## Subgenres

### Parody / Satire
Mocks genre conventions, institutions, or cultural phenomena. The humor has a target.

### Romantic Comedy
Humor drives the courtship. Witty banter, comic misunderstandings, warmth underneath the laughs.
**Detail file**: `comedy-romcom.md`

### Slice of Life Comedy
Everyday situations made funny. No high stakes — the humor is in the mundane observed sharply.
**Detail file**: `comedy-slice-of-life.md`

### Absurdist
Logic is abandoned. The world operates on dream logic, impossible premises, escalating nonsense.
**Detail file**: `comedy-absurdist.md`

### Dark Comedy
Humor derived from grim subject matter. Death, failure, cruelty made funny through perspective.
**Detail file**: `comedy-dark.md`

### Screwball
Fast-paced physical comedy and rapid-fire dialogue. Chaotic situations, mistaken identities.
**Detail file**: `comedy-screwball.md`

### Comedy of Manners
Humor from social conventions, class dynamics, and the gap between public behavior and private desire.
