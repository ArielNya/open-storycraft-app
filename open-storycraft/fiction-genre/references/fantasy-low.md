# Low Fantasy

**Definition**: Fantasy where magic is rare, subtle, or ambiguous, existing at the margins of an otherwise mundane or historically-grounded world.

## Mandatory Tropes

- **Magic at the Margins**: Magic is rare, unreliable, or might not be real at all.
- **Grounded World**: The setting feels realistic — historical or contemporary — with fantastical elements as exception, not rule.
- **Human-Scale Conflict**: Stakes are personal, political, or local, not "save the world."
- **Ambiguity**: The line between real and magical is often blurred.

## Optional Tropes

- **Historical Setting**: Medieval, Renaissance, or other historical period as base.
- **Unreliable Magic**: It works sometimes, or only for some people, or at a cost.
- **Superstition vs. Reality**: Characters debate whether the supernatural is real.
- **Gritty Realism**: Violence, politics, and social dynamics feel grounded.
- **Subtle Supernatural**: Ghosts, curses, or prophecies that might be coincidence.
- **Folk Magic**: Hedge witches, herbalists, minor enchantments.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: A world that feels real, where the magical is genuinely strange and unsettling.
- **Resolution**: The human conflict resolves. The magical elements may remain mysterious.
- **Pacing**: Character-driven. Can be slow but must be compelling.
- **Tone**: Grounded, often dark or melancholy. The wonder is in the contrast with the mundane.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The feeling that magic means something precisely because it's rare.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Ground the reader in the real world first. Magic enters gradually.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,500-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Low to moderate. Tension is sustained, not spiked.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act, character-driven.

## Comp Titles

1. **The Fifth Season** by N.K. Jemisin — Low magic feel despite systemic magic; grounded, brutal world.
2. **Tigana** by Guy Gavriel Kay — Historical-feeling fantasy, subtle magic, political conflict.
3. **Jonathan Strange & Mr Norrell** by Susanna Clarke — Magic in historical England, rare and strange.
4. **The Curse of Chalion** by Lois McMaster Bujold — Limited divine magic in a political setting.
5. **Piranesi** by Susanna Clarke — Magical setting, grounded narrator, ambiguous reality.

## Themes That Work
- **The numinous in the ordinary**: What makes the everyday suddenly strange.
- **Faith and doubt**: Believing in something you can't prove.
- **Power without magic**: Political, social, and personal power in a world where magic can't save you.
- **The cost of the extraordinary**: When magic touches your life, it changes you, and not always for the better.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Scope creep**: Starting low-magic and escalating to fireballs by Act 3. If you promised subtle, deliver subtle.
- **Boring without magic**: If the non-magical story isn't interesting, adding occasional magic won't save it. The human story must stand alone.
- **Ambiguity as laziness**: "Maybe it's magic, maybe not" only works if both interpretations are satisfying.
