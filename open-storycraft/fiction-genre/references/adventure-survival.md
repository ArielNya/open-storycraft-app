# Survival Adventure

**Definition**: Fiction where the protagonist is stranded, trapped, or isolated in a hostile environment and must use ingenuity, endurance, and limited resources to stay alive.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Hostile Environment**: The setting itself is the antagonist. Desert, ocean, arctic, jungle, space, or post-disaster landscape that actively tries to kill the protagonist.
- **Resource Scarcity**: Food, water, shelter, tools, or medical supplies are limited. Every resource decision carries weight.
- **Problem-Solving as Plot**: The narrative is driven by a series of practical challenges. How to make fire. How to find water. How to signal for rescue.
- **Physical Deterioration**: The protagonist's body is affected by their ordeal. Injuries accumulate, strength fades, and the clock of human endurance is always ticking.
- **Isolation or Small Group Dynamics**: The protagonist is alone or with a small group, and the psychological pressure of isolation compounds the physical danger.

## Optional Tropes
- **The Inciting Disaster**: A crash, shipwreck, storm, or catastrophe that strands the protagonist.
- **Flashbacks to Normal Life**: Memories of the world before, providing character depth and emotional stakes for survival.
- **The Discovery**: Finding something unexpected in the hostile environment, whether another survivor, ruins, resources, or a new threat.
- **Improvised Technology**: Building tools, shelters, or systems from available materials, satisfying the reader's problem-solving instinct.
- **Group Fracture**: If multiple survivors, disagreements about leadership, strategy, or morality that threaten group cohesion.
- **The Rescue Attempt**: A failed or complicated rescue that extends the ordeal.
- **Wildlife as Threat**: Animals, predators, or hostile fauna that add unpredictable danger.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: You will experience what it takes to survive the unsurvivable, and you will learn something about human endurance.
- **Resolution**: Survival (usually). The protagonist escapes, is rescued, or adapts permanently. The ordeal changes them.
- **Pacing**: Rhythmic. Intense crisis scenes alternate with quieter problem-solving and reflection.
- **Tone**: Tense and grounded. Realism matters. The reader wants to believe this could really happen.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The ingenuity of solutions, the accumulation of small victories, and the emotional payoff of endurance rewarded.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: The disaster or stranding should happen quickly, within the first chapter or two. Some stories open mid-crisis.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-5,000 words. Long enough to explore a problem and its solution within a single chapter.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Most chapters. New threats, failed solutions, or deteriorating conditions.
- **Act Structure**: Three-act. Act 1: stranding and immediate crisis. Act 2: adaptation, escalating challenges, and psychological strain. Act 3: the final crisis and escape or resolution.

## Comp Titles
1. **The Martian** by Andy Weir — An astronaut stranded on Mars survives through engineering, humor, and stubborn problem-solving.
2. **Hatchet** by Gary Paulsen — A boy alone in the Canadian wilderness with only a hatchet, the classic survival narrative distilled to its essence.
3. **Life of Pi** by Yann Martel — Survival at sea with a Bengal tiger, blending practical survival with philosophical and spiritual dimensions.
4. **Into Thin Air** by Jon Krakauer — A firsthand account of an Everest disaster that reads with the tension and structure of a survival thriller.
5. **The Road** by Cormac McCarthy — Post-apocalyptic survival stripped to its barest elements: a father, a son, and the will to keep going.

## Themes That Work
- **What You Are Made Of**: Survival strips away everything nonessential. Who a person is when comfort, society, and routine are removed reveals their core.
- **Humanity vs. Nature**: Not as antagonism but as relationship. The environment does not hate the protagonist; it is indifferent, and that indifference is more frightening than malice.
- **The Will to Live**: What keeps someone fighting when giving up would be easier? The answer is always deeply personal and reveals character.
- **Ingenuity as Heroism**: In survival fiction, intelligence and adaptability replace physical strength as the heroic virtues.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Convenient Resources**: If the protagonist always finds exactly what they need right when they need it, the survival tension evaporates. Scarcity must be real and persistent.
- **Competence Without Cost**: Solving every problem easily makes the environment feel unthreatening. The protagonist must fail, get hurt, and lose things along the way.
- **Monotonous Problem-Solving**: "Found water, found food, built shelter" repeated without escalation or complication becomes boring. Each challenge must introduce something new.
