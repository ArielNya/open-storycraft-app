# Cozy Mystery

**Definition**: A mystery featuring an amateur sleuth in a small, close-knit community where the crime is solved without graphic violence, explicit content, or profanity, and the setting itself is as comforting as the resolution.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Amateur Sleuth**: The protagonist is not a professional investigator but stumbles into the case through community ties or personal connection.
- **Small Community Setting**: A defined, intimate world (village, neighborhood, small town) where everyone knows everyone.
- **No Graphic Violence**: Death happens off-page or is described minimally; the focus is on the puzzle, not the gore.
- **Warm Supporting Cast**: A roster of quirky, recurring characters who populate the community and provide suspects, alibis, and comic relief.
- **Justice Served**: The culprit is always caught, and the community's sense of safety is restored.

## Optional Tropes
- **Themed Occupation**: The sleuth's job provides expertise and access (baker, librarian, florist, bookshop owner).
- **Pets and Animals**: A cat, dog, or other animal companion who may even "help" solve the case.
- **Recipes or Crafts**: Interludes featuring recipes, craft instructions, or hobby details that reinforce the cozy atmosphere.
- **Romantic Subplot**: A slow-burn romance, often with a local love interest who may be in law enforcement.
- **Bumbling Local Police**: Authorities who are well-meaning but need the amateur's help.
- **Seasonal or Holiday Setting**: The mystery coincides with a festival, holiday, or seasonal event.
- **Series Continuity**: Each book is standalone but characters and relationships evolve across volumes.
- **The Nosy Best Friend**: A sidekick who pushes the sleuth to investigate and provides moral support.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: A safe, enjoyable puzzle in a world the reader wants to inhabit.
- **Resolution**: The mystery is fully solved, the community is safe again, and personal relationships have advanced.
- **Pacing**: Moderate; the investigation weaves through daily life, social interactions, and community events.
- **Tone**: Light, warm, and humorous with genuine stakes but no dread.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The combination of a fair puzzle solved and time spent in a world that feels like a warm blanket.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: The body is discovered or the crime occurs within the first three chapters, often after establishing the cozy setting.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,000-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Occasional; chapters more often end on a social scene or a new clue than a shock.
- **Act Structure**: Three-act with a gentle midpoint shift when the sleuth commits to investigating seriously.

## Content Heat Map
| Dimension | Level (1-5) | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Violence  | 1 | Death occurs but is never depicted graphically; blood and brutality are absent. |
| Romance   | 2 | Sweet, closed-door romance; flirtation and emotional connection, nothing explicit. |
| Language   | 1 | Clean language throughout; the strongest words are mild exclamations. |
| Darkness  | 1 | The world is fundamentally safe and good; darkness is temporary and always resolved. |

## Comp Titles
1. **The Sweetness at the Bottom of the Pie** by Alan Bradley — A young amateur sleuth in a charming English village, combining wit with a genuine puzzle.
2. **Arsenic and Old Lace** by Craig Rice — A classic cozy tone with eccentric characters and a murder that never feels threatening.
3. **Murder Is Binding** by Lorna Barrett — A bookshop-themed cozy that exemplifies the occupation-driven amateur sleuth formula.
4. **Pies and Prejudice** by Ellery Adams — A food-themed cozy that balances community warmth with a well-constructed mystery.
5. **The Cat Who Could Read Backwards** by Lilian Jackson Braun — The defining pet-companion cozy, where the cat is as important as the clues.

## Themes That Work
- **Community bonds**: The mystery threatens the social fabric, and solving it reaffirms that people look out for each other.
- **Ordinary heroism**: You do not need a badge or a gun to stand up for what is right.
- **Belonging**: The sleuth's role in the investigation deepens their place in the community.
- **Appearances deceive**: Even in the friendliest village, people carry secrets worth killing for.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Stakes too low**: If the reader never believes the crime matters, the investigation feels like busywork; the victim needs to be someone the community (and reader) cares about.
- **Tone mismatch**: Injecting genuine menace, graphic content, or cynicism breaks the cozy contract and alienates the core readership.
- **Sleuth has no reason to investigate**: The amateur needs a personal, credible motivation to get involved; curiosity alone wears thin across a series.
