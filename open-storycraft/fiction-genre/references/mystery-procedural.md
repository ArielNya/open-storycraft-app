# Police Procedural

**Definition**: A mystery that follows law enforcement professionals solving crimes through realistic investigative methods, forensic science, institutional protocols, and teamwork rather than individual brilliance alone.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Professional Investigators**: The protagonists are police officers, detectives, or federal agents working within an institutional framework.
- **Realistic Methods**: The investigation proceeds through evidence collection, interviews, forensics, warrants, record systems, and legwork that reflects actual police work.
- **Institutional Context**: The bureaucracy, politics, chain of command, and resource constraints of law enforcement shape how the case unfolds.
- **Teamwork**: No single detective solves the case alone; partners, forensic techs, medical examiners, and prosecutors all contribute.
- **Evidence-Driven Resolution**: The case is built on physical evidence and testimony, not lucky guesses or dramatic confrontations.

## Optional Tropes
- **Multiple Cases**: The detective juggles more than one investigation simultaneously, mirroring real workloads.
- **Personal Life Under Strain**: The demands of the job erode the detective's relationships, health, or mental stability.
- **Jurisdictional Conflict**: Turf wars between agencies or departments complicate the investigation.
- **The Clock**: A serial offender, missing person, or political pressure creates urgency.
- **Flawed System**: The legal process sometimes lets the guilty walk or punishes the wrong person.
- **Forensic Detail**: Autopsy reports, ballistics, DNA analysis, and digital forensics are described with technical accuracy.
- **Cold Case Revival**: An old unsolved case resurfaces with new evidence or new eyes.
- **Media Pressure**: Press coverage influences the investigation or creates public pressure on the department.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: An authentic window into how real investigations work, with the satisfaction of watching professionals do their jobs well.
- **Resolution**: The case is solved through accumulated evidence; prosecution may or may not succeed, but the truth is established.
- **Pacing**: Methodical but never slow; each interview, lab result, or breakthrough propels the investigation forward.
- **Tone**: Professional and grounded, ranging from clinical detachment to emotional weight depending on the case.
- **Satisfaction Source**: Watching competent people piece together the truth from fragmentary evidence, one step at a time.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: The crime occurs in the first chapter, often shown from the victim's or perpetrator's perspective before the detectives arrive.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-5,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Every chapter ends with a new lead, a dead end that redirects, or a complication that raises stakes.
- **Act Structure**: Four-act (discovery/investigation/false lead or setback/breakthrough and resolution).

## Comp Titles
1. **The Black Echo** by Michael Connelly — Harry Bosch's debut establishes the gold standard for procedural fiction: relentless detective work within the LAPD's political machinery.
2. **Gorky Park** by Martin Cruz Smith — A Moscow homicide detective uses procedure and persistence against a system designed to obstruct him.
3. **Mystic River** by Dennis Lehane — A procedural investigation intertwined with the personal histories of three men connected to the crime.
4. **Still Life** by Louise Penny — Chief Inspector Gamache brings methodical investigation to a small Quebec village, blending procedural rigor with character depth.
5. **The Cuckoo's Calling** by Robert Galbraith — Cormoran Strike's private investigations follow procedural logic: legwork, interviews, and evidence over inspiration.

## Themes That Work
- **The system vs. justice**: Procedure exists to protect rights, but sometimes it protects the guilty too.
- **Competence under pressure**: Skilled professionals doing difficult work well is inherently compelling.
- **The toll of the job**: Constant exposure to the worst of human behavior changes a person.
- **Truth is earned, not given**: Every fact must be dug out, verified, and defended.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Inaccurate procedure**: Readers of this subgenre know how investigations work; getting forensic timelines, chain of custody, or legal process wrong breaks trust immediately.
- **Lone wolf detective**: If one person does everything and the institution is just background, it is a PI novel wearing a badge, not a procedural.
- **Pacing death by detail**: Exhaustive procedural accuracy that does not advance the plot or build tension turns the novel into a textbook.
