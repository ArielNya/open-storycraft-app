# Heist

**Definition**: Fiction structured around assembling a team, making a plan, and executing a theft or operation where the pleasure lies in watching the plan unfold, go wrong, and get salvaged through improvisation.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **The Crew Assembly**: A team of specialists is recruited, each with a distinct skill set and personality. The assembly itself is a satisfying sequence.
- **The Plan**: The operation is laid out in detail, giving the reader the pleasure of understanding the mechanics before watching them in action.
- **The Complication**: Something goes wrong during execution. The plan never survives first contact, and the team must adapt.
- **The Target**: Something specific must be stolen, infiltrated, or achieved. A vault, a person, a piece of information, a location.
- **The Mark or Antagonist**: A powerful figure or institution that the crew is working against, usually with superior resources.
- **The Reveal**: Information withheld from the reader (or other characters) is revealed, recontextualizing earlier events. The crew was always one step ahead.

## Optional Tropes
- **The Mastermind**: One character who sees the whole board and orchestrates everything.
- **Internal Betrayal**: A crew member with a hidden agenda who threatens the operation from within.
- **The Dry Run**: A rehearsal or smaller job that tests the team's dynamics before the main event.
- **Competing Crews**: Another team with the same target, adding time pressure and rivalry.
- **The Inside Person**: Someone already embedded in the target who provides access or information.
- **The Double Cross**: The client, mark, or a crew member changes the terms mid-operation.
- **Gadgets and Techniques**: Specialized tools, technology, or methods that feel clever and specific.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: You will watch brilliant people execute an impossible plan and enjoy every twist.
- **Resolution**: The heist succeeds, though often not in the way originally planned. The crew gets away with it, or most of them do.
- **Pacing**: Builds steadily. Slow burn during planning, accelerating through execution, explosive during complications.
- **Tone**: Cool and confident during planning, tense during execution, triumphant at the resolution.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The intellectual pleasure of the plan combined with the thrill of watching it adapt under pressure. The reveal that makes you want to reread.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Establish the protagonist and hint at the job within the first chapter. The crew assembly can serve as Act 1.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-5,000 words. Enough for both planning detail and execution tension.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Every chapter during the execution phase. Planning chapters can end on character revelations or complications.
- **Act Structure**: Four-act works well. Act 1: the setup and crew. Act 2: planning and preparation. Act 3: execution and complications. Act 4: the resolution and reveal.

## Comp Titles
1. **The Lies of Locke Lamora** by Scott Lynch — Fantasy heist fiction with an elaborate con, a crew of charming thieves, and devastating complications.
2. **Six of Crows** by Leigh Bardugo — A crew of criminal specialists attempts an impossible heist in a fantasy world, with tight character work and elaborate planning.
3. **Artemis** by Andy Weir — A heist on the Moon combining technical problem-solving with a resourceful protagonist working against the clock.
4. **Mistborn: The Final Empire** by Brandon Sanderson — A fantasy revolution structured as a heist, with a crew of specialists using magic as their toolkit.
5. **The Great Train Robbery** by Michael Crichton — A Victorian-era heist based on real events, meticulously planned and executed with period-authentic ingenuity.

## Themes That Work
- **Competence as Entertainment**: Watching experts do expert things is inherently satisfying. The heist genre is built on the pleasure of mastery.
- **Trust and Betrayal**: A crew is only as strong as the trust between its members. The tension between needing to rely on others and the risk of betrayal is the emotional core.
- **The System Deserves It**: The best heist fiction makes the reader want the thieves to succeed because the target represents something unjust, hoarded, or corrupt.
- **Plans vs. Reality**: The gap between how things should go and how they actually go is both the source of tension and a metaphor for how life works.

## Common Pitfalls
- **The Crew Blurs Together**: If the specialists are not distinct in personality, voice, and function, the ensemble becomes a mass. Each crew member must be individually memorable.
- **The Plan Is Too Perfect**: If nothing goes wrong, or the solution to every problem was already anticipated, there is no tension. The reader needs genuine uncertainty about whether the crew can adapt.
- **Withholding Too Much**: The reveal is satisfying only if the reader has enough information to appreciate the cleverness. If everything was hidden, the twist feels like a cheat rather than a revelation.
