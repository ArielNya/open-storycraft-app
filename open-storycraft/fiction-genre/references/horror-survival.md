# Survival Horror

**Definition**: Horror built around characters trapped in a dangerous environment with limited resources, where the primary tension comes from the struggle to stay alive against an overwhelming threat.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Isolation or Entrapment**: Characters are cut off from help, whether by geography, circumstance, or the antagonist's design.
- **Resource Scarcity**: Supplies, weapons, communication, or escape routes are limited and dwindling.
- **Escalating Threat**: The danger grows worse over time, forcing harder choices and higher stakes.
- **Attrition**: The group shrinks. Characters are injured, killed, or lost as the story progresses.
- **Fight-or-Flight Decisions**: Characters must constantly choose between confrontation and evasion, with neither option safe.

## Optional Tropes
- **Group Dynamics Under Stress**: Interpersonal conflict erupts as fear and desperation erode trust and cooperation.
- **The Weak Link**: One character whose panic, injury, or selfishness endangers the group.
- **Environmental Hazards**: The setting itself is hostile beyond the primary threat (weather, terrain, structural collapse).
- **Improvised Solutions**: Characters must use their environment creatively to survive, not rely on conventional weapons or tools.
- **Countdown or Ticking Clock**: A hard deadline (oxygen running out, rescue window closing, dam breaking) that compresses the timeline.
- **Monster Ecology**: The threat has rules, patterns, or weaknesses that can be learned and exploited.
- **False Safety**: Moments of apparent reprieve that are shattered by the next attack or discovery.
- **Sacrifice Play**: A character deliberately gives their life so others can escape or survive.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: Relentless tension and the primal thrill of characters fighting to survive against terrible odds.
- **Resolution**: A small number of survivors escape or neutralize the threat, but at devastating cost. Total party kills are valid if earned.
- **Pacing**: Fast and relentless after the initial setup, with brief recovery beats that never feel safe.
- **Tone**: Desperate, claustrophobic, visceral.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The intensity of the survival experience and the emotional weight of who lives and who dies.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Moderate. Establish the characters and setting quickly, then trigger the inciting threat within the first 10-15% of the book.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,000-4,000 words. Short chapters maintain urgency and momentum.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Most chapters. The threat is persistent, and lulls are brief.
- **Act Structure**: Siege structure. Act 1: arrival and initial threat. Act 2: escalating attacks, dwindling resources, internal fractures. Act 3: final stand or escape attempt.

## Content Heat Map
| Dimension | Level (1-5) | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Violence  | 4 | Death and injury are frequent and described with visceral clarity |
| Romance   | 1 | Survival leaves little room; at most a protective bond or brief comfort |
| Language   | 3 | Stress-driven profanity feels natural and earned |
| Darkness  | 4 | High body count, moral compromises, and the constant possibility of total failure |

## Comp Titles
1. **The Troop** by Nick Cutter — Boy scouts on an island facing a biological threat with dwindling numbers and escalating body horror.
2. **The Hunger** by Alma Katsu — The Donner Party reimagined with a supernatural predator stalking the wagon train.
3. **Fantasticland** by Mike Bockoven — Theme park employees trapped after a hurricane descend into tribal violence.
4. **Devolution** by Max Brooks — A remote eco-community under siege by Bigfoot, told through found documents.
5. **The Ruins** by Scott Smith — Tourists trapped on an archaeological site by a threat they cannot outrun.

## Themes That Work
- **Civilization is a Thin Veneer**: How quickly social norms collapse when survival is at stake.
- **The Cost of Survival**: What people are willing to sacrifice (morals, relationships, others) to stay alive.
- **Who You Really Are**: Extreme pressure reveals character in ways comfort never does.
- **Nature's Indifference**: The environment does not care about human survival, and neither does the threat.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Stupid decisions for plot convenience**: Characters must make bad choices under pressure, not because the author needs them to walk into danger. Every decision must feel rational from the character's limited perspective in the moment.
- **Flat attrition without stakes**: Killing characters the reader never connected with produces no emotional impact. Each death must cost something the reader feels.
- **Endless middle with no escalation**: If chapter 15 feels the same as chapter 5, the survival tension has flatlined. Each setback must be worse than the last, or force a harder choice.
