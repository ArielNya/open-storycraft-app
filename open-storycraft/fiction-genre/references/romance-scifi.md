# Sci-Fi Romance

**Definition**: Romance set in a science fiction world (space, future Earth, alien civilizations, advanced technology) where the speculative setting and the love story are co-equal narrative priorities.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **HEA/HFN**: The couple must achieve a happily ever after or happy for now ending.
- **Speculative Setting as Obstacle**: The sci-fi elements (interstellar distance, alien biology, AI consciousness, political systems) must create romantic obstacles unique to the setting.
- **Dual Plot Engine**: The sci-fi storyline (mission, war, discovery, survival) and the romance must each have their own arc and climax.
- **Technology or Science Shapes Intimacy**: How people connect, communicate, or are kept apart must reflect the speculative world.

## Optional Tropes
- **Alien/Human Pairing**: Cross-species romance with biological and cultural barriers.
- **Stranded Together**: Crash-landed, quarantined, or marooned in space with only each other.
- **Captain and Crew**: Power dynamics aboard a ship or station where fraternization is complicated.
- **Mail-Order Bride in Space**: Arranged matches across planets or systems, updated for a sci-fi context.
- **AI/Cyborg Romance**: One partner's humanity or consciousness is in question.
- **Last Survivors**: Post-apocalyptic or colony-founding scenarios where the couple represents hope.
- **Rebel and Authority**: One partner serves the system the other is fighting.
- **Time Dilation**: Relativity or cryo-sleep creating age gaps and separation.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: Love that survives across impossible distances, alien differences, or technological upheaval.
- **Resolution**: The couple finds a way to be together despite the universe's best efforts to keep them apart. The sci-fi conflict resolves in a way that makes the relationship sustainable.
- **Pacing**: Moderate to fast. Sci-fi plots provide action and discovery beats between romantic development.
- **Tone**: Adventurous, wonder-filled, sometimes gritty. Ranges from space opera warmth to hard sci-fi intensity.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The feeling that love is worth crossing galaxies for, and that human connection endures even when everything else changes.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Fast. Establish the world's rules and the charged dynamic between leads within the first two chapters.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-5,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Most chapters. Sci-fi romance benefits from alternating danger/discovery beats with emotional intimacy beats.
- **Act Structure**: Three-act. Often series-length, with each book featuring a different couple in the same universe or deepening one relationship across escalating sci-fi stakes. Typical length 80k-120k words.

## Comp Titles
1. **The Kiss Quotient** by Helen Hoang — Contemporary but influential in SFR circles for its neurodivergent representation and structured approach to intimacy.
2. **Polaris Rising** by Jessie Mihalik — Space opera romance with a princess and a wanted man on the run through hostile space.
3. **Strange Love** by Ann Aguirre — Alien abduction romance played for genuine emotion, with creative world-building around non-human biology.
4. **The Host** by Stephenie Meyer — Alien consciousness sharing a human body, exploring identity and love across species boundaries.
5. **Ice Planet Barbarians** by Ruby Dixon — Alien romance on a frozen world that launched a massive subgenre of alien mate stories.

## Themes That Work
- **Connection Across Difference**: Finding common ground with someone whose biology, culture, or consciousness is fundamentally alien.
- **What Makes Us Human**: Romance as the lens for exploring consciousness, identity, and the boundaries of personhood.
- **Home Is a Person**: When you are light-years from Earth, home becomes whoever you choose.
- **Technology and Intimacy**: How connection changes (or doesn't) when the medium transforms.
- **Colonialism and Power**: Love across political lines in settings shaped by conquest or exploitation.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Rubber Forehead Aliens**: Alien love interests who are just attractive humans with a cosmetic difference. If the species is alien, let the alienness matter to the romance.
- **World-Building Lectures**: Sci-fi romance readers want both plot engines running. Pages of technical exposition without romantic or emotional content lose the audience.
- **Romance as Afterthought**: If the sci-fi plot could stand alone and the romance is bolted on, this is sci-fi with a subplot, not sci-fi romance. The relationship must be structurally essential.
