# Mystery — Genre Index

## Overview
Mystery fiction centers a puzzle: something is unknown, and the story is the process of uncovering the truth. The core appeal is intellectual engagement — the reader wants to solve it alongside (or ahead of) the protagonist.

## Mystery Mechanics
- **Fair Play**: The reader must have access to the same clues as the detective. No pulling solutions from nowhere. (See Knox's Decalogue and Van Dine's rules.)
- **Clue Placement**: Hide clues in plain sight. Bury them among irrelevant details. Let the reader see without understanding.
- **Red Herrings**: False leads that are plausible but wrong. Must be debunked honestly, not hand-waved.
- **The Reveal**: The moment the solution is presented. It must recontextualize earlier events so the reader says "of course."

## Subgenres

### Whodunit
Classic mystery. A crime occurs, suspects are gathered, clues are followed, the culprit is revealed.
**Detail file**: `mystery-whodunit.md`

### Cozy Mystery
Gentle mystery. Amateur sleuth, small community, no graphic violence, warm tone.
**Detail file**: `mystery-cozy.md`

### Hardboiled / Noir
Cynical detective, corrupt world, moral ambiguity. The detective is as damaged as the case.
**Detail file**: `mystery-noir.md`

### Police Procedural
The investigation follows real police methods. Forensics, interviews, bureaucracy, teamwork.
**Detail file**: `mystery-procedural.md`

### Amateur Sleuth
A non-professional investigates. Baker, teacher, librarian, journalist. Often series-driven.
**Detail file**: `mystery-amateur.md`

### Historical Mystery
Mystery set in a specific historical period. The era's constraints shape the investigation.
**Detail file**: `mystery-historical.md`

### Locked Room
An "impossible crime" — the puzzle is not just whodunit but howdunit. Closed environments.
**Detail file**: `mystery-locked-room.md`

### Inverted Mystery
The reader knows the killer from the start. The suspense is watching the detective figure it out.
**Detail file**: `mystery-inverted.md`
