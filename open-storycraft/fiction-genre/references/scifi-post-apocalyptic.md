# Post-Apocalyptic

**Definition**: Civilization has collapsed. The story follows survivors navigating what remains — scavenging, rebuilding, or simply enduring in the aftermath.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Collapsed Civilization**: The old world is gone — infrastructure, government, supply chains.
- **Survival**: Finding food, shelter, safety in a world no longer designed for human comfort.
- **Remnants**: Artifacts of the old world — ruins, technology, memories — as setting elements.
- **New Social Order**: In the absence of old structures, new hierarchies, communities, or tyrannies emerge.

## Optional Tropes
- **The Journey**: Travel through the ruined landscape to reach somewhere better.
- **Warlords/Factions**: Groups competing for territory and resources.
- **The Cause**: What destroyed civilization (plague, nuclear war, climate, AI, asteroid).
- **Hope of Rebuilding**: Characters trying to restart civilization, not just survive.
- **Mutation/Adaptation**: Humans or nature changed by the catastrophe.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: What would you do if everything fell apart?
- **Resolution**: The protagonist reaches safety, builds something, or makes peace with the new world.
- **Pacing**: Moderate. Tension from environment and scarcity, not just combat.
- **Tone**: Bleak but with threads of hope. Pure nihilism is exhausting.
- **Satisfaction Source**: Resilience — humans enduring and finding meaning despite everything.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Show the ruined world. Make the reader feel the absence of normal.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,500-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Moderate to high.
- **Act Structure**: Journey-based or siege-based, often 3-act.

## Content Heat Map
| Dimension | Level (1-5) | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Violence  | 3-4 | Survival violence, faction conflict |
| Romance   | 2 | Present but not the focus |
| Language   | 3 | Gritty, realistic |
| Darkness  | 4 | The setting is inherently dark |

## Comp Titles
1. **The Road** by Cormac McCarthy — Father and son, pure survival, literary post-apocalyptic.
2. **Station Eleven** by June St. John Mandel — Cultural preservation after pandemic collapse.
3. **The Stand** by Stephen King — Plague + rebuilding + good vs evil.
4. **A Canticle for Leibowitz** by Walter M. Miller Jr. — Centuries-long rebuild after nuclear war.
5. **The Dog Stars** by Peter Heller — Literary post-apocalyptic, quiet survival.

## Themes That Work
- **What makes us human**: When survival strips everything away, what remains?
- **Community vs. isolation**: Do you trust others, or go it alone?
- **Memory and loss**: Mourning a world that no longer exists.
- **Rebuilding vs. surviving**: Is it enough to endure, or must you build something new?

## Common Pitfalls
- **Misery without purpose**: Suffering must serve the story. Relentless bleakness drives readers away.
- **Convenient resources**: Characters who always find exactly what they need. Scarcity should be felt.
- **Ignoring the boring parts**: Real survival is mostly tedious. Find the drama in the mundane.
