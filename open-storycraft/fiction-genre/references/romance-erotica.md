# Erotica

**Definition**: The sexual relationship is the plot, not an event inside it. The
story is structured around desire, its obstacles, and its consequences, and the
intimate scenes carry the character development rather than pausing it.

The line against Romance: romance owes the reader an emotionally satisfying,
optimistic ending. Erotica owes the reader transformation. A character can end
alone, changed, and the promise is kept. The line against high-heat Romance
(`romance-dark.md`, `romance-contemporary.md` at Scorching) is structural: there,
the sex is a scene in a love story; here, removing the sex removes the plot.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Desire as Engine**: What the protagonist wants sexually is what drives every
  decision. Remove it and the story stops.
- **Scenes That Advance**: Each intimate scene changes the relationship, the
  power balance, or the protagonist's understanding of themselves. A scene that
  changes nothing is cut.
- **Escalating Stakes**: What is risked rises. Reputation, autonomy, a marriage, a
  job, self-image.
- **Explicit On Page**: Door Wide Open by default. See `heat-door-scale.md`.
- **Consent Legible**: Established on the page, in the characters' behavior,
  whatever the fantasy being written. Ambiguity is a craft failure here, not an
  edge.

## Optional Tropes
- **Discovery Arc**: The protagonist learns something about their own wanting
  they did not know at the start.
- **Power Exchange**: Control as the explicit subject rather than the subtext.
- **Forbidden Context**: Social, professional, or situational cost attached to the
  relationship.
- **Multiple Partners**: Structured as a progression rather than a rotation.
- **Voyeur or Confidant**: A third character whose knowing changes the dynamic.
- **Return to the Ordinary**: The closing beat placing the changed protagonist
  back in normal life.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: Sustained, explicit intimacy with characters worth following
  between the scenes.
- **Resolution**: The protagonist is transformed by what they wanted. A couple is
  optional. Change is not.
- **Pacing**: The first intimate scene lands early, inside the first 15 percent.
  Delay reads as a bait and switch.
- **Tone**: Serious about desire. Humor is permitted; embarrassment about the
  subject is not.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The scenes themselves, plus the sense that they meant
  something.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Establish the want and the obstacle in the first scene.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,000-4,000 words. Chapters often map one to one onto
  encounters.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Moderate. Anticipation is the tension, so chapters
  more often end on a decision than a threat.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act. Want → escalation and cost → transformation.

## At Short Length
- **One-shot**: The natural home of the form. One encounter, one turn, one
  changed person. 3,000-7,500 words.
- **Novelette (10-12 ch.)**: Three or four encounters, each raising what is
  risked. The reversal at chapter 8 is usually the protagonist discovering the
  want was standing in for something else.
