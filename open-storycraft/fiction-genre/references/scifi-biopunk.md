# Biopunk

**Definition**: Science fiction centered on biological technology — genetic engineering, organic machines, body modification, and the commodification of life itself.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Biological Technology**: Genetics, bioengineering, or organic tech as central science.
- **Body as Commodity**: Biological modification available to those who can afford it.
- **Ethical Boundaries Crossed**: Science goes further than it should.
- **Corporate or State Control**: Institutions controlling biological technology for profit or power.

## Optional Tropes
- **Designer Organisms**: Custom-built creatures, plants, or ecosystems.
- **Genetic Caste System**: Society stratified by genetic modification.
- **Plague/Pandemic**: Engineered diseases as weapons or accidents.
- **Body Horror Elements**: Modification gone wrong.
- **Biohacker Underground**: DIY genetic modification outside institutional control.
- **Post-Human Evolution**: Humanity splitting into modified and unmodified branches.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: The visceral thrill and horror of biology pushed past natural limits.
- **Resolution**: The biological crisis is contained or its consequences are faced.
- **Pacing**: Moderate to fast. Thriller-adjacent.
- **Tone**: Unsettling, thought-provoking, often dark.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The "what if" carried to its logical, disturbing conclusion.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Show the bio-modified world as normal, then introduce the crack.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,500-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Moderate to high.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act, often mystery or thriller structure.

## Comp Titles
1. **The Windup Girl** by Paolo Bacigalupi — Bioengineered future, food politics, modified humans.
2. **Oryx and Crake** by Margaret Atwood — Genetic engineering, corporate dystopia, extinction.
3. **Ribofunk** by Paul Di Filippo — Short stories coining the biopunk genre.
4. **The Island of Doctor Moreau** by H.G. Wells — The proto-biopunk: what happens when you redesign life.
5. **Annihilation** by Jeff VanderMeer — Biological transformation in a mysterious zone.

## Themes That Work
- **Playing god**: The hubris of redesigning life.
- **Bodily autonomy**: Who owns your genes? Your body?
- **Natural vs. artificial**: Is there a meaningful distinction when biology is engineered?
- **Evolution by choice**: What happens when humans direct their own evolution?

## Common Pitfalls
- **Just cyberpunk with biology**: Biopunk needs its own aesthetic and concerns, not just swapping circuits for cells.
- **Science too vague**: The biology should feel plausible, not magic with test tubes.
- **Body horror as the only note**: Grotesque biology is effective, but the story needs more than disgust.
