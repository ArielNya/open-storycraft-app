# Adult Light Novel

**Definition**: Anime and manga sensibility rendered in prose. An archetype-forward
cast, escalating intimacy as a structural spine, and an intimate register that
borrows manga's sound effects. Not a setting and not a genre in the usual sense:
it is a mode that sits on a fantasy, isekai, contemporary, or science fiction
spine.

Distinct from Western erotica (see `romance-erotica.md`) in cast structure,
comedic tolerance, and voice. Distinct from romance in that no Happily Ever After
is owed. Distinct from *ecchi*, which stops at suggestion, and from *eroge*, which
is a game format rather than a prose one.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Archetype-Forward Cast**: Characters read as recognizable types on sight (the
  blunt childhood friend, the composed senior, the abrasive one who softens). The
  archetype is the entry point, not the endpoint.
- **Escalation as Structure**: Intimacy advances by discrete steps and does not
  retreat. Each step is an event the story treats as significant.
- **First-Person Immediate**: Present-tense-feeling interiority, close and
  running, with the point-of-view character narrating their own reactions as they
  happen.
- **Comedic Tolerance**: The mode permits comedy inside intimate scenes without
  breaking them. A Western erotic scene usually cannot survive a joke; this one
  expects at least one.
- **The Onomatopoeic Register**: See the section below. This is the signature.

## Optional Tropes
- **Isekai or Portal Premise**: The protagonist arrives from elsewhere and reads
  the world as a newcomer.
- **Ensemble Rotation**: Multiple love interests given real page time, resolved or
  deliberately unresolved.
- **Competence Ladder**: A visible skill, rank, or status the protagonist climbs
  alongside the relationships.
- **Interior Monologue as Comedy**: The gap between what the protagonist thinks
  and what they say carries the humor.
- **Status-Shift Reveal**: A character's archetype turns out to be a performance.
- **Slice-of-Life Interludes**: Low-stakes domestic chapters between escalations.

## The Onomatopoeic Register

Manga carries sound effects as drawn text, and translated and Western-written
light novels carry that convention into prose. In intimate scenes it produces
non-lexical vocalization written directly into the line rather than described:
sounds spelled out (*nn*, *hnn*, *ah*) instead of reported ("she made a small
noise").

**Why it works.** It collapses the distance between reader and scene. "She
whimpered" is the narrator telling you about a sound. The spelled sound is the
sound. It also does something plain prose struggles with: it renders a character
who cannot form words, which is often the point of the beat.

**Where it fails.** Four ways, all common:

1. **Volume.** Used every line, it stops reading as sound and starts reading as
   punctuation. Deploy it at the two or three moments where speech actually
   breaks down.
2. **Comedy leak.** English readers have no shared convention for these
   spellings, so an unfamiliar one reads as funny. Keep the inventory small and
   consistent within a book. A sound the reader has already met three times has
   stopped being strange.
3. **Substitution.** The sound is not a scene. If the vocalizations are doing the
   work that physical detail and interiority should be doing, the scene is empty
   and loud.
4. **Attribution drift.** In a two-person scene with spelled sounds and no tags,
   the reader loses track of who is making which. Anchor with an action beat.

**Craft rules.**
- Italicize consistently, or don't; pick one and hold it for the whole book.
- Keep it inside dialogue punctuation when a character is voicing it. It is
  speech, not narration.
- The point-of-view character's own sounds should surprise them. That reaction is
  the interiority the beat needs.
- Do not spell out a sound the character could have spoken. If they can say a
  word, let them say it.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: Escalating intimacy with a cast you enjoy spending time with,
  delivered with warmth rather than gravity.
- **Resolution**: The escalation arrives somewhere. Ensemble premises may leave
  the choice open, but the intimacy arc must land.
- **Pacing**: Fast. Short chapters, frequent scene turns, low tolerance for
  exposition.
- **Tone**: Warm and comedic even at high heat. Angst is permitted in bursts and
  is not the register.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The step being taken. Each escalation is the payoff of
  the tension built since the last one.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Establish the archetype and the situation inside the first scene.
  This mode does not hold back its premise.
- **Chapter Length**: 1,500-3,000 words. Shorter than most prose fiction.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: High. Chapters end on the interruption or the next
  step.
- **Act Structure**: Episodic escalation rather than a single rising arc. Each
  escalation is a small complete unit.

## At Short Length
- **One-shot**: One relationship, one escalation, one complication. Drop the
  ensemble entirely; there is no room to rotate a cast.
- **Novelette (10-12 ch.)**: Two love interests at most. Escalation steps at
  roughly chapters 3, 6, and 9, with the reversal at 8. Cut the competence ladder
  unless it is causing the intimacy.
