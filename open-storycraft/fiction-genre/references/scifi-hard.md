# Hard Sci-Fi

**Definition**: Science fiction where scientific accuracy drives the plot — real physics, biology, or engineering as narrative engine rather than backdrop.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Scientific Rigor**: The science is real or plausibly extrapolated. No handwaving.
- **Problem-Solving as Plot**: Characters use scientific thinking to overcome challenges.
- **Extrapolation**: "What if this real science were taken further?" as the premise.
- **Consequences of Technology**: Technology creates problems as well as solving them.

## Optional Tropes
- **Competence Porn**: Watching smart people solve hard problems.
- **First Principles Thinking**: Characters reason from physics/biology up.
- **Realistic Space Travel**: Proper orbital mechanics, radiation, delta-v constraints.
- **Near-Future Setting**: Close enough to today that the science feels immediate.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: A puzzle you could theoretically solve with a physics textbook.
- **Resolution**: The problem is solved through ingenuity and science.
- **Pacing**: Can be slow — the thinking IS the action.
- **Tone**: Cerebral, precise, often optimistic about human ingenuity.
- **Satisfaction Source**: "That's actually how it would work."

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Hook with the problem or the discovery.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-5,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Moderate.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act, problem-solution driven.

## Comp Titles
1. **The Martian** by Andy Weir — Survival through engineering, accessible hard sci-fi.
2. **Project Hail Mary** by Andy Weir — First contact + science puzzles.
3. **Seveneves** by Neal Stephenson — Orbital mechanics as survival drama.
4. **The Three-Body Problem** by Cixin Liu — Physics as existential threat.
5. **Rendezvous with Rama** by Arthur C. Clarke — Exploration of alien megastructure.

## Themes That Work
- **Human ingenuity**: We can think our way out of anything.
- **Nature doesn't care**: The universe is indifferent; only intelligence saves us.
- **Unintended consequences**: Every solution creates new problems.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Textbook syndrome**: Explaining the science at the expense of story and character.
- **No emotional stakes**: Even in hard sci-fi, readers need someone to root for.
- **Getting the science wrong**: The audience WILL check. Errors destroy credibility.
