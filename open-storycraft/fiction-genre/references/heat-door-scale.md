# The Door Scale

Shared vocabulary for how much of an intimate scene appears on the page. Every
subgenre detail file scores a `Romance` row from 1 to 5 in its Content Heat Map.
This names those numbers so a premise, an outline, and a draft all mean the same
thing by "spicy."

The scale measures **one thing only: how physically grounded the language gets.**
It does not measure how much sex happens, how central the romance is, or how dark
the relationship is. A story can be 5/5 on the door scale with one scene in it,
and 2/5 with a couple who sleep together constantly off the page.

## The bands

| Band | Score | What is on the page |
|---|---|---|
| No door | 1 | No romantic or sexual content. The scale does not apply. |
| **Closed Door** | **2** | Attraction, tension, and wanting are written. The scene stops at the threshold. The reader knows what happened and reads none of it. |
| **Open Door** | **3-4** | The scene is on the page. Bodies are present and moving, but the language stays at the level of gesture, sensation, and feeling rather than anatomy. Emotion carries the scene; the physical detail supports it. 3 is one or two scenes and restrained; 4 is frequent and more explicit. |
| **Door Wide Open** | **5** | Explicit, gratuitous, and repeated. Physically grounded language throughout: anatomy named plainly, mechanics on the page, no cutaways and no euphemism doing the work. Scenes are long, tantalizing, and return often. The choreography is tracked. The point-of-view character is present the whole way. |

## Choosing a band

Pick the band from the reader promise, not from personal comfort. A reader who
buys Door Wide Open and gets Closed Door has been shorted, and the reverse is
worse. The band is a contract.

Set it once at the premise stage and hold it. The common failure is drift: a
story opens Closed Door, gets to the scene that matters, and jumps to 5 because
the moment felt like it earned it. That reads as a different book starting.

## Register, not just explicitness

The band governs word choice, not only what is shown.

- **Closed Door** can be as charged as anything. Restraint is the technique. What
  the point-of-view character notices and refuses to name does the work.
- **Open Door** fails when it reaches for euphemism. Vague poetic substitution
  ("her core," "his need") reads as embarrassment, and embarrassment kills a
  scene faster than plainness. If a word is too much for the band, cut the
  sentence rather than dressing it.
- **Door Wide Open** fails when it becomes a parts list. Physically grounded
  means concrete and specific, not clinical and exhaustive. The point-of-view
  character still has a mind. Keep them in it. See below; this band has more
  requirements than the other two and most of them are craft, not permission.

## Door Wide Open in detail

The band is not "the same scene as Open Door with more words allowed." It is a
different contract, and four things define it.

**Gratuitous, and unapologetic about it.** The scenes do not need to be earned by
plot, justified by character growth, or paid for with consequences. They are the
product. A writer who keeps looking for a reason to permit the next scene is
writing Open Door and mislabeling it. The reader bought this.

**Repetitive by design.** One long explicit scene in a book is not this band.
Door Wide Open returns, often, and the return is the point. Each one can escalate
or vary, but frequency is a promise being kept and not padding.

**Tantalizing, not efficient.** The scenes are long and they linger. Delay,
interruption, anticipation, and the deliberate refusal to get to the point are
the technique. Rushing to the act and out again is the single most common way
this band disappoints; the wanting is most of what the reader came for.

**Choreographed.** This is the craft demand that separates good work here from
bad. Bodies occupy space. Who is where, facing which way, with which hand, in
what position, and how they got there from the last position all have to track.
The reader is following a physical sequence, and a moment that cannot be pictured
breaks the scene harder at 5 than at any other band, because there is nothing
else on the page to fall back on. Block it like a fight scene. Move deliberately,
account for every limb you mentioned, and never let a body part appear in a place
it could not have reached.

**And still in a mind.** All four of the above happen inside a point-of-view
character who is thinking, reacting, wanting, and occasionally wrong about what
is happening. Sustained explicit writing without interiority flattens into
stage directions within two pages. The character's attention is what selects
which physical detail gets named, which is what keeps a long scene from reading
as a transcript.

## Notation

In a heat map, write the band next to the number:

    | Romance | 5 | Door Wide Open. Explicit throughout. |

In a premise or an idea packet, one line is enough:

    **Door:** Open Door (4)
