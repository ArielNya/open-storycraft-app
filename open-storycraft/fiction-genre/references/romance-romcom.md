# Romantic Comedy

**Definition**: Romance driven by humor where witty banter, comedic situations, and laugh-out-loud moments are as essential to the reading experience as the love story itself.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **HEA/HFN**: The couple must achieve a happily ever after or happy for now ending.
- **Humor as Engine**: Comedy is not decorative; it drives scenes, reveals character, and creates the conditions for falling in love.
- **Witty Banter**: The couple's verbal chemistry must crackle. Their dialogue should be the best part of the book.
- **Comic Misunderstanding or Situation**: At least one major plot point arises from a comedic premise (mistaken identity, absurd bet, fake dating gone wrong, workplace chaos).

## Optional Tropes
- **Fake Dating**: Pretending to be together for external reasons until the pretense becomes real.
- **Enemies to Lovers**: Rivalry and bickering that masks attraction.
- **Opposites Attract**: Mismatched personalities forced together by circumstance.
- **Workplace Romance**: Office proximity, professional rivalry, and the comedy of maintaining boundaries.
- **Fish Out of Water**: One character in an unfamiliar environment, generating humor and vulnerability.
- **Bet or Dare**: A wager that forces proximity and creates escalating comedic stakes.
- **Meddling Friends/Family**: A well-meaning supporting cast that complicates everything.
- **Grand Gesture Gone Wrong**: The big romantic moment that spectacularly misfires before eventually landing.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: A joyful, laugh-out-loud reading experience where falling in love is fun, messy, and deeply satisfying.
- **Resolution**: The couple gets together after overcoming a comedic obstacle and a genuine emotional one. The humor does not undercut the sincerity of the ending.
- **Pacing**: Fast and light. Scenes move quickly, dialogue dominates, and introspection is brief and punchy.
- **Tone**: Light, warm, witty. Even serious moments are leavened with humor. The overall experience should leave the reader smiling.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The joy of watching two funny, charming people figure out that they belong together, and the balance of laughing hard and feeling deeply.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Fast. The comedic premise and the meet-cute (or meet-disaster) should land in chapter one.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,500-4,000 words. Brisk chapters that end on laugh lines or romantic tension.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Occasional. Rom-coms use chapter endings for comedic beats or "oh no" moments more than traditional cliffhangers.
- **Act Structure**: Three-act, closely mirroring classic romantic comedy film structure. Act 1 is the comedic setup and meet-cute. Act 2 is escalating proximity, growing attraction, and the comedic B-plot complicating everything. The "dark night of the soul" at the Act 2/3 break is the only sustained serious beat. Act 3 is the chase, the grand gesture, and the reunion. Typical length 70k-90k words.

## Comp Titles
1. **The Hating Game** by Sally Thorne — Enemies-to-lovers workplace rom-com that defined the modern genre; razor-sharp banter and escalating tension.
2. **Beach Read** by June Henry — Two writers swapping genres for a summer; meta-humor, genuine emotion, and a masterclass in balancing funny and heartfelt.
3. **The Kiss Quotient** by Helen Hoang — Neurodivergent heroine hires an escort to practice dating; fresh premise, sharp humor, and genuine tenderness.
4. **You've Got Mail** by Nora Ephron (screenplay/novelization) — The anonymous-pen-pal-who-is-actually-your-rival template that launched a thousand rom-coms.
5. **Red, White & Royal Blue** by Casey McQuiston — Political enemies-to-lovers with crackling dialogue, high-concept comedy, and sincere emotional stakes.

## Themes That Work
- **Vulnerability Behind the Joke**: Characters who use humor as armor learning to let someone see the real person underneath.
- **Unexpected Connection**: Finding love where you least expected it, often with the last person you would have chosen.
- **Self-Acceptance**: Learning to stop performing and let someone love the unpolished version.
- **Joy as Resistance**: In a cynical world, choosing to be open to love and laughter takes courage.

## Common Pitfalls
- **All Jokes, No Heart**: If the humor never pauses for genuine emotion, the romance feels hollow. The reader must believe these people actually love each other, not just that they are funny together.
- **Contrived Conflict**: The Act 3 breakup must arise from a real emotional issue, not a misunderstanding that a single conversation could resolve. Rom-com readers are especially allergic to manufactured drama.
- **One-Note Humor**: Relying on a single comedic register (only sarcasm, only physical comedy, only pop culture references). The best rom-coms vary their humor to keep the reader surprised.
