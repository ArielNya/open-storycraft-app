# Slice of Life Comedy

**Definition**: Fiction that finds humor in the mundane rhythms of everyday existence, using sharp observation and character voice to transform ordinary situations into comedic gold.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **The Mundane Made Extraordinary**: Ordinary events (grocery shopping, office meetings, family dinners) are rendered hilarious through voice, perspective, or escalation.
- **Character-Driven Humor**: The comedy emerges from who these people are, not from plot contrivances. Personality quirks, habits, and worldviews generate the laughs.
- **Observational Precision**: The prose notices small, specific, true details that the reader recognizes from their own life, and the recognition itself is funny.
- **Low Stakes, High Investment**: Nobody is saving the world. Somebody is trying to parallel park, host Thanksgiving, or survive a work retreat, and it matters enormously to them.

## Optional Tropes
- **Ensemble Dynamics**: A group of friends, family, or coworkers whose personalities create reliable comedic friction.
- **Recurring Situations**: The same type of event (weekly dinner, commute, holiday) that plays out differently each time.
- **The Outsider Perspective**: A newcomer who sees the absurdity of an established social ecosystem that insiders take for granted.
- **Seasonal or Calendar Structure**: The story follows a year, a semester, or a series of holidays, providing natural episodic rhythm.
- **Internal Monologue as Comedy**: The gap between what characters think and what they say (or do) is a primary humor source.
- **Understated Crisis**: Something that should be minor becomes the emotional center of a chapter, and the disproportionate response is the joke.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: You will recognize your own life in these pages and laugh at it.
- **Resolution**: Warm. Things may not change dramatically, but understanding deepens. The characters are a little more themselves by the end.
- **Pacing**: Relaxed and episodic. Individual scenes and chapters can be savored rather than raced through.
- **Tone**: Warm, wry, occasionally bittersweet. The humor has affection in it.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The pleasure of recognition. The feeling that someone else notices the same absurdities you do.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Can be leisurely. Establish the world, the voice, and one character's perspective on their ordinary life within the first chapter.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,500-5,000 words. Flexible, as each chapter often functions as a self-contained vignette.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Rare. Chapters end on emotional notes, punchlines, or quiet moments of clarity.
- **Act Structure**: Often episodic rather than strictly three-act. May follow a loose seasonal or thematic arc.

## Comp Titles
1. **Where'd You Go, Bernadette** by Maria Semple — Epistolary comedy built from the hilarious observations of a reclusive architect's daily frustrations with Seattle life.
2. **Anxious People** by Fredrik Backman — An ensemble of ordinary people trapped together, each one funny and flawed and struggling with recognizable problems.
3. **The Storied Life of A.J. Fikry** by Gabrielle Zevin — A curmudgeonly bookstore owner's life changes in small, funny, emotionally resonant increments.
4. **Convenience Store Woman** by Sayaka Murata — A woman perfectly content working at a convenience store, funny because the world insists she should want more.
5. **A Man Called Ove** by Fredrik Backman — A grumpy old man's daily routine disrupted by neighbors, generating comedy from stubbornness meeting warmth.

## Themes That Work
- **The Dignity of Small Lives**: Not everyone has a dramatic arc. Finding meaning and humor in routine existence affirms that ordinary lives are worth paying attention to.
- **Community by Proximity**: The people we are stuck with (neighbors, coworkers, family) become our people, whether we chose them or not. The comedy and warmth come from this involuntary intimacy.
- **Change Resistance**: Characters who insist things should stay the same while everything shifts around them are inherently funny and eventually moving.
- **The Performance of Normalcy**: Everyone is improvising adulthood. The gap between how competent people appear and how lost they feel is a bottomless comedic well.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Nothing Happens (for real)**: Low stakes does not mean no stakes. Each scene still needs tension, desire, or a question. If the character wants nothing and nothing resists them, there is no comedy and no story.
- **Quirky Without Substance**: Characters defined entirely by surface-level eccentricities (collects snow globes, only eats beige food) without emotional depth underneath become annoying rather than charming.
- **Sentimentality Creep**: The warm tone can slide into saccharine if the writer loses the sharpness of observation. The humor must stay honest, not soft-focus.
