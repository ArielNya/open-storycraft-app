# Urban Fantasy

**Definition**: Magic exists within modern cities, either hidden from the mundane world or woven into contemporary life alongside smartphones, coffee shops, and traffic.

## Mandatory Tropes

- **Modern Setting**: Contemporary city (or near-future). Cars, phones, electricity.
- **Magic in the City**: Supernatural elements operate within or alongside urban life.
- **Action-Oriented Protagonist**: Usually a practitioner, investigator, or someone caught between worlds.
- **Supernatural Community**: A hidden (or open) society of magical beings existing in the modern world.

## Optional Tropes

- **Masquerade**: The supernatural world is hidden from ordinary people.
- **Noir/Detective Elements**: Protagonist investigates supernatural crimes or mysteries.
- **Supernatural Politics**: Vampire courts, fae kingdoms, wizard councils operating in secret.
- **Romantic Subplot**: Often with a supernatural love interest.
- **Day Job**: Protagonist has a mundane life that conflicts with their supernatural one.
- **Chosen One / Special Power**: Protagonist has unique abilities that make them central to events.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: The thrill of magic hiding behind the familiar, and a protagonist cool enough to navigate both worlds.
- **Resolution**: The immediate supernatural threat is handled. Series threads continue.
- **Pacing**: Fast. Urban fantasy reads like a thriller with magic.
- **Tone**: Ranges from dark and gritty to snarky and fun.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The fantasy of being the person who knows the truth about what's really going on in the city.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Start with action or immediate supernatural weirdness. The city is already magical.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,500-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: High. Series-driven, chapter-hook expected.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act per book, typically long series (6-15 books).

## Comp Titles

1. **The Dresden Files** by Jim Butcher — The benchmark. Wizard PI in Chicago, long series, escalating stakes.
2. **Rivers of London** by Ben Aaronovitch — Police procedural meets London magic. Witty, detailed.
3. **The October Daye** series by Seanan McGuire — Fae politics in San Francisco.
4. **Neverwhere** by Neil Gaiman — Hidden magical London beneath the real one.
5. **Moon Called** by Patricia Briggs — Mercy Thompson series. Shapeshifters in the modern Pacific Northwest.

## Themes That Work
- **Hidden worlds**: What's really going on behind the mundane facade.
- **Belonging to two worlds**: Not fully fitting in either the normal or supernatural community.
- **Power and responsibility in the modern age**: Magic doesn't exempt you from human problems.
- **The city as character**: The urban setting itself shapes and reflects the supernatural elements.

## Common Pitfalls
- **World-building dump in chapter 1**: The reader doesn't need the complete supernatural taxonomy upfront. Reveal through action.
- **Protagonist is too special**: When the protagonist is the most powerful being in the city by book 3, tension dies. Power must have limits.
- **Modern setting as wallpaper**: If you could move the story to a medieval village and nothing changes, the "urban" part isn't doing work.
