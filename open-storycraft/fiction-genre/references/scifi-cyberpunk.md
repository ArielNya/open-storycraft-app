# Cyberpunk

**Definition**: Near-future dystopia where advanced technology coexists with societal collapse — megacorporations rule, hackers resist, and the line between human and machine blurs.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Corporate Dystopia**: Megacorporations have replaced or subsumed governments.
- **Technological Saturation**: Pervasive tech — cybernetics, VR, AI, surveillance.
- **Hacker/Outsider Protagonist**: Someone operating outside or against the system.
- **Urban Decay + High Tech**: "High tech, low life." Neon-lit streets, rain-slicked alleys.

## Optional Tropes
- **Body Augmentation**: Cybernetic implants as commonplace modification.
- **Virtual Reality**: Cyberspace or the net as a parallel world.
- **AI Consciousness**: Artificial minds with their own agendas.
- **Drugs/Altered States**: Chemical or electronic consciousness modification.
- **Class Warfare**: Extreme wealth inequality made visible through technology.
- **Noir Elements**: Detective stories in a cyberpunk setting.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: A future that feels disturbingly close to now, turned up to 11.
- **Resolution**: The protagonist survives and strikes a blow against the system (but the system endures).
- **Pacing**: Fast. Cyberpunk moves like a thriller.
- **Tone**: Dark, cynical, stylish. Noir-influenced.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The cool factor — style, tech, and attitude — combined with genuine commentary on where we're heading.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Start in the world. No origin story. Drop the reader into the neon.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,500-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: High.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act, often heist or investigation structured.

## Comp Titles
1. **Neuromancer** by William Gibson — The original cyberpunk novel.
2. **Snow Crash** by Neal Stephenson — Satirical cyberpunk, faster pace.
3. **Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?** by Philip K. Dick — Identity in a tech-saturated world.
4. **Altered Carbon** by Richard K. Morgan — Noir cyberpunk, consciousness transfer.
5. **The Windup Girl** by Paolo Bacigalupi — Biopunk-cyberpunk hybrid.

## Themes That Work
- **Identity**: Who are you when your body and mind are modifiable?
- **Corporate vs. individual freedom**: The system owns you unless you fight.
- **Technology as liberation AND oppression**: The same tools free and enslave.
- **Authenticity**: What's real when everything can be simulated?

## Common Pitfalls
- **All aesthetic, no substance**: Neon and chrome without something to say about technology and power.
- **Dated references**: Cyberpunk born in the 80s can feel retro. Update the tech, keep the ethos.
- **Nihilism without hope**: Even cyberpunk needs a spark — the protagonist fights because something matters.
