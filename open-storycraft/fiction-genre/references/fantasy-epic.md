# Epic Fantasy

**Definition**: Vast-scope fantasy with world-altering stakes, large casts, and a clear struggle between good and evil (or order and chaos) played out across kingdoms, continents, or planes.

## Mandatory Tropes

- **World-Altering Stakes**: The fate of nations, worlds, or reality itself hangs in the balance.
- **Large Cast**: Multiple POV characters across factions, each with their own arc.
- **Quest or War**: A driving mission or conflict that spans the entire narrative.
- **Deep World-Building**: History, languages, cultures, magic systems, and geography that feel lived-in.
- **Chosen One or Unlikely Hero**: A protagonist thrust into events larger than themselves.

## Optional Tropes

- **Ancient Prophecy**: A foretold event that the protagonist must fulfill or prevent.
- **Dark Lord**: A singular antagonist embodying existential threat.
- **Magic System**: Structured or mysterious, but present and significant.
- **Fellowship/Party**: A group assembled for the quest, each member essential.
- **Political Intrigue**: Thrones, alliances, betrayals within the larger conflict.
- **Lost Kingdom/Artifact**: Something ancient that must be found, restored, or destroyed.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: An immersive world you can get lost in for hundreds of pages.
- **Resolution**: The great evil is defeated or held back; the world is changed but saved.
- **Pacing**: Slow build with epic payoffs. Readers accept setup chapters if the climax delivers.
- **Tone**: Grand, serious, often hopeful despite darkness.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The feeling that this world is real, these stakes matter, and the ending was earned across thousands of pages.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Can be slow (world introduction) but must hook with character or mystery.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-6,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Moderate. Multi-POV switching creates natural suspense.
- **Act Structure**: Often 3-act across multiple books. Each book has its own arc within the series arc.

## Content Heat Map
| Dimension | Level (1-5) | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|

| Darkness  | 3 | Stakes are real, characters die, but hope persists |

## Comp Titles

1. **The Lord of the Rings** by J.R.R. Tolkien — The template. Quest, fellowship, world-building depth.
2. **The Wheel of Time** by Robert Jordan — Massive scope, chosen one, 14-book series.
3. **The Stormlight Archive** by Brandon Sanderson — Modern epic with hard magic system and layered world.
4. **A Song of Ice and Fire** by George R.R. Martin — Political epic pushing toward grimdark edge.
5. **The Name of the Wind** by Patrick Rothfuss — First-person epic with literary prose quality.

## Themes That Work
- **Destiny vs. Choice**: Are heroes born or made?
- **Power and Corruption**: What happens when good people gain too much power?
- **Sacrifice for the Greater Good**: What must be given up to save the world?
- **Fellowship and Loyalty**: The bonds that hold against impossible odds.

## Common Pitfalls
- **World-building at the expense of character**: Pages of lore with no one to care about. The world serves the characters, not the other way around.
- **Endless setup, no payoff**: Multiple books of rising action that never climax. Each book must deliver its own satisfying arc.
- **Cardboard villain**: The Dark Lord must have motivation beyond "being evil." Even existential threats need logic.
