# Cozy Fantasy

**Definition**: Low-stakes fantasy with a warm tone, emphasizing found family, community, and comfort over conflict, danger, or darkness.

## Mandatory Tropes

- **Low Stakes**: No world-ending threats. The "conflict" might be opening a bakery, winning a contest, or healing a community rift.
- **Warm Tone**: The world is fundamentally safe and kind. Bad things may happen but they resolve gently.
- **Found Family**: Chosen relationships — friends, neighbors, mentors — form the emotional core.
- **Comfort Elements**: Food, cozy spaces, warm clothing, seasonal rhythms, small pleasures described with care.

## Optional Tropes

- **Cottage/Shop/Tavern**: A central cozy location the protagonist tends.
- **Animal Companions**: Magical or mundane pets who are characters in their own right.
- **Gentle Magic**: Magic used for everyday life — growing herbs, enchanting tea, weather charms.
- **Small Community**: A village, neighborhood, or found-family group rather than a kingdom.
- **Healing Arc**: Protagonist recovering from trauma or burnout in a safe environment.
- **Slice of Life**: Episodic structure focused on daily life rather than a quest.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: A safe reading experience that leaves you feeling warm and comforted.
- **Resolution**: The protagonist finds their place. The community is strengthened. No one dies.
- **Pacing**: Slow and deliberate. The pace IS the experience. Rushing defeats the purpose.
- **Tone**: Warm, gentle, sometimes whimsical. Think autumn afternoon, not battlefield dawn.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The literary equivalent of a warm blanket and a cup of tea.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Introduce the cozy world and the protagonist's gentle problem. No urgency needed.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,000-3,500 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Low. Chapter endings are satisfying resting points, not hooks.
- **Act Structure**: Loose 3-act, but can be episodic. The "climax" is emotional, not action-based.

## Comp Titles

1. **Legends & Lattes** by Travis Baldree — Ex-adventurer opens a coffee shop. The genre-defining cozy fantasy.
2. **The House in the Cerulean Sea** by TJ Klune — Found family, gentle stakes, institutional kindness.
3. **A Psalm for the Wild-Built** by Becky Chambers — Cozy sci-fi/fantasy about a monk and a robot.
4. **Under the Whispering Door** by TJ Klune — Afterlife tea shop, grief processing, warm community.
5. **Bookshops & Bonedust** by Travis Baldree — Prequel to Legends & Lattes, same warm energy.

## Themes That Work
- **Rest as resistance**: Choosing peace over ambition in a world that rewards striving.
- **Community over individuality**: Strength in connection, not in isolation.
- **Small things matter**: A good cup of tea, a repaired friendship, a garden tended well.
- **Healing takes time**: Recovery from past hardship, given space and gentleness.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Boring, not cozy**: Cozy still needs a story. There must be a question the reader wants answered, even if it's small.
- **Forced whimsy**: Trying too hard to be cute. The warmth should feel organic, not performative.
- **Dark subplot injection**: Adding a grimdark B-plot because the author worries "nothing is happening." Trust the genre. Readers chose cozy on purpose.
