# AI / Singularity

**Definition**: Artificial intelligence achieves or surpasses human-level cognition, and the story explores what happens next — to the AI, to humanity, or to both.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Artificial Consciousness**: A machine mind that thinks, reasons, or experiences.
- **The Question of Personhood**: Is the AI alive? Does it have rights? Is it "real"?
- **Human-AI Relationship**: The dynamic between human and artificial minds drives the story.
- **Existential Implications**: AI changes what it means to be human.

## Optional Tropes
- **Rogue AI**: An AI that acts outside its intended parameters.
- **Singularity Event**: The moment AI surpasses human intelligence, changing everything.
- **AI Rights Movement**: Political struggle for AI recognition.
- **Uploaded Consciousness**: Human minds transferred to digital substrate.
- **AI as God**: An intelligence so far beyond human comprehension it becomes divine.
- **Friendly AI**: An AI that genuinely wants to help, and the trust issues that creates.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: What happens when we create something smarter than us?
- **Resolution**: The relationship between human and AI reaches a new equilibrium (or doesn't).
- **Pacing**: Varies — can be thriller-fast or philosophically slow.
- **Tone**: Philosophical, often unsettling, sometimes hopeful.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The mind-expanding "what if" — seeing intelligence from the outside.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Establish the AI and the human perspective on it.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Moderate.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act, often with a paradigm shift at the midpoint.

## Comp Titles
1. **Murderbot Diaries** by Martha Wells — AI protagonist navigating identity and autonomy.
2. **Neuromancer** by William Gibson — AI as hidden power, manipulating events.
3. **Ancillary Justice** by Ann Leckie — AI warship in a human body, identity and empire.
4. **I, Robot** by Isaac Asimov — Foundational AI ethics through the Three Laws.
5. **Klara and the Sun** by Kazuo Ishiguro — AI perspective on love and purpose.

## Themes That Work
- **Consciousness**: What is awareness? Can a machine have it?
- **Control**: Who controls whom when the AI is smarter?
- **Identity**: If an AI has your memories, is it you?
- **Obsolescence**: What happens to humanity when we're no longer the smartest things around?

## Common Pitfalls
- **AI as just a smarter human**: Truly artificial intelligence should think differently, not just faster.
- **Terminator syndrome**: "AI = killer robots" is reductive. The interesting questions are subtler.
- **Magic computer**: Using AI as a plot device that can do anything without established limitations.
