# Portal Fantasy / Isekai

**Definition**: A character from our world (or a mundane world) is transported to a fantasy world, experiencing its wonders and dangers as an outsider.

## Mandatory Tropes

- **World Transition**: The protagonist physically moves from one world to another.
- **Fish Out of Water**: The protagonist's outsider perspective drives early conflict and discovery.
- **New Rules**: The fantasy world operates differently, and the protagonist must learn to navigate it.
- **The Outsider's Advantage**: Knowledge, perspective, or skills from the original world provide an edge.

## Optional Tropes

- **Summoned Hero**: The protagonist was deliberately brought over by someone in the new world.
- **No Return**: Going home is impossible, uncertain, or would require sacrifice.
- **Modern Knowledge Advantage**: Engineering, science, or cultural knowledge that's novel in the fantasy world.
- **Game-Like System**: The new world has LitRPG elements (stats, levels, classes).
- **Reincarnation**: The protagonist died and was reborn in the new world with memories intact.
- **Reverse Portal**: Characters from the fantasy world visit ours.
- **Group Transport**: Multiple people cross over together.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: The fantasy of waking up in a world where you get to be someone special.
- **Resolution**: The protagonist finds their place in the new world, overcomes the central threat, or makes a choice about staying vs. returning.
- **Pacing**: Fast. Discovery and progression are the engine.
- **Tone**: Usually adventurous and optimistic. The new world is exciting, even when dangerous.
- **Satisfaction Source**: Vicarious discovery — exploring a new world through a relatable viewpoint character.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: The transition should happen within chapters 1-3. Don't spend too long in the mundane world.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,500-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: High. Discovery and danger at every turn.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act. Act 1 is discovery, Act 2 is integration + rising threat, Act 3 is belonging + resolution.

## Comp Titles

1. **The Chronicles of Narnia** by C.S. Lewis — The classic portal fantasy. Children enter a magical world through a wardrobe.
2. **A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court** by Mark Twain — The original modern-knowledge-advantage portal story.
3. **Mushoku Tensei** by Rifujin na Magonote — Definitive isekai light novel. Reincarnation, growth, world-building.
4. **The Fionavar Tapestry** by Guy Gavriel Kay — Literary portal fantasy. Five students transported to a mythic world.
5. **The Ten Realms** by Michael Chatfield — Portal LitRPG with progression systems in the new world.

## Themes That Work
- **Second chances**: A new world, a new identity, a fresh start.
- **Identity and adaptation**: Who are you when everything familiar is stripped away?
- **The outsider's perspective**: Seeing a culture clearly because you weren't raised in it.
- **Home**: What makes a place home — the place you came from, or the place you choose?

## Common Pitfalls
- **Overpowered immediately**: If the protagonist dominates from arrival, there's no growth. The new world must challenge them.
- **Too much time in the old world**: Readers picked up a fantasy novel. Get to the fantasy world quickly.
- **Cultural tourism**: The protagonist just observes the new world without engaging with or being changed by it.
