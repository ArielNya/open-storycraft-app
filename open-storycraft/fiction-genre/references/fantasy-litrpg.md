# LitRPG / Progression Fantasy

**Definition**: Fantasy with game-like mechanics (stats, levels, skills, classes) woven into narrative, where characters grow measurably stronger through effort, strategy, and discovery.

## Mandatory Tropes

- **System Interface**: Visible stats, skills, levels, or progression metrics the character and reader can track.
- **Measurable Progression**: The protagonist gets demonstrably stronger over time — readers can see the growth in numbers or abilities.
- **Earned Power**: Strength comes from effort, strategy, or clever optimization, not birthright or destiny.
- **Challenge Escalation**: Enemies and obstacles scale with the protagonist's power, maintaining tension.

## Optional Tropes

- **Dungeon Delving**: Structured challenge zones with increasing difficulty and loot rewards.
- **Class/Build Optimization**: The protagonist makes strategic choices about how to develop their abilities.
- **Party Formation**: A team assembles with complementary builds and roles.
- **Crafting Systems**: Making equipment or items as a progression path.
- **Hidden Mechanics**: The system has secrets that give strategic advantages to those who discover them.
- **Isekai/Portal**: Character transported from our world into the game-like world.
- **Unique Ability**: Protagonist has an unusual class, skill, or approach that others overlooked.
- **Economy/Trading**: In-world markets, item values, economic strategy.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: Satisfying power growth you can watch happen in real time.
- **Resolution**: The protagonist overcomes a challenge that was previously impossible, using everything they've built.
- **Pacing**: Fast. Frequent progression milestones. Readers want regular dopamine hits from level-ups and new abilities.
- **Tone**: Usually optimistic or adventurous. Dark LitRPG exists but the progression should still feel rewarding.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The "I built this" feeling — watching strategic decisions compound into power.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Fast. System introduction within the first 1-3 chapters. First level-up or ability gain early.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-5,000 words (LitRPG chapters tend to run longer).
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: High. Chapter-ending hooks are expected.
- **Act Structure**: Episodic arcs within a larger progression. Often "zone" or "floor" based structure.

## Comp Titles

1. **Dungeon Crawler Carl** by Matt Dinniman — Gold standard for humor + progression + escalating stakes
2. **Cradle** series by Will Wight — Progression fantasy with martial arts cultivation, fast-paced
3. **He Who Fights with Monsters** by Shirtaloon — Isekai LitRPG, strong character voice, long series
4. **The Primal Hunter** by Zogarth — System apocalypse with optimization focus
5. **Defiance of the Fall** by TheFirstDefier — Isekai/system apocalypse, progression through combat

## Themes That Work
- **Optimization as expression of intelligence**: The smart protagonist finds what others miss.
- **Earned vs. given power**: Grinding beats talent. Effort matters.
- **Adapting to new rules**: Navigating a system that doesn't care about you.
- **Community through shared challenge**: Bonds formed in dungeons and combat.
- **The game behind the game**: Systems have creators with agendas.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Stat-dump paralysis**: Pages of numbers with no narrative context. Stats must serve the story, not replace it.
- **Power without cost**: If the protagonist levels up without sacrifice, tension evaporates. Growth must cost something.
- **Protagonist has no personality**: So focused on the system that the character is just a vessel for optimization. Readers need someone to root for, not a spreadsheet.
