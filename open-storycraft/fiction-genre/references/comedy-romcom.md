# Romantic Comedy

**Definition**: Fiction where humor is the primary engine driving a love story, with witty banter, comedic misunderstandings, and charming awkwardness propelling two people toward each other.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Meet Cute (or Meet Disaster)**: The love interests' first encounter is memorable, funny, and sets the comedic tone for their entire relationship.
- **Witty Banter**: The central couple trades sharp, fast dialogue that reveals attraction through verbal sparring rather than earnest confession.
- **Comedic Obstacles**: The barriers keeping the couple apart are funny, not tragic. Misunderstandings, bad timing, meddling friends, professional rivalry.
- **The Grand Gesture or Grovel**: One or both characters must do something publicly vulnerable and often ridiculous to earn the happy ending.
- **Guaranteed HEA/HFN**: The couple ends up together. This is a covenant with the reader, not a suggestion.

## Optional Tropes
- **Forced Proximity**: Stuck together by circumstance (shared apartment, work project, snowstorm, wedding party).
- **Opposites Attract**: Contrasting personalities that create friction and comedy.
- **Ensemble Cast of Meddlers**: Friends, family, or coworkers who commentate, interfere, and provide subplot comedy.
- **The Fake Relationship**: Pretending to date for practical reasons, then catching real feelings.
- **Professional Rivalry**: Competing for the same job, client, or award while falling for each other.
- **Second Chance**: Former flames reunited by circumstance, with old chemistry and new comedy.
- **The Humiliating Public Moment**: A scene where a character embarrasses themselves spectacularly, often as the catalyst for emotional honesty.
- **Holiday or Event Deadline**: A wedding, reunion, or holiday that creates a ticking clock for the relationship.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: You will laugh, swoon, and feel good about love by the last page.
- **Resolution**: Happy ending, always. The couple together, the comedic conflicts resolved, the world a little warmer.
- **Pacing**: Brisk. Romantic tension and comedic set pieces alternate to keep energy high.
- **Tone**: Warm and light, even when dealing with real emotions. The humor never disappears for long.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The inevitability of the couple getting together combined with the fun of watching them resist it.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: The meet cute should happen within the first chapter or two. Establish both characters and their chemistry fast.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-5,000 words. Long enough for scenes to breathe, short enough to maintain momentum.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Occasional. Chapters more often end on a comedic beat or a moment of unexpected vulnerability.
- **Act Structure**: Classic three-act. Act 1: meet and spark. Act 2: growing closer while obstacles multiply. Act 3: the dark moment, then resolution.

## Comp Titles
1. **The Flatshare** by Beth O'Leary — Forced proximity rom-com with alternating POVs, sharp humor, and a slow-burn romance built on sticky notes.
2. **Beach Read** by June Henry — Two writers with opposite genres swap styles for a summer, generating comedy from the collision of worldviews.
3. **The Rosie Project** by Graeme Simsion — A socially awkward genetics professor designs a questionnaire to find the perfect wife, then meets someone who fails every criterion.
4. **Bridget Jones's Diary** by Helen Fielding — The self-deprecating, disaster-prone heroine who defined modern rom-com voice.
5. **You've Got Mail** by Nora Ephron (novelization) — Rivals in business, anonymous soulmates online. The ur-text of enemies-to-lovers rom-com.

## Themes That Work
- **Vulnerability as Strength**: The funniest characters are often the most guarded. The comedy comes from their defenses; the romance comes from those defenses dropping.
- **Being Known**: The deepest romantic satisfaction is not being loved generically but being seen specifically, quirks and all. Comedy accelerates this by forcing characters into situations where pretense is impossible.
- **Chosen Family**: Rom-coms thrive when the couple exists within a warm community. The ensemble reflects and amplifies the central love story.
- **Imperfection as Charm**: Characters who try to be perfect are funny because they fail. Characters who embrace their mess are lovable because they are real.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Conflict That Could Be Solved by a Conversation**: If the entire third-act crisis would dissolve if the characters just talked to each other, the reader feels cheated. The obstacle must be structural or emotional, not just a withheld piece of information.
- **Humor That Undermines Emotional Beats**: When it is time for genuine vulnerability, the jokes need to step back. If a character cracks wise during their emotional climax, the reader cannot feel the weight of the moment.
- **One-Sided Chemistry**: Both characters must be funny, appealing, and active in the romance. If only one character drives the humor and pursuit, the relationship feels unequal and the comedy goes flat.
