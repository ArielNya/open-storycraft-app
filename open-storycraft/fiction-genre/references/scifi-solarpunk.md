# Solarpunk

**Definition**: Optimistic near-future fiction where humanity has solved (or is solving) ecological crises through sustainable technology, community cooperation, and creative adaptation.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Optimistic Future**: The world is getting better, not worse.
- **Sustainable Technology**: Solar, wind, biotech, permaculture — green tech as norm.
- **Community Solutions**: Problems solved collectively, not by lone heroes.
- **Ecological Harmony**: Humanity living with nature, not against it.

## Optional Tropes
- **Green Architecture**: Living buildings, vertical farms, garden cities.
- **Appropriate Technology**: Tech that fits the community rather than imposing on it.
- **Post-Scarcity**: Basic needs are met; the conflict is about meaning, not survival.
- **Cultural Diversity**: Multiple approaches to sustainability across cultures.
- **Activism/Transition**: Stories set during the change from our world to the solarpunk future.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: A future worth wanting — proof that things CAN get better.
- **Resolution**: The community challenge is met through cooperation and ingenuity.
- **Pacing**: Moderate to slow. The journey matters more than the crisis.
- **Tone**: Hopeful, warm, sometimes activist.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The feeling that a better world is possible.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Introduce the beautiful world, then the challenge within it.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,500-3,500 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Low to moderate.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act, community-driven rather than hero-driven.

## Content Heat Map
| Dimension | Level (1-5) | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Violence  | 1-2 | Conflict through ideas, not combat |
| Romance   | 2-3 | Often present, gentle |
| Language   | 1-2 | Clean |
| Darkness  | 1-2 | Deliberately light; the genre is the antidote to dystopia |

## Comp Titles
1. **A Psalm for the Wild-Built** by Becky Chambers — Cozy solarpunk, monk meets robot.
2. **The Ministry for the Future** by Kim Stanley Robinson — Near-future climate solutions.
3. **Walkaway** by Cory Doctorow — Post-scarcity, community building, maker culture.
4. **The Dispossessed** by Ursula K. Le Guin — Utopian sci-fi exploring what "better" really means.
5. **Sunvault: Stories of Solarpunk and Eco-Speculation** (anthology) — Multiple solarpunk voices.

## Themes That Work
- **Hope as action**: Optimism isn't naive; it's a choice that requires work.
- **Community over individualism**: We solve problems together or not at all.
- **Living with limits**: Sustainability as liberation, not deprivation.
- **Technology in service of life**: Tools that heal rather than exploit.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Conflict-free utopia**: Even in a better world, there must be story tension. Interpersonal, philosophical, or transitional conflicts.
- **Preachy environmentalism**: Show the better world; don't lecture about why it's better.
- **Ignoring human nature**: People in solarpunk worlds still have flaws, disagreements, and desires.
