# Time Travel

**Definition**: Characters move through time, confronting paradoxes, consequences, and the question of whether the past can or should be changed.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Temporal Displacement**: Characters physically move to a different time period.
- **Consequences**: Changing the past has effects (or the impossibility of change is the point).
- **Temporal Rules**: The story establishes how time travel works and follows those rules.
- **The Paradox Question**: The story engages with the logical implications of time travel.

## Optional Tropes
- **Butterfly Effect**: Small changes cascade into massive consequences.
- **Predestination Paradox**: The time traveler was always part of the past.
- **Time Loop**: The same period repeats until something is resolved.
- **Time Police**: An organization enforcing temporal integrity.
- **Fish Out of Water**: A modern person navigating a historical period.
- **Multiple Timelines**: Branching realities from different choices.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: The thrill of "what if you could go back?" combined with the puzzle of temporal mechanics.
- **Resolution**: The temporal puzzle is solved, or the character accepts what cannot be changed.
- **Pacing**: Varies by subtype — loops are fast, historical journeys are moderate.
- **Tone**: Ranges from adventure to philosophical to heartbreaking.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The moment the temporal logic clicks — when the reader sees how all the pieces fit.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Establish the normal timeline, then break it.
- **Chapter Length**: 2,500-4,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: High — temporal reveals make great hooks.
- **Act Structure**: Often circular or spiral rather than linear.

## Comp Titles
1. **The Time Machine** by H.G. Wells — The original. Future as social commentary.
2. **11/22/63** by Stephen King — Changing a historical event, consequences and cost.
3. **The Time Traveler's Wife** by Audrey Niffenegger — Time travel as relationship drama.
4. **All You Need Is Kill** by Hiroshi Sakurazaka — Time loop military sci-fi.
5. **Dark Matter** by Blake Crouch — Multiverse/time-adjacent thriller.

## Themes That Work
- **Regret and acceptance**: Can you fix the past, and should you?
- **Determinism vs. free will**: Is the future fixed, or can it be changed?
- **The weight of knowledge**: Knowing the future is a burden, not a gift.
- **Loss across time**: People separated by centuries, not miles.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Inconsistent rules**: Establish how time travel works and stick to it. Readers track paradoxes.
- **Solving everything with time travel**: If the protagonist can just go back and try again, stakes evaporate.
- **Ignoring the personal cost**: Time travel should change the traveler, not just the timeline.
