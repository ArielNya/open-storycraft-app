# High Fantasy

**Definition**: Fantasy set entirely in a secondary world with its own rules, history, geography, and cultures — no connection to Earth.

## Mandatory Tropes

- **Secondary World**: The story takes place in a fully invented world, not Earth with magic added.
- **Internal Consistency**: The world has its own logic, history, and rules that are followed.
- **Magic as Part of the World**: Magic exists as a natural element, not an intrusion.
- **Significant Stakes**: The conflict matters at a societal or world level.

## Optional Tropes

- **Complex Magic System**: Defined rules, costs, and limitations.
- **Multiple Cultures**: Distinct civilizations with their own customs and languages.
- **Political Conflict**: Nations or factions in competition.
- **Non-Human Races**: Original species with distinct cultures.
- **Ancient History**: Deep past shaping the present.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: A world so detailed and coherent it feels real.
- **Resolution**: The central conflict resolves. The world endures.
- **Pacing**: Moderate. World-building needs setup but every chapter must advance.
- **Tone**: Serious, immersive. Hopeful to dark range.
- **Satisfaction Source**: Depth of immersion — visiting a real place.

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Can open with world immersion but needs a character hook in chapter 1.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-5,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Moderate.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act, often across series.

## Comp Titles

1. **The Stormlight Archive** by Brandon Sanderson — Massive secondary world, hard magic, deep lore.
2. **The Earthsea Cycle** by Ursula K. Le Guin — Elegant secondary world, philosophical.
3. **The Realm of the Elderlings** by Robin Hobb — Character-focused, deep emotional arcs.
4. **The Goblin Emperor** by Katherine Addison — Political high fantasy, warm tone.
5. **Mistborn** by Brandon Sanderson — Tight magic system, heist structure.

## Themes That Work
- **The weight of history**: Past events shape present conflicts.
- **Culture clash**: Multiple civilizations create natural friction.
- **The cost of magic**: Power should have rules and consequences.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Exposition overload**: Explaining the world before the story starts.
- **Earth analogue laziness**: Cultures that are just "medieval Europe with elves."
- **Magic without cost**: If magic solves everything, there's no tension.
