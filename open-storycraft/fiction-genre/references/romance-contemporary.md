# Contemporary Romance

**Definition**: Love stories set in the present day where two people navigate real-world obstacles to find their happily ever after.

## Mandatory Tropes
- **Meet Cute or Inciting Connection**: The leads must cross paths in a way that sparks tension or chemistry from the first interaction.
- **Central Love Story**: The romance IS the plot — not a subplot. Remove the romance and there is no book.
- **Conflict Between the Leads**: Internal or external forces must keep them apart until earned resolution.
- **HEA or HFN**: The story MUST end with a Happily Ever After or Happy For Now. Non-negotiable genre contract.
- **Emotional Vulnerability**: At least one scene where a lead drops their guard completely.

## Optional Tropes
- **Enemies to Lovers**: Friction before attraction.
- **Fake Relationship**: Pretending becomes real.
- **Second Chance**: Reunited after time apart.
- **Friends to Lovers**: Slow realization of what was always there.
- **Forced Proximity**: Stuck together by circumstance.
- **Opposites Attract**: Complementary personalities collide.
- **Secret Identity / Hidden Past**: One lead hides something that could break them apart.

## Reader Expectations
- **Promise**: You will feel the butterflies, the heartbreak, and the relief of two people choosing each other.
- **Resolution**: Together. Always together. The question is how they get there.
- **Pacing**: Moderate — tension builds through push-pull, not action sequences.
- **Tone**: Warm to angsty depending on subtype, but ultimately hopeful.
- **Satisfaction Source**: The emotional payoff of the relationship being earned, not given.

## Heat Levels
| Level | Label | Description |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Sweet | Closed door. Kisses max. Emotional intimacy is the focus. |
| 2 | Warm | Some physical scenes, tastefully described. Fade to black common. |
| 3 | Hot | On-page intimacy with moderate detail. 2-4 scenes typical. |
| 4 | Steamy | Detailed, frequent intimate scenes integral to character development. |
| 5 | Scorching | Explicit content is a primary feature. High frequency and detail. |

## Pacing Conventions
- **Opening**: Hook with voice or situation within 2 pages. Meet cute by chapter 2-3.
- **Chapter Length**: 3,000-5,000 words.
- **Cliffhanger Frequency**: Emotional cliffhangers most chapters. Plot cliffhangers at act breaks.
- **Act Structure**: 3-act. Act 1: meet + spark. Act 2: deepen + complicate. Act 3: dark moment + resolution.

## Content Heat Map
| Dimension | Level (1-5) | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Violence  | 1 | Rare. Emotional violence (betrayal, lies) is the real threat. |
| Romance   | 5 | This IS the genre. Central and constant. |
| Language   | 2-3 | Casual modern dialogue. Profanity varies by heat level. |
| Darkness  | 2 | Angst yes, trauma handled with care, ultimately hopeful. |

## Comp Titles
1. **The Hating Game** by Sally Thorne — Enemies-to-lovers office romance with sharp banter and slow burn.
2. **Beach Read** by June Henry — Two writers swap genres and fall in love. Meta, witty, emotional.
3. **The Kiss Quotient** by Helen Hoang — Unique protagonist, fake relationship trope, emotionally authentic.
4. **People We Meet on Vacation** by June Henry — Friends-to-lovers with dual timeline structure.
5. **It Ends with Us** by Colleen Hoover — Darker contemporary romance that pushed content boundaries.

## Themes That Work
- **Self-worth before partnership**: A lead must value themselves before they can be loved well.
- **Communication as intimacy**: The hardest conversations are the most romantic scenes.
- **Choosing vulnerability**: Walls exist for good reasons, but love requires tearing them down.
- **Growth through connection**: Both leads should be different people by the end.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Miscommunication as sole conflict**: If one conversation would solve the plot, the conflict is too thin. Layer external and internal obstacles.
- **Losing the lead's agency**: Both characters must actively choose each other. Passive leads kill tension.
- **Rushing the dark moment resolution**: The breakup/separation in Act 3 needs real weight. Don't fix it in two pages.
