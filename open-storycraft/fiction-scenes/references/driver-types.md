# Chapter Driver Types

Every chapter must have a driver - something that changes by the end. Vary driver types to prevent monotony.

---

## Plot Drivers

### Information Revealed
Character learns something that changes their understanding.
- Discovery of a secret
- Revelation about another character
- New obstacle revealed
- Plan exposed

**Test**: Does knowing this change what the character does next?

### Obstacle Introduced
A new barrier appears between character and goal.
- Physical barrier (locked door, guarded passage)
- Social barrier (opposition, prohibition)
- Internal barrier (fear, doubt, conflicting loyalty)
- Resource barrier (lack of time, money, allies)

**Test**: Is this obstacle significant enough to require adaptation?

### Decision Point
Character must choose between options with real stakes.
- Moral dilemma (right vs. right, or least-wrong choice)
- Strategic choice (which path, which ally, which sacrifice)
- Identity choice (who they want to be vs. who they've been)

**Test**: Would a different choice lead to a different story?

### Stakes Escalation
The cost of failure increases.
- Time pressure tightens
- More people endangered
- Personal stakes raised
- Consequences clarified

**Test**: Does the reader feel more tension leaving than entering?

---

## Relationship Drivers

### Alliance Formed
Characters move from separate to united.
- Reluctant partnership
- Trust established
- Shared goal identified
- Mutual need recognized

**Test**: Are these characters demonstrably closer than before?

### Alliance Tested
Existing relationship faces strain.
- Conflicting priorities surface
- Secret threatens trust
- External pressure applied
- Loyalty questioned

**Test**: Is the relationship genuinely at risk?

### Alliance Broken
Characters who were united now oppose.
- Betrayal (real or perceived)
- Irreconcilable difference
- Sacrifice of one for another
- Values clash

**Test**: Can this breach be repaired? If easily, it's not a real break.

### Power Dynamic Shift
Who leads, who follows changes.
- Mentor falls, student rises
- Leader challenged
- Hierarchy inverted
- New respect earned

**Test**: Do subsequent scenes reflect the new dynamic?

---

## Character Drivers

### Belief Challenged
Character's worldview faces contradiction.
- Evidence against assumption
- Person they trusted disappoints
- System they believed in fails
- Their own actions contradict their self-image

**Test**: Does the character have to reckon with this?

### Skill Gained
Character becomes capable of something new.
- Training pays off
- Desperation unlocks ability
- Mentor's lesson clicks
- Practice becomes mastery

**Test**: Will this skill matter later?

### Wound Opened
Character's vulnerability is exposed or deepened.
- Old trauma triggered
- New loss inflicted
- Weakness exploited
- Fear realized

**Test**: Does this change how they approach subsequent challenges?

### Want vs. Need Clash
External goal conflicts with internal need.
- Getting what they want costs what they need
- Pursuing need means abandoning want
- Both paths have real consequences

**Test**: Does the character feel the pull of both?

---

## Structural Drivers

### Promise Made
Chapter establishes expectation reader wants fulfilled.
- Prophecy or warning given
- Goal clearly stated
- Danger clearly established
- Question clearly posed

**Test**: Will the reader remember this promise?

### Promise Kept
Earlier setup pays off.
- Chekhov's gun fires
- Foreshadowed event occurs
- Prophecy fulfilled (or subverted)
- Question answered

**Test**: Does this feel earned, not convenient?

### Reversal
Situation inverts.
- Hunter becomes hunted
- Safe becomes dangerous
- Ally becomes enemy
- Strength becomes weakness

**Test**: Is the reversal surprising but logical?

### Revelation
Something hidden becomes known.
- True identity revealed
- Hidden motive exposed
- Secret history uncovered
- Lie exposed

**Test**: Does this recontextualize earlier scenes?

---

## Combining Drivers

Strong chapters often layer multiple drivers:

**Example**: Character learns their mentor lied (Information + Belief Challenged), must decide whether to confront them (Decision Point), and realizes the mission is more dangerous than promised (Stakes Escalation).

**Warning**: Too many drivers in one chapter = reader overwhelm. 2-3 is ideal.

---

## Driver Distribution

Track driver types across your outline:

| Chapter | Primary Driver | Secondary Driver |
|---------|---------------|------------------|
| 1 | Promise Made | Information Revealed |
| 2 | Alliance Formed | Obstacle Introduced |
| 3 | Stakes Escalation | Belief Challenged |
| ... | ... | ... |

**Check for**:
- Too many consecutive chapters with same driver type
- Driver types clustering (all relationship, no plot)
- Missing driver types entirely
- Escalation building toward climax
