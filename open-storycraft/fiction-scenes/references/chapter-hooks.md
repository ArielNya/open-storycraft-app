# Chapter Hooks & Endings

Most chapters benefit from forward momentum or a meaningful aftereffect. The ending should give the reader a reason to continue without forcing every chapter into a cliffhanger.

---

## The Hook Requirement

**Guideline**: Avoid accidental dead stops. A quiet chapter may end at physical rest when a decision, changed relationship, unresolved implication, or emotional aftereffect remains active.

Quiet chapters can preserve forward pull or resonance through:
- An unresolved question
- A decision pending
- An approaching threat
- An anticipated event
- A new tension

---

## Hook Types

### The Question Hook
Chapter ends with an unanswered question.

> "She opened the door. The room was empty—except for the blood."

Reader asks: Whose blood? What happened?

**Best for**: Mystery, discovery, revelation setup

### The Threat Hook
Danger is established but not resolved.

> "They found us. The howls came from three directions now."

Reader asks: Will they survive? What happens next?

**Best for**: Action, thriller, horror

### The Decision Hook
Character faces a choice, answer not yet given.

> "She could save one of them. Only one. She looked between them."

Reader asks: Who will she choose? What will it cost?

**Best for**: Moral dilemmas, relationship drama

### The Revelation Hook
Something is revealed, implications unexplored.

> "He finally recognized the face in the photograph. It was her mother."

Reader asks: What does this mean? How will she react?

**Best for**: Plot twists, mystery, family drama

### The Arrival Hook
Someone or something arrives, impact unknown.

> "The ship had landed. The hatch was opening."

Reader asks: Who's inside? Friend or enemy?

**Best for**: Sci-fi, fantasy, any external force entering

### The Departure Hook
Character leaves toward unknown outcome.

> "She walked into the forest. She didn't look back."

Reader asks: What will she find? Will she return?

**Best for**: Journey starts, point of no return

### The Promise Hook
Something is about to happen.

> "Tomorrow, they would storm the castle. Tonight, they sharpened their blades."

Reader asks: Will the attack succeed? Who will die?

**Best for**: Building anticipation, pre-climax

### The Emotional Hook
Relationship in flux, resolution pending.

> "He wanted to say he was sorry. The words wouldn't come."

Reader asks: Will he ever say it? Is it too late?

**Best for**: Romance, relationship drama, reconciliation arcs

---

## Hook Strength Levels

### Cliffhanger (Strongest)
Immediate danger or revelation, demands continuation.

> "The gun fired."

Use sparingly. Too many cliffhangers exhaust readers.

### Strong Hook
Clear tension, reader wants to know what happens.

> "She had three days. After that, the deal was off."

Standard ending for most chapters.

### Soft Hook
Subtle pull, reader curious but not desperate.

> "Something had changed between them. She wasn't sure what yet."

Good for quieter moments, transition chapters.

### Dead Stop (Usually Revise)
Everything resolved, character at rest.

> "She went to bed satisfied. Tomorrow would be a new day."

This often kills momentum unless closure itself is the intended structural effect.

---

## Hook Placement

### The Final Line
The hook is often (not always) the last line of the chapter.

Strong final lines:
- Short and punchy
- Create question or tension
- Imply what's next without showing it
- Leave something unsaid

### The Final Beat
Sometimes the hook is the final scene beat, not just the line.

> Beth stood at the window, watching the treeline. The shapes were back. And this time, they weren't hiding.

The entire paragraph is the hook.

### Setup in Penultimate Beat
Sometimes you need a beat before the hook to make it land.

> Everything was quiet now. The attack hadn't come.
>
> But Preston's ears were still flat against his skull. He could hear something she couldn't.

The first line creates false relief. The second reverses it.

---

## Varying Hooks

Don't repeat the same hook type too often.

**Monotonous**:
- Ch 1: Threat arrives
- Ch 2: Threat escalates
- Ch 3: New threat
- Ch 4: Threat continues

**Varied**:
- Ch 1: Question (What is that sound?)
- Ch 2: Threat (They're here)
- Ch 3: Decision (Who to save?)
- Ch 4: Revelation (She knew all along)

---

## Hook Failures

### The Resolution Ending
Everything wraps up neatly. Reader has no questions.

**Fix**: Leave one thread open. Hint at what's next.

### The Pause Ending
Character stops and rests. Nothing pending.

**Fix**: End on anticipation, not completion.

### The Summary Ending
Narrator tells us what happened next.

> "And so they traveled to the city, arriving three days later."

**Fix**: End at the moment of tension, skip to next scene in next chapter.

### The Reflection Ending
Character thinks about events without forward motion.

**Fix**: Reflection should reveal something new or point toward action.

---

## Genre-Specific Hooks

### Thriller
End on threat, danger, ticking clock.

### Romance
End on emotional tension, almost-moment, misunderstanding.

### Mystery
End on clue, question, revelation.

### Fantasy/Adventure
End on journey continuation, new obstacle, arrival.

### Horror
End on dread, wrongness, approaching danger.

---

## The Hook Test

When a chapter needs forward pull, ask:

1. **What question does this leave unanswered?**
2. **What tension remains unresolved?**
3. **What is the reader anticipating?**
4. **Why would they turn the page?**

If you can't answer at least one of these clearly, the hook is weak.

---

## Scene Document Integration

When the project tracks ending beats, include the field its convention expects. A project-neutral option is:

```markdown
## Chapter Beats
- [Beat 1]: Opening action
- [Beat 2]: Pressure develops
- [Beat 3]: Escalation
- [Turn]: The consequential change
- **Ending beat**: [Hook, resonance, or earned closure] - [The specific final action, image, line, or aftereffect]
```

**Example**:
```markdown
- **Ending beat**: Threat hook - Preston growls at the treeline. "They're testing us."
```

Plan the ending explicitly. Use a hook when forward pull is needed; use resonance or earned closure when that better serves the chapter and surrounding rhythm.
