# Religion profile guide

Use only the depth the story can reveal or contest.

## Identity and truth status

- `name`, `also_known_as`, `tag`, `form`, `scale`, `status`
- `central_claim`: what adherents believe is true about existence, obligation, death, transcendence, history, or personhood
- `truth_status`: verified, partly verified, disputed, symbolic, fabricated, unknowable, or setting-specific; keep this separate from what believers know
- `public_reputation`: how outsiders commonly describe it

## Sacred focus

Use the structure that fits the belief system, not all of these:

- `deities_or_powers`: name, domain, title, relationship to worshippers, and disagreements among traditions
- `ancestors_or_exemplars`: who is remembered and what their example demands
- `cosmology`: what the world is, where it came from, and what threatens its order
- `origin_or_revelation`: how the tradition says its truth became known
- `death_and_afterlife`: belief, uncertainty, burial or disposal practice, and consequences for the living

Do not build a pantheon as a list of portfolios. Give divine or sacred figures relationships, contradictions, contested interpretations, or consequences.

## Lived practice

- `ordinary_practice`: daily or weekly acts available to common adherents
- `rituals`: initiation, marriage or bonding, mourning, confession, pilgrimage, sacrifice, meditation, maintenance, festivals, or setting-specific acts
- `holiday_or_cycle`: what changes in work, food, dress, movement, law, or social permission
- `tenets`: principles believers try to enact
- `taboos`: forbidden acts and the practical cost of observance
- `material_culture`: symbols, clothing, architecture, objects, food, sound, scent, interfaces, or bodily marks visible in scenes
- `sacred_text_or_transmission`: book, oral lineage, dream, algorithm, performance, archive, implanted memory, apprenticeship, or another channel

## Institution and power

- `clergy_or_authorities`: who interprets, teaches, disciplines, and performs rites
- `organization`: centralized, cellular, household, hereditary, elected, monastic, networked, charismatic, or informal
- `resources`: property, donations, labor, data, magic, legitimacy, education, medicine, courts, burial, communication, or access
- `entry_and_exit`: conversion, birth, initiation, apostasy, excommunication, and the cost of leaving
- `relationship_to_law`: state faith, tolerated minority, persecuted tradition, civic requirement, regulator, parallel court, or deliberately separate

Separate spiritual authority, administrative power, and coercive power.

## Difference and instability

- `internal_variations`: at least two sincere interpretations with distinct social bases
- `schism_or_crisis`: the active fracture, revelation, scandal, succession, persecution, reform, or failed prophecy
- `concealed_truth`: optional; include only if it changes present behavior
- `failure_consequence`: what adherents and dependents lose if the institution or tradition breaks
- `outsider_misconception`: optional; a useful correction to a common caricature

## Story pressure

- `relationship_to_protagonist`: what each side wants, what help costs, and how the relationship may change
- `relationships`: one subsection per named faith, faction, polity, or character; name the contested practice, claim, place, resource, constituency, or legal privilege
- `prevents`: one sentence naming an option the faith or its institution closes
- `forces`: one sentence naming a duty, deadline, choice, performance, compromise, or danger it imposes
- `enables`: one sentence naming costly access, shelter, status, knowledge, healing, community, transport, or power it offers
- `first_reveal`, `escalation`, `payoff`: optional story-specific handling, not permanent theology

Strong scene pressure is actionable: “The mourning law prevents Ren from opening the dead pilot's sealed memory until the nine-day vigil ends,” not “The faith creates tension.”
