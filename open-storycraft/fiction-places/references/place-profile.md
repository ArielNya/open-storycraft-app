# Place profile guide

Use the smallest profile that lets a writer stage the relevant scenes.

## Identity and placement

- `name`, `also_known_as`, `tag`, `type: Location`, `subtype`, `status`
- `region_or_parent`: linked settlement, province, vessel, planet, structure, network, or other container
- `environment`: biome, climate, engineered conditions, plane, orbit, depth, or setting-specific context
- `near`: nearest useful anchor plus travel time or relationship, not merely direction
- `access`: routes, permissions, tides, seasons, keys, vehicles, rituals, interfaces, or costs required to enter

## Spatial and sensory model

- `physical_description`: shape, scale, materials, levels, boundaries, landmarks, lines of sight, bottlenecks, and exits
- `sensory_description`: dominant light, sound, smell, air, texture, temperature, motion, gravity, or other bodily impression
- `zones`: 2–5 distinct areas only when movement between them matters
- `change_over_time`: day/night, tide, weather, season, maintenance cycle, magical phase, occupancy, decay, or other transformation

A writer should be able to block a scene from this description. Avoid lists of adjectives without spatial relationships.

## Use, danger, and reward

- `purpose_or_origin`: what made the place and what it is used for now
- `hazard`: environmental, social, legal, technological, magical, ecological, psychological, or mixed; state warning signs and escalation
- `inhabitants_or_users`: who belongs, visits, patrols, nests, maintains, exploits, or avoids it
- `bounty_or_opportunity`: resource, route, truth, shelter, leverage, beauty, status, technology, magic, or encounter worth reaching
- `scarcity_or_cost`: why the opportunity is not easily obtained elsewhere
- `custody_or_claims`: who believes they own or protect it and what makes the claim credible

The hazard should obstruct access to the bounty, complicate its use, or make taking it consequential.

## Meaning and story pressure

- `history`: only past events that remain physically, legally, culturally, or emotionally active
- `legend_or_belief`: what people say, who believes it, and how belief changes conduct
- `author_truth`: optional spoiler-bearing fact; omit when mystery should remain genuinely open
- `relationship_to_protagonist`: desire, fear, memory, obligation, or misreading attached to the place
- `prevents`: the option this place closes
- `forces`: the movement, deadline, exposure, sacrifice, or choice it imposes
- `enables`: the costly route, refuge, encounter, evidence, resource, or transformation it permits
- `first_reveal`, `escalation`, `payoff`: optional handling for the current story

Strong: “At high tide the flooded gallery prevents Orren from retreating by the route he entered.”

Weak: “The dangerous ruins create tension.”

## Continuity check

Confirm distance, travel time, direction, scale, entrances, exits, vertical movement, visibility, sound carry, weather, water level, gravity, atmosphere, and who knows what about the site. Record uncertainty rather than inventing a map fact the project has not established.
