# Dialogue Preflight

Use this before drafting or substantially rewriting a dialogue scene. Keep the working notes compact; they are a decision aid, not manuscript content.

## 1. Define the scene's conversational job

Record:

- Entry state: what is true before anyone speaks.
- Exit state: what must be different when the exchange ends.
- Practical pressure: what people are physically doing or waiting on.
- Setting pressure: how access, privacy, acoustics, movement, observers, or usable objects constrain the exchange.
- Emotional pressure: what makes direct speech expensive.
- Information pressure: what must be learned, concealed, corrected, or left uncertain.

If the scene only transfers facts, decide who resists the transfer and why. If nobody resists and no relationship changes, compress the exchange or move the information into action.

## 2. Build a speaker card from canon

Create one card per significant speaker:

```text
Speaker:
Immediate objective:
First attention: what this person notices first in this scene
Knowledge: known / assumed / misunderstood / unknown
Withheld material:
Social strategy: direct, soften, joke, test, deflect, lecture, bargain, withdraw, etc.
Sentence behavior: length, completion, vocabulary source, tolerance for abstraction
Pressure leak: how speech changes under stress
Avoidance: what this person will not say plainly
Relationship variant: how this listener changes the voice
Humor permission: none / initiates / responds once / sustained banter
Canon anchors: 2-5 recent lines or exchanges, used for calibration rather than copying
```

Do not copy personality adjectives into the card without translating them into choices. "Warm" is not a speech instruction. "Answers the fear beneath a question while leaving the factual challenge to the expert" is.

## 3. Separate cognition

For every consequential topic, distinguish:

- Shared fact: all speakers know it.
- Specialized fact: only a role or expert knows it.
- Private fact: one speaker knows and may conceal it.
- Assumption: a speaker treats it as true without proof.
- Misreading: a speaker has interpreted evidence incorrectly.
- Missing category: a speaker lacks the cultural or conceptual frame to understand it.

Knowledge changes language only when it affects what a character chooses, hides, tests, or misunderstands. Do not use dialogue to restate shared facts for the reader.

## 4. Map pressure moves, not lines

Sketch the exchange as actions before wording it. A useful shape is uneven:

```text
A probes for commitment.
B answers the practical issue and avoids commitment.
A watches B act and lets the question sit.
C introduces a deadline unrelated on the surface.
B reveals too much while solving the deadline.
A does not capitalize on it.
```

Available moves include probe, evade, narrow, redirect, refuse, concede, misread, expose, test, threaten, reassure, withdraw, act, interrupt, or remain silent. Do not assign a move to every turn merely to produce variety.

Let the setting do work when canon supports it. Noise can shorten or expose speech; surveillance can force indirection; a physical task can delay an answer; an object or exit can replace a line. Do not add decorative interruptions that do not change pressure or choice.

## 5. Allocate wit deliberately

Name the humor source for each beat, if one exists. Everyone else may ignore the joke, answer literally, react physically, or stay focused on the problem.

Before keeping a joke, ask:

- Would this speaker spend attention on humor now?
- Does the joke reveal coping, relationship, or power?
- Did the preceding line exist independently, or was it manufactured as a setup?

Cut the joke when its main purpose is to keep the exchange entertaining.

## 6. Draft with nonresponse available

At every question, consider at least one alternative to a clean answer:

- answer only the dangerous noun
- correct the premise
- answer an earlier question
- give a practical instruction
- act instead
- misunderstand honestly
- refuse without explaining
- let the other person abandon the question

Choose the behavior supported by the speaker card and scene pressure. Do not introduce messiness at random.

## Preflight completion test

Draft only after you can answer:

- Why does each major speaker participate?
- What would each prefer not to discuss?
- Who is allowed to be funny here?
- What does the setting permit, expose, interrupt, or prevent?
- Where can the conversation fail to cooperate?
- What changes by the end besides the reader's knowledge?
