# Locations Guide

How to develop locations that serve the story.

---

## Location Profile Template

For each key location, define:

### Physical
What does it look like? Size, architecture, state of repair, notable features.

**Not**: "A big castle."
**Better**: "Stone walls blackened by centuries of coal smoke. Towers at uneven heights, added by different generations. The east wing collapsed in the last war and was never rebuilt."

### Function
What happens here? What is this place FOR?

- Administrative (court, government)
- Commercial (market, port, business)
- Residential (homes, neighborhoods)
- Sacred (temple, shrine, ritual space)
- Military (fortress, training, strategic)
- Transitional (crossroads, station, liminal space)

### Atmosphere
How does it FEEL to be here? Emotional texture.

- Tense and watchful
- Chaotic and overwhelming
- Quiet and abandoned
- Vibrant and welcoming
- Oppressive and claustrophobic

### Who's Here
Who inhabits or uses this space?

- Permanent residents
- Regular visitors
- Who controls it
- Who's excluded
- Power dynamics present

### Story Use
What scenes happen here? What does this location enable narratively?

- Confrontations
- Secrets revealed
- Safe haven
- Danger zone
- Transformation point

---

## Types of Locations

### Hub Locations
Where protagonists return to. Home base. Should feel familiar, safe (or increasingly unsafe as story progresses).

### Arena Locations
Where major confrontations happen. Climactic scenes. Physical features should enable interesting action.

### Threshold Locations
Borders, gates, crossings. Symbolize transitions. Characters are changed by crossing them.

### Hidden Locations
Secret places that must be discovered. Reward for characters and readers.

### Contrast Locations
Places that highlight differences. Rich vs poor areas. Safe vs dangerous. Order vs chaos.

## Sensory Grounding

For each location, define at least 3 senses:

### Sight
- Light quality (bright, dim, flickering)
- Color palette
- Movement (busy, still, occasional)
- Scale (cramped, vast, human)

### Sound
- Ambient noise (silence, murmur, roar)
- Characteristic sounds (bells, machinery, nature)
- What's missing (no birds, no children)

### Smell
- Often forgotten, very evocative
- Cooking, industry, decay, nature
- Changes with time of day

### Touch/Feel
- Temperature
- Air quality (humid, dry, stale)
- Textures underfoot

---

## Location Relationships

### Maps (Mental or Literal)
How do locations relate spatially? Travel time matters for plot.

### Contrast Pairs
Pair locations that highlight differences:
- The palace and the slums
- The safe village and the dangerous forest
- Home and exile

### Progression
Locations often get more dangerous/strange as story progresses. Or the reverse - protagonist moves toward safety they must earn.
