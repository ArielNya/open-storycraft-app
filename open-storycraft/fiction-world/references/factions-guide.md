# Factions Guide

How to develop groups that create conflict and complexity.

---

## Faction Profile Template

For each faction:

### Who
- Members (types of people, not just leaders)
- Leadership structure
- Size and reach
- Where they operate
- How you join / leave

### Want
- Primary goal (what they're working toward)
- Why this matters to them
- What success looks like

### Method
- How they pursue their goal
- Tactics they use
- Lines they won't cross (or will)
- Resources they have

### Internal
- Divisions within the faction
- Debates they have
- Secrets the rank-and-file don't know
- Tensions that could fracture them

### Relationship to Protagonist
- Ally, enemy, neutral, complicated?
- What they want FROM protagonist
- What protagonist wants from them
- How this might change

---

## Faction Dynamics

### No Pure Evil Groups
Every faction should have:
- At least one legitimate grievance or motivation
- Members who believe they're doing good
- Internal disagreement about methods

### No Perfect Ally Groups
Every allied faction should have:
- Goals that partially conflict with protagonist
- Methods protagonist might question
- Limits to their support

### Faction Conflicts
Map out:
- Who opposes whom
- Why (competing goals, old grievances, ideology)
- Where conflict is hot vs. cold
- How protagonist's actions shift the balance

## Power Dynamics

### Resources
What does each faction control?
- Territory
- Wealth
- Information
- Magic/technology
- People (loyalty, labor)
- Legitimacy

### Relationships
- Formal alliances
- Secret arrangements
- Trading relationships
- Hostilities

### History
- How did they get where they are?
- Old wounds that haven't healed
- Debts owed
- Past betrayals

## Using Factions in Story

### Faction as Obstacle
They stand between protagonist and goal. Methods of opposition vary - violence, politics, economics, social pressure.

### Faction as Resource
They offer something protagonist needs. But at what cost? With what strings?

### Faction as Mirror
Their situation reflects protagonist's choices. What happens to them shows what could happen to protagonist.

### Shifting Alliances
Factions can:
- Betray established alliances
- Become allies after being enemies
- Split into opposing groups
- Be absorbed by other factions

## Avoiding Common Problems

### The Monolith
**Problem**: All members think and act the same.
**Fix**: Show internal debate, dissenting members, different approaches within the group.

### The Irrelevance
**Problem**: Faction exists but doesn't affect the plot.
**Fix**: Either cut them or give them a scene where they matter.

### The Exposition Dump
**Problem**: Reader learns everything about faction history at once.
**Fix**: Reveal through action and conflict, not explanation.

### The Obvious Allegiance
**Problem**: Good guys faction vs. bad guys faction.
**Fix**: Make protagonist's faction morally complicated too.
