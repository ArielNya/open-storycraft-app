# Polity profile and consistency guide

Use the smallest scope that makes the political pressure understandable.

## Identity and authority

- `name`, `also_known_as`, `tag`, `form`, `scale`, `status`
- `government`: formal structure and the real path by which decisions are made
- `legitimacy`: why enough people obey—law, inheritance, consent, faith, protection, wealth, expertise, infrastructure, coercion, tradition, or another basis
- `ruler_or_executive`: title, generated name, powers, dependencies, motivation, flaw, and relevant secret
- `succession_or_continuity`: how authority survives a leader's removal
- `limits_on_power`: legal, economic, geographic, technological, magical, cultural, ecological, or coalition constraints

## Territory and population

- `capital_or_center`: a real settlement, vessel, network node, court, seasonal gathering, or mobile seat
- `divisions`: each division's name, type, leader, environment, productive base, capital, selected settlements, relationship to the center, and live tension
- `transport_and_communication`: how orders, people, goods, and news move; include delay
- `population`: rounded estimate and relevant composition
- `settlement_pattern`: where most people live and why
- `important_sites`: only locations that constrain policy or scenes

When showing a total and components, make the arithmetic exact. Rounded estimates may be used, but say what is estimated. Do not hide most of the population in an implausible multiplier. If unnamed rural or distributed population is summarized, keep its average unit smaller than named settlements of the same class.

## State capacity

- `laws`: 2–4 consequential rules with enforcement and affected groups
- `economy`: production, scarcity, trade, currency or allocation, taxation or extraction, and dependency
- `military_or_enforcement`: actual forces, readiness, command, logistics, and limits
- `administration`: who records, licenses, adjudicates, collects, maintains, or allocates
- `faith_or_civic_ideology`: official relationship and practical effect; do not assume one state religion
- `infrastructure`: systems whose failure changes political power

Power should follow capacity. A government cannot reliably enforce a law where it cannot communicate, travel, identify people, pay agents, or maintain legitimacy.

## History and present pressure

- `founding`: the useful political memory, not a complete chronology
- `golden_age_or_legitimizing_victory`: what present leaders invoke
- `catastrophe_or_old_wound`: what still shapes institutions or borders
- `current_crisis`: the active shortage, succession, rebellion, war, exposure, disaster, reform, debt, migration, technological change, or legitimacy break
- `internal_tension`: the groups bearing unequal costs
- `failure_consequence`: what breaks for ordinary people as well as elites

## Relationships

- `internal_factions`: link story-important groups and name what each controls
- `neighbors_and_rivals`: for each, record public relationship, actual dependence or conflict, contested resource, breaking point, and who benefits from the status quo
- `relationship_to_protagonist`: what the polity wants from them, what it can offer, the price, and how their actions could shift the balance

## Story pressure

- `prevents`: a route, right, alliance, refuge, truth, or resource the polity closes
- `forces`: a tax, oath, draft, hearing, deadline, journey, compromise, identity performance, or dangerous choice
- `enables`: legal standing, transport, protection, intelligence, audience, resources, sanctuary, or force—at a price
- `first_reveal`, `escalation`, `payoff`: optional story-specific uses

Prefer consequences over lore: “The registry prevents unlicensed clones from buying passage off-world,” not “Citizenship is tightly controlled.”
