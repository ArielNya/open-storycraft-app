## Fragment Detection Criteria

You are hunting **sentence fragments in narrative prose**. A fragment is any construction that lacks a subject, a verb, or both, and cannot stand as a complete sentence.

### What Counts as a Fragment

#### 1-Word Fragments
Single words used as standalone sentences in narration:
- "Nothing." / "Silence." / "Gone." / "Finally."
- Exception: Dialogue is exempt. A character can say "Nothing." in speech.

#### 2-Word Fragments
Two-word constructions posing as sentences:
- "No thrusters." / "Too late." / "Pure chaos." / "Dead silence."
- Noun + adjective combos: "Absolute carnage." / "Total silence."
- Negation pairs: "No plan." / "No time." / "Not good."

#### 3-Word Fragments
Three-word constructions without a subject-verb core:
- "Not a chance." / "Out of time." / "Same as before."
- Adjective stacks: "Cold. Dark. Empty."
- Prepositional orphans: "Into the void." / "Across the deck."

### What is NOT a Fragment (Do Not Flag)

- **Dialogue:** Characters can speak in fragments. Only flag narration.
- **Imperative sentences:** "Run." / "Fire." have an implied subject (you). These are complete.
- **Interjections in The POV voice used sparingly for genuine impact:** One "So." or "Right." per chapter in a moment of real stress is acceptable. Two is a pattern. Three is a problem.
- **System notifications:** Content inside callout blocks (`> [!system]`, `> [!warning]`, etc.) is exempt.

### Severity Levels

**Kill on sight (merge or expand, no exceptions):**
- Staccato atmosphere lists: "Dust. Silence. Nothing." or "Tanks. APCs. Infantry."
- Back-to-back fragments (two or more in a row)
- Fragments used as paragraph openers or closers for false drama
- Any fragment that a voice actor would stumble on or have to invent context for

**Flag and evaluate:**
- Single isolated fragments with clear rhetorical purpose (rare, must earn their place)
- Fragments at the climax of a genuine life-or-death beat (battle scenes get limited allowance, but "limited" means 1-2 per scene, not 10)

### The Voice Actor Test

Read the sentence out loud as if you are narrating an audiobook. If you have to:
- Add words to make it make sense
- Guess at the tone or emphasis because there is no grammatical scaffolding
- Pause awkwardly because the "sentence" has no natural rhythm

Then it is a fragment and it needs to die.

### How to Fix

Never just delete. Every fragment you kill must be **merged into an adjacent sentence** or **expanded into a complete, cinematically muscular sentence**.

**Bad (fragment):**
> No thrusters. No plan.

**Good (merged and expanded):**
> They had no thrusters and no plan, just a ship full of twenty-year-olds drifting through space on borrowed momentum.

**Bad (staccato list):**
> Tanks. APCs. Infantry. The whole convoy stretched across the ridge.

**Good (single grounded sentence):**
> Tanks, APCs, and infantry stretched across the ridge in a convoy long enough to make Rowan reconsider every decision that had brought him here.

**Bad (false drama closer):**
> The airlock sealed behind them. Darkness.

**Good (complete and evocative):**
> The airlock sealed behind them and the corridor went dark, the kind of dark that made Rowan's skin crawl.

### Preserving Rowan's Voice

The POV narration is wry, informal, and conversational. When you expand a fragment, the replacement must sound like Rowan's internal voice, not a textbook. His voice is the reason the sentence exists. Keep it.

**Wrong fix (too formal):**
> The situation had deteriorated beyond the point of recovery.

**Right fix (The POV voice):**
> The situation had gone sideways in about four different directions, and none of them were good.
